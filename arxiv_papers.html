The chemical DNA of the Magellanic Clouds -- VII. The lithium abundance in the Small Magellanic Cloud and the discovery of 4 lithium-rich stars

A. Mucciarelli, M. Palla, C. T. Nguyen, L. Monaco, E. Pancino, D. A. Alvarez Garay, S. Anoardo, C. Lardo, F. Matteucci, D. Romano

Measuring lithium abundance in galaxies beyond the Milky Way (MW) remains a challenging task. In these systems, only stars close to the tip of the red giant branch (RGB) are sufficiently bright to be observed spectroscopically.Their A(Li) are expected to be altered by mixing episodes that occur during the stellar evolution, making the interpretation of measured abundances more complex. In this context, we present A(Li) of 209 RGB stars in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC),observed with FLAMES@VLT. Approximately one-third of the sample shows a Li line detected at a significance level above 5sigma, while about half of the sample has detections above 3sigma; for the remaining stars, only upper limits can be derived. This sample exhibits a significant star-to-star scatter in A(Li). Using theoretical models, we find that the observed scatter can be likely explained by the combination of 2 effects.First,our stars span a wide range of ages, masses, [Fe/H]. Thermohaline mixing, one of the leading mechanisms proposed to explain the extra mixing occurring at the RGB bump, predicts different levels of A(Li) depletion depending on stellar mass and [Fe/H], naturally contributing to the observed scatter in A(Li). Second, in the SMC, the enrichment in A(Li) driven by novae is expected to occur at lower [Fe/H] than in the MW, because the SMC has experienced a lower star formation efficiency than the MW. This is also supported by the rise of A(Li) with decreasing age, as measured in 3 SMC globular clusters. Furthermore, we report the first identification of 4 low-mass Li-rich giant stars in the SMC, reinforcing the existence of Li-rich stars in extra-galactic systems. All these results demonstrate that A(Li) measured in stars close to the RGB tip, still provide valuable constraints on both stellar/galactic chemical evolution of A(Li) in external galaxies.
Forward modeling linear variable filter data: High resolution PAH and Paschen-$α$ maps from SPHEREx

Andrew K. Saydjari, Connor Hainje, Benjamin Dodge, Philipp Frank, David W. Hogg, Susan E. Clark
Comments
34 pages, 16 figures

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission is a powerful tool for the study of the interstellar medium (ISM) in both Galactic and extragalactic contexts. It can reveal ionization state, radiation field, metallicity, composition, and ISM structure as sculpted by processes like turbulence and stellar feedback. Here we use NASA's SPHEREx to produce clean, high-resolution PAH maps at spectral resolution R $\sim$ 35 (compared to WISE at R $\sim$ 2). Prior treatments of the SPHEREx linear variable filter data seek to convert them to more conventional band-averaged maps that can leave striping artifacts associated with spatially variable wavelength sampling, especially at emission lines. In contrast, we deliver a scalable forward-modeling method capable of generating high-quality all-sky SPHEREx emission-line maps at 8.5 arcsec FWHM angular resolution (on 6.4 arcsec pixels), while naturally producing maps without visible striping. We reformat the data into a pixel-level database, mask stellar contamination, and infill missing pixels with a conditional, data-driven, anisotropic Gaussian process. We apply our method to produce 3.3 $μ$m and 3.4 $μ$m PAH and Paschen-$α$ emission maps of a set of fields spanning a range of Galactic and extragalactic conditions including the Rosette nebula, the Corona Australis molecular cloud, M33, and M31. We demonstrate the quality and synergies of our SPHEREx 3.3 $μ$m PAH maps by comparisons with other available gas and dust tracer maps and achieve sensitivities up to $σ(I_{3.3}) = 2.7 \times 10^{-8}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ sr$^{-1}$. We estimate that a two-year extension of the SPHEREx survey, with modest modifications to the scanning strategy, would match or exceed the PAH sensitivity of WISE over the whole sky.
Signatures of mass transfer in open-cluster blue straggler stars. I. Collinder 261: Evidence from rotation, binarity, and chemical abundances

M. J. Rain, L. Monaco, S. Villanova, G. Carraro
Comments
26 pages, 14 figures, 5 tables

We present the first dedicated $v\sin i$ survey of blue straggler stars (BSS) in the old open cluster Collinder 261 ($t=6.7$ Gyr), based on VLT/FLAMES-GIRAFFE spectroscopy of 40 BSS and 42 turn-off/subgiant (TO/SGB) reference stars. Seventeen of the 39 BSS entering the statistics (44%) rotate faster than 40 km/s, whereas all TO/SGB reference stars are slow rotators; fast rotation is strongly linked to spectroscopic binarity. TESS light curves identify seven eclipsing or ellipsoidal binaries with $P_{orb}=0.5$-2.1 d, all rotating at the velocity set by tidal synchronisation, so in these systems the fast rotation is maintained by a close post-mass-transfer orbit and does not date the formation event. Differential C, O, Ba, and Fe abundances for the 22 slow rotators reveal that 62% carry the surface-pollution signature of mass transfer, in two distinct patterns separated by an empty ~1 dex gap in [Ba/Fe]: a Ba-rich group tracing AGB donors and a C/O-enhanced, Ba-normal group tracing donors below the $s$-process activation mass. The BSS iron abundance is consistent with the cluster value; the few apparent Fe excesses are partly explained by differential reddening and do not affect the abundance pattern. The correlated C-O enrichment is opposite to the depletion seen in globular-cluster BSS, indicating binaries wide enough for donors to reach the AGB. With $A^+_{r_h}=0.171\pm0.034$ and $f_{FR-BSS}=44\pm8$%, Collinder 261 extends the globular-cluster rotation-density trends into the open-cluster regime: its BSS were assembled by a continuously operating binary channel, and their chemistry records the dynamical environment of the host cluster.

A 35.6\,deg$^2$ field of Euclid Data Release~1 that covers part of the corona and tidal tails of the young (100--130\,Myr) nearby (234\,pc) open cluster Blanco~1 is presented. We select 22 ultracool dwarfs from Euclid photometry and slitless spectra, and measure proper motions over a median baseline of 13\,yr by combining Euclid astrometry with VIKING, KiDS and Rubin positions. Ten objects are comoving late-M and L dwarf candidates with membership probabilities of at least 0.5 under two field models. Nine of them are typed from their Euclid spectra, and the tenth is a candidate only if its colour-based type is adopted. Brown dwarf evolutionary models give masses of 0.03--0.06\,$M_\odot$ for the cluster age. The substellar population is about twice that predicted by a lognormal mass function scaled to the cluster's Gaia members. The remaining 12 objects are classified as late-M, L and T dwarfs with low probability of membership in the cluster. Using the Euclid sample to calibrate a search over 246\,deg$^2$ of VIKING and KiDS photometry and motions, we identify 60 ultracool candidates, with about seven expected by chance. In the core region of Blanco 1 archival Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam images and motions over 2011--2025 yield 35 candidates of types M7 or later, at least 20 of which are kinematic members at 95\% confidence. These results extend the spectroscopic sequence of Blanco 1 into the L-type brown dwarfs and the list of candidate members over the outskirts and tidal tails of the cluster.
Investigating the timescales traced by dynamical ages in young stellar systems

Isabella Armstrong, Alison Sills, Claude Cournoyer-Cloutier

Stars form in clustered environments, and then the stars may congregate into star clusters. The timescale on which individual stars form is not necessarily the same as the timescale on which star clusters assemble. Therefore, developing methods to probe these different timescales is a crucial part of understanding star cluster assembly and early evolution. Dynamical ages can be constructed from the kinematics of individual stars in clusters in the solar neighborhood using Gaia data. Using a star formation simulation, we test five different dynamical age methods. We divide dynamical ages into two approaches: methods which trace the size evolution of the cluster, and methods which trace the trajectories of individual stars. We confirm that size methods cannot accurately derive ages in bound clusters whereas methods tracing individual stars can be applied to both bound and unbound systems. We find that different dynamical age methods trace different epochs of star cluster formation and assembly: The method using stars with the oldest traceback ages provides an upper limit on stellar ages and the method using the median traceback ages is the best proxy for isochronal ages. Methods which trace the size evolution of the cluster best trace the onset of cluster expansion. Our work suggests that the median traceback time of stars moving away from the cluster provides the most reliable proxy for isochronal age, and that this method can be applied to any expanding system.

The central regions of the Magellanic Clouds are crucial for understanding galaxy interactions and calibrating the cosmic distance scale, yet they remain observationally challenging due to extreme stellar crowding. We utilised deep VISTA observations (VMCDeep) to determine the highest-precision near-infrared Period-Luminosity (PL) and Period-Wesenheit (PW) relations to date for Classical Cepheids (delta Cep class, DCEPs) in the dense central regions of the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC). We analysed J and Ks time-series data covering the central 1.77 deg^2 of each galaxy. Utilising approximately 40 epochs per filter and PSF-based photometry, we derived high-quality light curves for 573 and 2090 DCEPs in the LMC and SMC, respectively. We derived exceptionally precise PL and PW relations, exhibiting the lowest photometric dispersion published so far for the Magellanic Clouds. Fourier parameters calculated from the light curves indicate that pulsational models incorporating a mildly non-canonical mass-luminosity relation and a mixing-length parameter of alpha = 1.7 best reproduce the observations. Age derivations confirm a bimodal star formation history in the SMC centre. Crucially, we discovered a striking line-of-sight spatial dichotomy within the young SMC DCEP population (t < 116 Myr), which is bifurcated into two distinct structures separated by ~4 kpc. This provides independent stellar evidence tracing a recent direct collision with the LMC. Finally, by unifying fundamental and first-overtone pulsators, we derived a highly constrained relative distance between the Clouds. Comparing this to detached eclipsing binary distances, our data suggest a moderate metallicity correction for the Leavitt Law with gamma ~ -0.2 mag/dex.
How Intensity Threshold Shapes the Velocity Dispersion-Size Relation in Molecular Clouds

Yaru Qi, Xin Liu, Fujun Du

The velocity dispersion-size relation of molecular clouds, $σ_v = C\,(R/1\,{\rm pc})^γ$, has been reported with a power-law index ranging from $γ\approx$ 0.38 to 0.5 or higher, and a correspondingly varying normalization coefficient $C$, but the role of the intensity threshold in producing this scatter has not been quantitatively characterized. Using 4916 $^{13}$CO clouds identified with GDCluster (Liu & Du 2025) in the Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting (MWISP) survey, we derive $γ$ and $C$ as functions of the intensity threshold $T_{\rm th}$. The index $γ$ increases monotonically with threshold, reaching a saturation value $γ_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.56 for the full sample; splitting the sample by spiral arm gives higher values, $γ_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.62 and 0.70. The normalization $C(T_{\rm th})$ declines monotonically, saturating at $C_{\rm sat} \approx$ 0.35 km/s and implying a surface density of ~46 Msun/pc^2 under a virial approximation. On the $γ(T_{\rm th})$ curve, $γ\approx$ 0.38 (Larson 1981) and $γ\approx$ 0.5 (Solomon et al. 1987) correspond to a low threshold ($T_{\rm th} \approx$ 1.2 K) and a near-saturation one (2.6 K), respectively, roughly consistent with the $^{13}$CO-equivalent boundary temperatures of these two studies. These two historically debated values, together with the broader $γ\approx$ 0.38-0.7 range reported in the literature, may largely reflect different positions along this single threshold-dependent curve and differences in sample composition. This threshold effect should therefore be considered when using Larson-type relations to assess the roles of turbulence and gravity. As an independent check, DBSCAN-identified $^{13}$CO and $^{12}$CO samples show the same monotonic trend in $γ$, though the curve shape differs quantitatively.

Understanding massive star formation in low-metallicity environments is crucial for constraining the universality of star formation across cosmic evolution. In this Letter, we present the first systematic survey of massive protostellar outflows in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC, $Z\sim$0.2--0.5 $Z_\odot$), as part of the Magellanic Outflow and Chemistry Survey (MAGOS). MAGOS targets 40 massive young stellar objects (MYSOs) with luminosities of (1--23)$\times 10^4$ $L_\odot$ with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) at 0.85 mm at a linear resolution of $\sim$0.1 pc. Using CO (3--2), we identify blue- and red-shifted outflows associated with the primary continuum sources toward 29 targets in the LMC and 10 targets in the SMC. Comparing the outflow mass, momentum, energy, and force versus luminosity scaling relations in MAGOS with those in the Galaxy, we find that the LMC CO outflows have a similar slope in the mass-luminosity relation, suggesting that CO remains an effective tracer of massive protostellar outflows at $Z\sim$0.5 $Z_\odot$. In contrast, despite the large scatter, the SMC CO outflows are deficient at high luminosity and show flatter scaling relations than the LMC and the Galaxy. This is consistent with reduced CO abundances due to enhanced photodissociation at high luminosity and low metallicity ($Z\sim$0.2 $Z_\odot$), although the possibility of intrinsically weak outflow launching cannot be ruled out with the current data.

We study the resolved stellar kinematics and star formation histories, and their dependence on the environment, in a sample of 218 intermediate redshift (0.1 < z < 1) galaxies located in the CANDELS and COSMOS fields, observed with VLT/MUSE. The sample covers galaxies with stellar masses ranging M_star ~10^8 - 10^12 M_sun, including both star-forming and quiescent systems. For 113 galaxies, found in dense environments of galaxy groups, we derive the spin parameter ($λ_{R}$), while for the rest of the sample, we use data from previous papers in this series. Given that the surveys used in this work are flux-limited, the $λ_{R}$ distribution of our full sample lacks the bimodality observed in the local Universe, but the subsample of galaxies at z < 0.6 resembles the galaxy population in the nearby Universe, in terms of mass, sizes, and stellar population properties, more than galaxies at z > 0.6. We classified the galaxies into fast and slow rotators, finding that ~8% (17/218) of the galaxies are slow-rotating. The fraction of slow rotators does not change significantly down to z = 0, but we find that in the z ~ 0 sample, the spin parameter is, on average, higher than at higher redshifts. In our subsample at z > 0.6, we observed evidence for the existence of the kinematic morphology-density relation. The slow rotators in our sample mostly assemble their stellar mass in one or two main episodes of formation, and they finished assembling their mass via star formation by redshift of z $\sim$ 1. It is however, not clear if the loss of angular momentum can be associated with the last star formation episode. Based on their location in the mass - size diagram, the intermediate-redshift galaxies are consistent with being the progenitors of the most massive slow rotators observed at z~0, where the subsequent growth is facilitated through gas-poor (dry) mergers.

We investigate anomalously low-metallicity (ALM) star-forming regions in 11 galaxies from the Deciphering the Interplay between the Interstellar Medium, Stars, and the Circumgalactic Medium (DIISC) survey. We combine optical spectroscopy of 542 H$α$-bright regions from MMT/Binospec with imaging from the Vatican Advanced Technology Telescope and H I 21 cm imaging from the Very Large Array. We find 75 ALM regions: 19 identified using deviations from each galaxy's radial metallicity gradient, 27 using the resolved mass--metallicity relation (rMZR), and 29 via both methods. Regions identified by both methods are metal-poor relative to their galactocentric positions and local stellar mass surface densities by $>$ 0.1 dex, making them the strongest candidates for localized metallicity dilution. We estimate accreted masses of $5.6<\log(M_{\rm acc}/{\rm M_\odot})<7.5$ for the 48 radial-gradient-selected regions, comparable to observed and simulated high-velocity cloud masses. We calculate a mean hydrogen accretion rate of $1.02\ {\rm M_\odot yr^{-1}}$ per galaxy, which is comparable to the mean star formation rate of the sample. The strongest ALM candidates lie within or near $R_{25}$, while rMZR-only regions dominate the outer population. We find no evidence that circumgalactic Ly$α$ absorption along a single sightline traces disk chemical structure, as quantified by either the radial metallicity gradient or the fraction of radial-gradient-selected ALM regions. These results suggest that localized accretion of low-metallicity gas can sustain star formation in galaxies and demonstrate that ALM regions provide a spatially resolved indirect view of ongoing gas cycling.

M67 is a well-studied, solar-metallicity, old open cluster that hosts a diverse set of exotic stellar populations formed via mass transfer (MT). While Case A, B, and C MT systems have been individually examined in several clusters, a comprehensive investigation of all MT types across different evolutionary stages within a single cluster remains limited. This study aims to detect the presence of post mass-transfer systems using two different methods and thereby obtain a holistic view of binary evolution and mass-transfer mechanisms prevalent in M67. We constructed spectral energy distributions (SEDs) for 141 UV-detected stars in M67 using far-UV data from AstroSat/UVIT, combined with archival data from Swift/UVOT, GALEX, Gaia, 2MASS, WISE, and other sources. Furthermore, we performed a detailed chemical abundance analysis of cluster members using high-resolution spectroscopy from the GALAH DR3+ survey. We identified 11 new MS+WD binaries with previously undetected hotter companions, effectively doubling the known MS+WD binary population in M67. This includes five confirmed ELM WDs with orbital periods of 100-200 days and no chemical peculiarities, confirming Case A or B post-MT systems. We also detected 13 long-period SB1 systems with orbital periods longer than 1000 days and enhancements in s-process elements (Ba, La, Y), indicating Case C post-MT systems enriched by thermally pulsing asymptotic giant branch (AGB) companions which have evolved into undetected WDs. This study demonstrates that M67 hosts a diverse population of post-mass transfer binaries formed via Case A, B, and C mechanisms. The unified view makes M67 a key benchmark for studying binary star evolution and highlights the role of UV observations and high-resolution spectroscopy in uncovering hidden stellar histories.

The extraplanar diffuse ionised gas (eDIG) is a key tracer of the feedback processes that connect galactic discs and halos. We present the first study of spatially resolved gas-phase chemical abundances in the eDIG of edge-on galaxies, based on integral field spectroscopy obtained with MUSE. Using a sample of four galaxies from the BETIS sample, we derived maps of the oxygen abundance, 12+log(O/H), the nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio, log(N/O), and the ionisation parameter, log(U), with the HII-CHI-Mistry v5.5 code, and we tested the reliability of this method when different ionisation sources are present. We separated the eDIG into star-formation-dominated and shock-contaminated regimes according to the relative contribution of photoionisation and shocks, and investigated their impact on the derived chemical abundances. We find that HII-CHI-Mistry returns systematically lower O/H and N/O values in shock-contaminated regions, especially for N/O, with typical offsets of ~0.1-0.15 dex relative to star-formation-dominated regions, together with a more homogeneous and lower ionisation state (log(U) ~ -3.1). In contrast, star-formation-dominated regions span a wider range of ionisation parameters (log(U) ~ -3.3 to -2.8) while maintaining approximately solar oxygen abundances. Despite these differences, the abundance offsets are comparable to the sampling step of the HII-CHI-Mistry model grid. The inferred oxygen abundance shows little variation between discs and halos. This is compatible with a scenario in which star formation feedback and galactic fountains efficiently recycle oxygen-rich gas between both components, in agreement with hydrodynamical simulations. In contrast, the N/O ratio shows significant radial and vertical variations, particularly in environments with weaker star formation feedback.

We present new radio-continuum observations of the well-known Wolf-Rayet (WR) nebula PCG 11 (Cohen's Ring; named after and in honour of the co-author on the discovery paper, Martin Cohen) using observations from the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP) Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU), Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA) and MeerKAT surveys. The angular size of the elliptical emission is measured to be 54\arcsec$\times$45\arcsec\ (at P.A.=69.4\D), which agrees well with observations of the source in H$α$ and at 24\,$μ$m in the infrared. Using the measured angular size and a distance of 3.94$\pm$0.27\,kpc, we determine that the nebula has a linear size of $1.03(\pm0.07)\times0.86(\pm0.06)$\,pc. Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are absorption features of uncertain origin observed in the optical and near-infrared spectra of stars and galaxies. Although their correlation with dust extinction is well established, the physical conditions governing their strength across different interstellar medium (ISM) environments remain poorly understood. We investigate the environmental dependence of the DIB $\lambda5780$ using spatially resolved MaNGA spectroscopy for 837 nearby galaxies spanning a broad range of stellar masses, star-formation rates, and ISM properties. Stellar and nebular parameters are derived via spectral fitting, while DIB absorption is measured from high-signal-to-noise spatially binned residual spectra. We detect the DIB $\lambda5780$ in 159 galaxies and report the most distant spatially resolved DIB detection to date, at 232 Mpc. The DIB $\lambda5780$ equivalent width correlates positively with both stellar and gas-phase extinction. DIB $\lambda5780$ is preferentially detected in regions of high extinction, near- or super-solar metallicity ($12+\log({\rm O/H})\gtrsim8.5$), and moderate specific star-formation rates ($-10.5\lesssim\log({\rm sSFR}/{\rm yr}^{-1})\lesssim-9$). By contrast, it falls below our detection threshold in metal-poor or strongly ionized environments. It is rarely detected in regions with little or no recent star formation, with the few detections in such environments occurring primarily in regions driven by composite ionization. Our results indicate that DIB $\lambda5780$ is preferentially detected, within the sensitivity limits of the MaNGA data, in dust-rich, chemically evolved, and moderately ionized environments.
