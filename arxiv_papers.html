Episodic Protostellar Outflows: A Proper Motion Study in Serpens South
Authors
Simran Kaur, Adele L. Plunkett, Zhi-Yun Li, Per Bjerkeli, Manuel Fernandez-Lopez, Jon P. Ramsey, Hector G. Arce, Mihika Rao
Comments
No comment found
Primary
astro-ph.SR
Categories
astro-ph.SR astro-ph.GA
Serpens South is a notable protostellar cluster with signatures of recent star formation, including a high protostellar fraction. Within the region, several protostars provide important mechanical feedback via outflows. We investigate the molecular outflow driven by the Class 0 protostar known as CARMA-7, which is located in the central hub of Serpens South. Two epochs of observations with the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) with sub-arcsecond resolution capture the well-collimated, episodic outflow with distinct ejecta knots. This outflow, traced with 12CO J = 3-2 and SiO J = 8-7, appears to be mostly blueshifted to the south and redshifted to the north, with radial velocities up to at least 25 km/s. We present the first proper motion study of this outflow by comparing observations taken in 2017 and 2021. The measured proper motions trace the ejection history and refine the inferred outflow morphology. We find that proper motions of the 12CO J = 3-2 and SiO J = 8-7 knots range between 10 - 40 mas/yr, corresponding to 30-90 km/s. Using radial velocities and proper motions, we determined that the inclinations of the outflow lobes are ~60 -80 degrees relative to the line of sight. Finally, we measure ejection timescales as recent as ~10 to ~120 years, and we discuss this in the context of other known protostars with recent ejecta events.
A VLT/CRIRES+ study of helium 1.083$μ$m absorption features towards the inner Orion Nebula
Authors
Thomas J. Haworth, Mark McCaughrean, Mario van den Ancker, James E. Owen, John Bally, Samuel Pearson
Comments
Accepted for publication in MNRAS
Primary
astro-ph.SR
Categories
astro-ph.SR astro-ph.EP astro-ph.GA
JWST NIRCam F115W observations towards the inner Orion Nebula revealed a diverse array of previously unknown dark features. The most likely cause was absorption or suppressed emission of the helium 1.083$μ$m triplet which lies in that filter. We have obtained VLT/CRIRES+ high-resolution spectrographic observations over a selection of different dark targets, confirming that the features are indeed explained by a reduction in the flux of the helium 1.083$μ$m triplet, a reduction that is fairly uniform across the line. The Orion Nebula is a blister HII region, with a dense, bright ionised layer on the far side from the observer near the Orion A molecular cloud and lower density gas elsewhere. Using a simple radiative transfer model, we show that dark features arise when emission from the dense layer is absorbed by intermediate density helium (e.g. in clumps, globules, or winds from objects like proplyds) on the near side of the dense layer.

Only five out of the $\sim$350 molecules detected in the interstellar medium (ISM) carry the isocyanate functional group (NCO), the essential substructure of peptide bonds in proteins. Among them are isocyanic acid (HNCO) and its most stable N-protonated isomer H$_2$NCO$^+$. Previous studies have predicted that the O-protonated isomer HNCOH$^+$ should be present in the ISM; yet this species has remained elusive. We search for the metastable HNCOH$^+$ towards the Galactic Center molecular cloud G+0.693-0.027, where H$_2$NCO$^+$ has already been detected. We use an ultra-deep broadband spectral survey carried out towards G+0.693-0.027 with the Yebes 40-m, IRAM 30-m and APEX telescopes covering nearly 150 GHz of bandwidth between 31 and 275 GHz. We report the first detection of HNCOH$^+$ in the ISM with a column density of $N$ = (9.9 $\pm$ 0.5)$\times$10$^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$, which corresponds to a molecular abundance relative to H$_2$ of (7.3$^{+1.3} _{-1.0}$)$\times$10$^{-11}$. With the measured H$_2$NCO$^+$ column density of $N$ = (2.6 $\pm$ 0.3) $\times 10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$, this yields a HNCOH$^+$/H$_2$NCO$^+$ ratio of 3.8$^{+0.5} _{-0.4}$. This result is consistent with recent kinetic calculations that favour the formation of HNCOH$^+$ over H$_2$NCO$^+$. The detection of HNCOH$^+$ in the ISM broadens the inventory of molecules known to contain the isocyanate functional group and reinforces recent works showing that isomer abundances in the ISM do not necessarily correlate with thermodynamic stability.
Dust-induced reversal of the optical wavelength dependence of spiral-arm pitch angles
Authors
Eric E. Martínez-García, Rosa A. González-Lópezlira, Ivânio Puerari, Arturo I. Gómez-Ruiz
Comments
Accepted for publication in MNRAS
Primary
astro-ph.GA

astro-ph.GA astro-ph.CO
The classical density-wave scenario predicts a blue-to-red age--colour gradient across the spiral arms of disc galaxies as a consequence of shock-induced star formation. However, observations of spiral-arm pitch angles at different wavelengths sometimes suggest the opposite trend, namely a red-to-blue ``colour jump''. The origin of this apparent contradiction remains unclear. In this work, we investigate the effects of dust attenuation on post-shock stellar emission, together with the streamline motions of gas and stellar populations. We find that dust attenuation in regions with relatively high optical depths, $\hatτ_{V} \gtrsim 0.2$, can produce an apparent red-to-blue spatial sequence, with respect to the shock location, by reversing the relative positions of the flux maxima of stellar populations observed in different bands. Moreover, streamline motions confine post-shock stellar populations near the spiral arms for longer than in a circular-orbit model, thereby modifying the spatial distribution of the emitted light. We conclude that the apparent red-to-blue colour jump does not necessarily contradict the classical density-wave scenario. Instead, it can arise from the same post-shock stellar populations that intrinsically produce a blue-to-red age--colour gradient if their emission is modified by dust attenuation.
UnEZ: A Fully Unsupervised Self-Templating Neural Network for Redshift and Line Strength Estimation
Authors
Asha Abd, Karl Glazebrook, Colin Jacobs
Comments

As spectroscopic surveys grow, estimating galaxy properties such as metallicity, star formation rate, and redshift becomes computationally cumbersome. Methods of estimating such properties rely on template spectra or curated redshift catalogs, which fail to represent the diversity of objects seen in large-scale surveys. We present UnEZ, a fully unsupervised, physics-informed machine learning model that can predict spectroscopic redshifts and line strengths with no prior redshift or line strength labels. An encoder predicts emission and absorption line amplitudes/widths, and a fixed deterministic decoder constructs a unique template, estimated redshift, redshift confidence curve, and spectral model. UnEZ was tested on the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Data Release 18 due to its large dataset and thoroughly vetted redshift catalog which serves as an independent benchmark. Despite the lack of templates, line strength labels, or redshift labels, UnEZ's performance competes with modern redshift estimation algorithms. At SNR > 10, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 0.459% and a normalized median absolute deviation (NMAD) of $1.237 \times 10^{-4}$. At SNR > 3, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 3.89% and an NMAD of $1.471 \times 10^{-4}$. Furthermore, we demonstrate how UnEZ's line strength and redshift predictions can be utilized for various purposes such as constructing template spectra required in traditional redshift estimation algorithms. UnEZ illustrates how a fully unsupervised neural network can allow redshift, among other galaxy properties, to emerge naturally from the spectra themselves, providing a template-free, label-free approach that scales to the demands of future surveys.

Context. Hickson Compact Groups (HCGs) present an ideal environment for studying galaxy interactions. HCG 16 is a group of five galaxies in an intermediate stage of evolution (Phase 2), with several members displaying signs of interaction, including distorted morphologies, tidal features, and enhanced star formation rates. Aims. We aim to better understand the evolution of HCG 16 and its member galaxies, focusing on the relationship between neutral hydrogen and star formation, while reconstructing the interaction history of the group. Methods. We analysed MUSE data processed with pyPipe3D to extract H$α$ images and stellar population synthesis results, and reduced MeerKAT data to produce a high-resolution HI cube. We examined the structure and dynamics of the H$α$ and HI lines in both 2D and 3D, and produced spatially resolved star formation histories to compare the inner and outer parts of the galaxies. Results. We identify a major star formation episode in HCG 16 beginning ~400-500 Myr ago, coinciding with the passage of NGC 848 close to the other member galaxies, and that continued at least until 50-60 Myr ago in some galaxies. The HI distribution is highly perturbed in the group, although HCG 16a retains a nearly regular disk, while HCG 16b has associated H$α$ tails. Conclusions. The passage of NGC 848 probably activated star formation in HCG 16, but cannot by itself explain the current starburst activity in HCG 16c and 16d. The evolution of the group is better explained by multiple interactions among its members than by a single strong encounter.
Stellar Bars Reshape the Spatial Distribution of UV-bright Clumps in Nearby Galaxies
Authors
Zhimin Zhou, Jianfei Liu
Comments
20 pages, 16 figures. Accepted for publication in A&A
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA
Stellar bars redistribute gas and angular momentum in disk galaxies, but whether they mainly change the amount or the spatial organization of recent star formation remains uncertain. We use archival HST/WFC3 F275W imaging to study 70 barred galaxies and 95 unbarred control galaxies matched in stellar mass, optical color, redshift, and F275W background noise. A uniform dendrogram-based procedure identifies 95,486 UV-bright star-forming clumps. We compare their radial and azimuthal distributions, central F275W emission, and luminosity and size distribution functions. Relative to their controls, barred galaxies show a deficit of clumps at intermediate radii broadly corresponding to the bar region, followed by a local recovery near the bar ends. Their clumps are also preferentially concentrated along the bar major axis. These spatial differences are stronger in high-mass galaxies. Normalized bar length is more closely associated with the radial redistribution and central F275W concentration than bar ellipticity, although its relation to the azimuthal distribution is less uniform. When galactocentric radius is normalized by bar length, the recovery occurs near $R/R_{\rm bar}\sim1$, indicating that the bar sets a characteristic scale for the redistribution. Despite these spatial differences, barred and control galaxies have broadly similar clump luminosity and size distribution functions. Our results suggest that stellar bars mainly reorganize where recent star formation occurs, rather than substantially altering the observed luminosities and sizes of individual UV clumps, and that this effect depends on galaxy mass and bar extent.

We investigate past star formation in NGC 6357 using Gaia DR3 astrometry and Three-mm Ultimate Mopra Milky Way Survey (ThrUMMS) $^{12}$CO($J=1$--0) data. A Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) search independently recovers the previously cataloged cluster G352.8+0.6 in addition to Pismis 24, G353.1+0.6, and G353.2+0.6. Isochrone fitting yields an age of $7.9^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Myr for G352.8+0.6, compared with approximately 1--3 Myr for the three young clusters. Assuming constant relative plane-of-sky motion, a linear traceback places G352.8+0.6 closest to their reference center approximately 1.8 Myr ago, comparable to their inferred ages. The $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ CO component overlaps G352.8+0.6 in projection and shows localized depressions in integrated intensity around three spectroscopically identified massive stars belonging to G352.8+0.6. The $-4$ and $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ components show complementary projected distributions and intermediate-velocity CO emission in position--velocity space. Assuming that the $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ component was physically associated with G352.8+0.6 and shared its transverse motion, our principal interpretation is that the two molecular structures interacted around the epoch indicated by the stellar traceback, approximately 1.8 Myr ago. The resulting gas compression may have contributed to the recent episode of clustered and high-mass star formation in NGC 6357 and to the formation of Shell A, which subsequent stellar feedback may have reshaped.

We investigate the link between dust obscuration and star formation rate (SFR) estimators using Halpha luminosities, L_Halpha and radio luminosities, L_radio. While the Balmer Decrement (BD) is the standard tool for attenuating Halpha, it can fail to account for optically thick dust regions from which Halpha cannot escape. The ratio of SFRs estimated from radio luminosity and L_Halpha corrected for dust attenuation using the BD can be used as a probe of the degree of optically thick dust affecting Halpha. We explore this combining Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU) Early Science data at 887.5MHz in the Galaxy and Mass Assembly (GAMA) G23 field with the GAMA data. We draw on dust estimates using both the BD and the dust mass, M_d, demonstrating systematic underlying dependencies with L_radio/L_Halpha. By combining the BD and M_d, we quantify how they relate to L_radio/L_Halpha and other galaxy properties. We use this approach to establish an Halpha SFR (SFR_Halpha) estimator that accounts for optically thick dust, demonstrating its consistency with conventional SFR indicators. Rather than proposing a replacement for standard correction methods, we aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of a dust geometry model that integrates both BD and M_d parameters. This H_dust parameter provides a more physically complete representation of optically thick environments that traditional methods may overlook, and can produce SFR estimates consistent with traditional techniques.

We present new IRAM 30 m observations of CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) emission in five Coma cluster galaxies undergoing ram pressure stripping. Molecular gas is detected in four galaxies, and CO emission is clearly detected in the stripped tails of three, extending up to $\sim50$ kpc from the stellar disks. The molecular gas masses in the tails range from a few $\times10^8\,M_\odot$ and account for up to 10-20% of the molecular gas remaining in the parent disks. Compared with field scaling relations, several galaxies exhibit elevated molecular-to-stellar gas ratios despite being H I deficient. This enhancement persists toward the lower-mass end of our sample, even in the massive Coma cluster environment, consistent with ram-pressure-induced compression of atomic gas and enhanced H I-to-H$_2$ conversion in the disks. At the same time, the total gas fractions of most galaxies remain close to those of field galaxies with similar stellar masses when the molecular gas in the tails is included, indicating that gas removal and phase transformation act simultaneously. We further investigate the relation between molecular and ionized gas by comparing CO and H$α$ emission in the stripped regions. Although CO is preferentially detected in H$α$-bright regions, the increased scatter compared with previous studies suggests the presence of both star-forming and diffuse CO components in the tails. Our results highlight the dual role of ram pressure stripping in dense environments: it efficiently removes gas from galaxies while also promoting molecular gas formation in both the disks and stripped material, thereby shaping short-term star formation in cluster galaxies.

We present Hubble Space Telescope (HST) imaging of six Local Volume dwarf galaxies identified by the SEAMLESS survey - Cetus B, Hydrus A, Ursa Major C, Hydra B, Grapes, and M83-dw14 - along with Centaurus C (Cen C), discovered serendipitously in the M83-dw14 field. We report the discovery of M83-dw14 and Cen C for the first time. Our HST imaging resolves each system into individual stars, enabling measurements of tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) distances (3.6-6.5 Mpc), structural parameters, and star formation histories (SFHs). We complement these data with new HI observations from the Very Large Array for Ursa Major C, Hydra B, and M83-dw14 (also covering Cen C), and from the Green Bank Telescope for Grapes, together with existing HI limits for Cetus B and Hydrus A. With accurate TRGB distances, we place each target in its environmental context, ranging from a confirmed satellite (M83-dw14) to isolated systems (Hydrus A, Hydra B, Grapes, Cen C). Cen C ($M_V=-7.96$) is among the faintest isolated dwarfs known. It matches the size and luminosity of Leo T, the lowest mass gas-rich galaxy currently known, yet unlike Leo T it shows neither detected HI ($\log(M_{\rm HI}/M_{\odot})<5.3$) nor recent star formation. Ursa Major C shows strongly asymmetric, spatially offset HI, suggesting that feedback may efficiently displace gas in such low-mass systems. Overall, our sample exhibits a broad range of SFHs and quenching states that does not track simply with present-day environment. This diversity is consistent with recent evidence that similarly varied SFHs occur even among isolated low-mass dwarfs, pointing to the important roles of inhomogeneous reionization, stochastic mass assembly, and internal feedback alongside environmental effects.

Here we present deep JWST/NIRISS wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) that covers $\mathrm{Hα}$ and $\mathrm{Hβ}$ for galaxies at $0.5 < z < 3.5$ in the NGDEEP survey. The NGDEEP NIRISS observations detect emission line fluxes from galaxies in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field to $\mathrm{m_{F150W}} \lesssim$ 28 mag, enabling studies of star-formation based on $\mathrm{Hα}$ (or $\mathrm{Hβ}$, if necessary) in low-mass galaxies with $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 7}$ at z = 0.5 and $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 8}$ at z = 3.5. For 284 NGDEEP galaxies with $>3σ$ detections of $\mathrm{Hα}$ or $\mathrm{Hβ}$, we determine dust-corrected star-formation rates, $\mathrm{SFR_{Hα}}$, via flux measurements of these Balmer lines and compare them to the galaxies' stellar masses to investigate the properties of the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) on short, $\sim10$~Myr timescales across three redshift bins. When we contrast the intrinsic scatter in the $\mathrm{Hα}$--based SFMS to that of a SFMS comprised of UV--based SFRs, which trace star formation within the past $\sim 100$~Myr, we find that the \Ha--based SFMS's intrinsic scatter ($\approx0.35-0.5$~dex over $0.5 < z < 3.5$) is $\approx0.2-0.4$~dex higher than the UV--based SFMS's intrinsic scatter at $z < 2.5$. We argue that brief ($\lesssim10$~Myr) bursts of star formation likely account for this disparity in SFMS scatter. Understanding dust evolution is essential for interpreting the properties of galaxies across cosmic time. Nearby galaxies have revealed a detailed picture of dust enrichment, but whether the same picture applies in the early Universe remains unclear. Here we explore the origin of interstellar dust in simulated galaxies at $z \sim 5$. Starting from the dust-to-stellar mass relation, we select five simulated star-forming galaxies with $SFR> 1 M_\odot yr^{-1}$ and $ log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 8$. We focus on three low-mass systems with comparable stellar masses ($log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 8.7$) but significantly different dust masses ($log(M_{\rm dust}/M_\odot) \sim 5.1-6.2$), to investigate the origin of the scatter in the relation. Our analysis combines outputs from the cosmological hydrodynamical simulation dustyGadget, a semi-analytical model for stellar dust production, and radiative-transfer post-processing with SKIRT. We confirm that stellar dust production alone reproduces only the low-dust branch of the relation. Grain growth is required to recover both its S-shape and the observed scatter, as established for nearby galaxies. The transition to efficient grain growth occurs at lower metallicity than locally, consistent with the denser interstellar medium of high-redshift systems. Differences in dust content reflect different stages of galaxy assembly. Progressively longer star formation histories lead to greater chemical enrichment and denser ISM, promoting increasingly efficient grain growth. We therefore identify the assembly stage as the key regulator of dust growth at high redshift. Mock observations show that JWST multi-band imaging can reveal the structural diversity of galaxies within the scatter, although the reconstruction of clumpy morphologies may remain incomplete. Their dust continuum emission remains below the sensitivity of current ALMA continuum surveys.
Can the $\sim 5$ kpc starburst ring in MW create eROSITA bubbles?

Mukesh Singh Bisht, Kartick C. Sarkar

eROSITA bubbles (eRBs) in our Galaxy are thought to be due to the energy injection by the central star-forming region or the central Super Massive Black Hole. Identifying the origin of these bubbles is crucial in constraining the origin of the Fermi Bubbles and the nature of the Galactic feedback. However, some recent opinions suggest that the eRBs may not even have been produced by any central source, but rather a result of a star-formation ring at $R\sim 5$ kpc. In this letter, we perform detailed hydrodynamic simulations to refute that claim and show that such a ring of star formation alone is not able to produce the symmetric eROSITA bubbles - one needs a compact energy injection from the Galactic Center to produce these bubbles. Our study, therefore, suggests that the eROSITA bubbles truly represent the nuclear outflow from our Galaxy.

Reliable collisional data are essential for interpreting the rotational emission of HMgC$_3$N under the non-equilibrium conditions of molecular clouds. We investigate the rotational excitation and de-excitation of HMgC$_3$N induced by collisions with He, combining \textit{ab initio} calculations with quantum scattering theory. A two-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) for HMgC$_3$N--He was constructed using the CCSD(T)-F12A method in conjunction with the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set. Quantum close-coupling (CC) calculation were then performed to obtain state-to-state integral cross sections and temperature-dependent rate coefficients for rotational transitions with $Δj=\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm3$, covering rotational levels up to $j=20$ and temperatures from $3$ to $50$~K. The rate coefficients exhibit a clear propensity for $Δj=\pm2$ transitions. The resulting collisional data were incorporated into radiative transfer modelling to constrain the physical conditions and column density of HMgC$_3$N from its observed spectral line emission. A $χ^2$ minimization yields a best-fitting H$_2$ volume density of $n(\mathrm{H}_2)=1.233\times10^5~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and column density of $N(\mathrm{HMgC}_3\mathrm{N})=1.831\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. The derived column density is in approximate agreement with the observational estimate of $(3.0\pm0.6)\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. These results provide new collisional data for non-LTE studies of HMgC$_3$N and support a more realistic treatment of its rotational excitation and radiative transfer in molecular clouds.

While numerous studies have shown that satellite galaxies experience declining star formation rates after infall, direct comparisons across different surveys and redshifts are complicated by varying sample selections, environmental metrics, and techniques used to derive galaxy properties. In this work, we select satellite galaxies from the highly complete Galaxy and Mass Assembly (GAMA) Survey and the Deep Extragalactic VIsible Legacy Survey (DEVILS) to investigate environmental quenching out to z~0.4. These surveys derive galaxy properties and environments in a consistent manner, minimizing systematic biases and enabling a robust comparison across redshift. Using projected phase space as a proxy for infall time, we quantify the evolution of the quenched fraction and trace how satellites evolve relative to the star-forming main sequence (SFMS). We find consistent trends between GAMA and DEVILS, with satellites exhibiting a steady decline in star formation relative to the SFMS at a rate of ~0.14 dex/Gyr following infall. Extending this analysis to include visual morphology, we find similar declining trends for both early- and late-type systems. Overall, we find no significant redshift evolution between GAMA and DEVILS, indicating that group and cluster environments have a comparable impact on the star formation activity of satellite galaxies over the past ~4.5 Gyr.

Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to ~100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.

Using JWST/NIRISS slitless spectroscopy from the PASSAGE program, we analyze 17 spectroscopically confirmed emission-line galaxies in candidate interacting systems at $z \sim 1.7$--$3.2$. These galaxies span stellar masses of $7.3 \lesssim \log(M_*/M_\odot) \lesssim 10.4$ and global gas-phase metallicities of $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H}) \sim 7.6$--$8.7$. From spatially resolved emission-line maps, we measure radial metallicity gradients separately for each galaxy, finding values from approximately $-0.2$ to $+0.45$ dex kpc$^{-1}$. The mean metallicity gradients in bins of stellar mass and redshift are consistent with previous measurements, and their scatter is not significantly different from that of the heterogeneous literature compilation in either the metallicity-gradient--stellar-mass or metallicity-gradient--redshift relation. We find tentative evidence for an anti-correlation between the metallicity gradient and the metallicity offset from the mass--metallicity relation, with a best-fit slope of $-0.30 \pm 0.06$, possibly reflecting interaction-driven variations in star formation and chemical enrichment. Galaxies in pairs with comparable component masses tend to exhibit positive metallicity gradients, whereas galaxies in unequal-mass pairs tend to exhibit negative gradients; neither trend is statistically significant in the present sample. Overall, our results reveal substantial diversity in the metallicity distributions of candidate interacting systems at cosmic noon and provide tentative evidence that galaxy interactions may influence the redistribution of gas and metals.

We present a study of Coma-B122, an unusually clumpy star-forming ultra-diffuse galaxy (UDG) in the Coma cluster outskirts, using deep Keck Cosmic Web Imager integral-field spectroscopy and Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam imaging. B122 hosts $\sim10$ luminous star-forming clumps contributing $\sim25$% of its luminosity and $\sim7$% of its stellar mass, with typical clump masses of $\sim10^6\ M_\odot$ comparable to the most massive super star clusters known in nearby starbursting dwarfs. The clumps form a distinctive ring configuration $\sim2$ kpc from the center. Full-spectrum fitting shows that $\sim12.5$% of the diffuse body's stellar mass formed during an extended episode of enhanced star formation over the past $\sim300$ Myr, within which all the clumps assembled. B122 shows evidence of weak ram-pressure stripping (RPS), including a diffuse light distribution truncated on the side facing the Coma center and extended to the opposite side, along with an asymmetric H I velocity profile. Its ordered stellar and ionized-gas rotation, cold gas fraction, and phase-space position are consistent with an early stage of infall. Spatial variations in clump star formation histories and gas-phase abundances further indicate that RPS-driven metal-poor gas inflow may have triggered and sustained the recent star formation. B122 may offer a rare present-day view of how UDGs assemble their globular cluster (GC) systems during cluster infall, although it remains uncertain if the clumps could survive as GCs. Future observations with higher spatial resolution and of more analogs are important to provide a complete picture of the formation and evolution of clumpy low-surface-brightness dwarf galaxies.

[Abridged]Extreme Emission Line Galaxies (EELGs) are compact, low-mass systems undergoing intense bursts of star formation, whose physical conditions resemble those of galaxies in the early Universe. We characterise the local environment of EELGs identified in the J-PAS survey to determine whether their extreme properties are associated with the presence of nearby companions, as expected if galaxy-galaxy interactions were the main trigger of the burst. We analyse 917 EELGs from the J-PAS internal data release at $0.03 \lesssim z \lesssim 0.9$ over $\sim$30 deg$^2$. We use the Pair Probability Function formalism, based on the full photometric redshift probability distributions, to identify companions within projected separations up to 200 kpc and velocity differences up to 500 km s$^{-1}$. Stellar masses, star formation rates, dust attenuation, and metallicity estimates were derived for the EELGs and their companions through SED fitting with CIGALE, and Monte Carlo simulations were used to correct for projection and completeness effects. The average pair fraction of the EELGs is $f_\mathrm{pair} = 0.162^{+0.048}_{-0.033}$, indicating that only $\sim$16% of the sample has detectable companions, a value lower than the observed average for other star-forming galaxies within the adopted limits. When detected within the sensitivity and stellar-mass completeness limits of J-PAS, companions tend to be more massive and to show higher SED-based metallicity estimates than the EELGs, suggesting that the latter are typically the low-mass components of asymmetric pairs. However, lower-mass companions below these limits may remain undetected. The Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR) extends the mass-metallicity relation (MZR) by including star formation rate (SFR) as a third parameter. At fixed stellar mass, galaxies with higher SFRs tend to have lower gas-phase metallicities. We investigate the physical origin of the FMR in the context of episodic star formation (Gui et al. 2026, paper I of this series) by tracing the evolution histories of 8,979 central star-forming galaxies selected at $z=0$ in TNG100 back to $z=1$. We find that the overall MZR scatter at $z=0$ ($\sim0.11$ dex) comprises both long-lived differences among galaxies ($\sim0.09$ dex) and temporal fluctuations within individual galaxies ($\sim 0.07$ dex). The galaxy-to-galaxy component shows an anticorrelation between SFR and gas-phase metallicity, as shown by the observed FMR. As for the consequence from temporal fluctuation, we track individual histories and stack the upward and downward SFR phases of a refined sample. Time-shift analysis and stacked histories show a well-behaved sequence in which the cold non-star-forming gas reservoir increases (decreases) first, followed by a decrease (increase) in gas-phase metallicity and then a rise (decline) in SFR. This ordering is consistent with a physical scenario where metal-poor gas first replenishes the cold reservoir and dilutes the gas before SFR rises to the peak, followed by feedback-driven gas heating/removal, resulting in a shift in metallicity from dilution to enrichment as SFR decreases towards the valley. This naturally explains the anticorrelation between the SFR and gas-phase metallicities as indicated by the observed FMR.

Leo T, a Milky Way (MW) satellite, is the lowest-mass gas-rich galaxy known to show recent star formation, making it an excellent laboratory for investigating star formation in the ultraviolet (UV) regime within a low-density, metal-poor environment. We present a far-UV (FUV) study using Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope (UVIT) data, aiming to characterize FUV star-forming clusters (SFCs), identify FUV-emitting stars, constrain their properties, and explore star formation in an extremely low-mass, metal-poor galaxy. We examined the galaxy's FUV extent and compared it with the H I distribution. We extracted thirty SFCs and derived their properties, including area, luminosity, and star formation rate (SFR), and compared them with previous studies. FUV-emitting stars and their stellar parameters were identified through cross-matching with catalogs from the Hubble Space Telescope and the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE). We also estimated the global SFR and star formation efficiencies (SFEs) associated with previously identified CO clouds. The FUV luminosities of the SFCs confirm the absence of massive O and B-type stars in Leo T. The FUV-emitting stellar population consists of main-sequence (MS) stars, blue loop (BL) stars, horizontal branch stars, and emission-line (EM) stars. The upper MS and EM stars are concentrated near the optical center, whereas the lower MS and BL stars are more spatially extended. We find that five out of the six FUV-emitting stars with MUSE spectroscopy are extremely metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\leq$ -3). Despite the recent detection of CO(1-0) emission, no SFCs are associated with the molecular clouds, which exhibit extremely low SFEs. The spatial distribution of the SFCs suggests a directional interaction with the MW.

Recent high-sensitivity and high-resolution surveys of the Third Galactic Quadrant [$219.75 \leq l \leq 229.75$ and $-5.25 \leq b \leq 5.25$], such as the Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting ( MWISP) survey, have revealed a significant population of molecular clouds (MCs) that appear virially unbound ($α_{vir} > 2$) according to traditional isolated MC models. However, these apparently unstable cloud structures are actually found long-lived and actively undertaking star formation. This inconsistency presents a Virial Paradox. In the present work, we employ a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) to address this discrepancy by re-evaluating the stability of 2,091 MCs . Unlike traditional machine learning, our PINN model embeds the complete Eulerian Virial Theorem including external surface pressure support ($P_{ext}$) directly into the network's loss function. This model autonomously discovers a global Galactic pressure field that scales inversely with vertical height ($|z|$) and Galactocentric radius ($R_{GC}$), consistent with the properties of the Galactic Flare. By incorporating this AI-derived pressure support, we demonstrate a dramatic shift in the stability census: 99.9\% of previously thought unbound clouds are found to be in a state of pressure confined equilibrium ($α_{AI} \leq 2$). Furthermore, we find a robust log-log correlation ($R = 0.64$) between our corrected stability parameter and the star formation rate (SFR). This correlation provides independent physical validation that environmental confinement is a primary regulator of star forming efficiency in the outer Galaxy.
