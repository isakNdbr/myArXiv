Sherlock 'Chrones: The Curious Case of NGC 6791 and Asteroseismic Targets Therein Reveals Mixing Length Dependence on Evolutionary Stage

As single-aged populations containing stars spanning a range of masses but otherwise with similar properties, star clusters provide valuable testbeds for stellar evolution theory. The old, metal-rich, open cluster NGC 6791 is well studied, but the availability of asteroseismic observations of individual cluster members allows for a more in-depth analysis. Driven by mounting evidence that the solar assumption for convection used in almost all 1D stellar models is not adequate, and in light of the increased precision provided by modern data and asteroseismic constraints, we built and compared custom isochrones with varying values of the convective mixing length, $α_\text{MLT}$, to two clusters with well-determined ages: NGC 6791 and the well-studied, metal-poor globular cluster M92. For both clusters, we found that the best-fitting mixing length parameter decreases monotonically as a function of evolutionary phase, finding values between $0.98-1.10α_{\odot}$ for NGC 6791 and $1.16-1.21α_{\odot}$ for M92. In NGC 6791, this corresponds to an 18% decrease in the best fitting mixing length between the main sequence turn-off and upper end of the red giant branch. The degree of variation found within this one object is therefore comparable to variation across the literature in $α_\text{MLT}$ fits for other non-solar targets, calling single-valued $α_\text{MLT}$ fits to clusters into question. It is also consistent with $\log g$-$α_\text{MLT}$ trends predicted by higher-fidelity, 3D convection analyses. Our results therefore illustrate the need for more flexibility in our treatment of the mixing length in isochrones to properly model clusters.
The origin of the apparent ULX deficit in NGC 3256

Ultra-luminous X-ray (ULX) sources are bright, off-nuclear X-ray point sources, mostly associated with high-mass X-ray binaries (HMXBs), with a compact object accreting from a donor star at super-Eddington luminosities. Although their HMXB nature implies a connection to star formation processes, previous works have found that luminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs) appear to have a deficit of ULXs compared to their very high star formation rates (SFRs). Here, we analyze three Chandra observations of NGC 3256, a merging LIRG with SFR ~50-60 Msun/yr. Based on local scaling relations, it could be expected to host between 100-120 individual ULXs, which could have a non-negligible impact on the energetics and feedback processes of the galaxy. We aim to characterize all its detected X-ray point sources to determine if there is a ULX deficit in this galaxy. We also aim to explore the potential impact of host-galaxy obscuration and metallicity in said deficit. We characterize the luminosity of each X-ray point source in each Chandra observation, and use all observations to determine their potential ULX nature and the fraction of transient ULXs, as well as construct a ULX luminosity function for this galaxy. We find that the galaxy hosts 10-15 ULXs, making it one of the most ULX-rich galaxies known to date. While comparison with the ULX/SFR ratio of nearby galaxies predicted as many as ~100 ULXs, we show that, when accounting for its very high metallicity, NGC 3256 does not have a ULX deficit; and may even present an excess. While the high obscuration in LIRGs has previously been invoked to explain the ULX deficit, we conclude that it is not required to explain the ULX to SFR ratio in NGC 3256. If obscuration still plays a role, then LIRGs may actually be significantly more efficient at producing ULXs than normal galaxies (of the same metallicity), in disagreement with previous results.
On the feasibility of using [Eu/$α$] to identify accreted and in-situ extragalactic globular clusters

The chemical abundance ratio [Eu/$α$] has been proposed as a discriminator between ex-situ and in-situ stellar populations in the Milky Way and external galaxies. Here we show with a high-quality, homogeneous dataset of integrated-light abundances for 28 globular clusters (GCs), where 21 are extragalactic and 7 galactic, that this behaviour holds in other Local Group galaxies. In particular, we find metal-rich ([Fe/H] > --0.7) M31 GCs have uniformly low [Eu/Si]$\sim-0.4$, indicating they represent an in-situ population. Lower metallicity GCs in M31 generally show high [Eu/Si] $\sim+0.4$, suggesting they originated in accreted progenitors, and these GCs lie at larger projected radii from the M31 centre. Furthermore, we compare the M31 GC abundances with similar determinations for the M31 bulge for Na, Mg, Si, Ti, Ca, Eu, Cr, Mn, Ba, Ni, and Cu. The in-situ M31 GCs and M31 bulge exhibit similar abundance trends, excepting Ba -- and possibly Cr and Ti, suggesting similar star formation processes. [Eu/Si] in the M31 bulge is consistent with in-situ Milky Way stars, but is higher than the M31 in-situ GCs. For M33 and intermediate-mass dwarf galaxies, we identify a mixture of in-situ and ex-situ GCs, while for lower-mass dwarfs, we identify primarily in-situ GCs. These results suggest that accreted GCs in massive galaxies originated, at least in part, from intermediate-mass, intermediate-metallicity progenitors. We support earlier suggestions that [Eu/$α$] is a powerful in-/ex-situ separator for extragalactic GCs, demonstrating the potential of high-resolution integrated-light abundances for extragalactic archaeology.
Ultra-diffuse Galaxies in the Kilo-Degree Survey: Globular Cluster Systems in Cluster and Field Environments

Ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs) are extreme low-surface-brightness systems whose formation pathways remain debated. Globular clusters (GCs) can trace their early assembly, but wide-area studies of UDG GC populations remain scarce. We study GC systems of 545 high-quality UDG candidates identified in the Kilo-Degree Survey (KiDS) with UDGnet, using a dedicated GC-detection pipeline. We identify 24 UDGs with $N_{\rm GC} \geq 3$ (14 associated with groups or clusters and 10 in the field), and use the globular cluster luminosity function to estimate their distances and physical sizes. At fixed luminosity or stellar mass, group- and cluster-associated UDGs tend to have higher GC counts and specific frequencies than field UDGs, although the distributions overlap substantially. Most systems with $S_N \gtrsim 40$ reside in groups or clusters, while field UDGs generally occupy the lower part of the distribution. Group- and cluster-associated UDGs also more often lie near the upper envelope of the $N_{\rm GC}$--$M_\star$ relation. Empirical GC--halo mass relations consequently imply higher halo masses at fixed stellar mass and lower stellar-to-halo mass ratios, although this environmental trend is mild. These findings are consistent with efficient early GC formation followed by suppressed or prematurely quenched field-star formation, suggesting that GC-rich UDGs may represent an extension of the dwarf-galaxy population. The environmental trends may reflect differences in early assembly, quenching, infall, and survival histories rather than a sharp field--cluster dichotomy.

Context: Mapping gas flows in the interstellar medium is key to understanding the formation of structure in our Milky Way. Methods that simultaneously reconstruct clouds' distances and line-of-sight velocities are called kinetic tomography (KT). Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are powerful tracers for such maps because they trace the integrated dust density and the dust-weighted velocity. Previous DIB-KT works had small samples with coarse kpc-scale resolution and could not capture vertical disk structure. Aims: Make 4D (3D position + radial velocity) maps of the Milky Way from new, high-quality 15,273 Å DIB detections in APOGEE DR17. Methods: We combine variational inference and Gaussian processes with a mixture likelihood for low signal-to-noise detections and marginalization over distance uncertainties. We calibrate the model on mock fields and spectral injection tests and validate the map against independent tracers of the dust density (3D dust maps) and velocity substructure (masers and young stellar clusters). Results: We recover a posterior distribution over 3D maps of the density and velocity structure of the 15,273 Å DIB within 6 kpc of the Sun. We measure the large-scale structure of the DIB disk (scale length, height and flare) and detect vertical distortions consistent with the warp. On smaller scales, the map resolves distance ambiguities in gas longitude-velocity diagrams. From the second-order velocity structure function, we measure a slope (0.64 $\pm$ 0.04) consistent with Kolmogorov turbulence, amplitude consistent with expectations for molecular clouds, and a preliminary ratio of compressive-to-solenoidal modes. Conclusions: With larger DIB catalogs expected from SDSS-V, this framework should resolve kinematic substructure at the scales needed to constrain the formation and dynamics of individual clouds and voids. Water-ice formation in Ophiuchus follows the local irradiation

Stefan Meingast, Zhetai Cao

Primary

astro-ph.GA
Water ice only appears on interstellar dust grains beyond a certain depth into a molecular cloud. This depth has so far been quoted as one extinction threshold per cloud. We measured the threshold along 43009 sightlines through the Ophiuchus cloud with the ice color-excess method, which converts archival WISE and Spitzer photometry of background stars into the peak optical depth of the 3 $μ$m water-ice band. We sorted the sightlines by the 12 $μ$m surface brightness of the cloud at fixed extinction, a proxy for the ultraviolet field that excites the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons at the cloud surface. The onset of ice formation lies at higher extinction toward sightlines with brighter 12 $μ$m emission, ranging from $A_{K_S}^{\rm th} = 0.67$ to $1.15$ mag. It increases by 0.58 to 0.90 mag in $A_{K_S}$ per factor of ten in 12 $μ$m brightness, depending on the adopted reddening coefficient. Because the 12 $μ$m-bright and -faint sightlines lie in different parts of the cloud, we repeated the comparison within each of the five subregions, and four of the subregions show the same trend. The trend also persists among sightlines with a similar amount of diffuse material around them. A later onset in more strongly irradiated gas is expected from photodesorption by external ultraviolet light, although a comparison of its size with models requires a calibration of the 12 $μ$m brightness against the strength of the field. The extinction at which water ice appears thus varies with the local environment, which complicates comparisons between clouds. Photometric ice maps resolve this variation across entire clouds, and the SPHEREx and JWST ice surveys can test it spectroscopically.

Deuterated molecules probe the physical and chemical evolution of molecular clouds and star-forming regions. Their abundances depend on temperature, density, and evolutionary stage. DCN and DNC are relatively abundant deuterated isomers that probe temperature-dependent deuterium chemistry. Although [HCN]/[HNC] is often thought to increase in warmer gas, the temperature dependence of [DCN]/[DNC] remains less well constrained. To investigate how [DCN]/[DNC] depends on the gas kinetic temperature, we observed the $J$=1-0 transitions of DCN, DNC, H$^{13}$CN, HN$^{13}$C, HC$^{15}$N, and H$^{15}$NC toward 101 late-stage massive star-forming regions using the Yebes 40m telescope. Gas kinetic temperatures were estimated from CH$_3$CCH 5-4, whose rotation temperature is closely related to the kinetic temperature of dense gas. These temperatures were adopted in the column density calculations and in the analysis of temperature-dependent abundance ratios. We find a moderate positive correlation between the abundance ratio [DCN]/[DNC] and kinetic temperature, which is statistically significant and indicates that DCN becomes progressively more abundant relative to DNC in warmer gas. A weak negative correlation with temperature is observed for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HNC})$, whereas no statistically significant correlation is found for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HCN})$. The [HCN]/[HNC] ratios show substantial scatter and no statistically significant correlation with temperature, possibly due in part to optical-depth effects in the $^{13}$C isotopologues and the limited sensitivity of the $^{15}$N isotopologue observations. These results suggest that [DCN]/[DNC] may be governed not only by HCN/HNC isomeric chemistry but also by temperature-dependent deuterium fractionation pathways and the chemical evolution of dense gas.

A new orbital solution for $ξ$ Bootis (GJ 566 AB, P=152 yrs) and the recently revised orbital solution for 70 Ophiuchi (GJ 702 AB, P=88 yrs) yield component masses precise to 0.4\%. Their relatively wide separations ($>0.4^{\prime\prime}$) allow the components to be studied independently, making them ideal for testing the predictions of stellar evolutionary models. We use the six-telescope near-infrared MIRC-X and MYSTIC beam combiners at the CHARA Array to measure the angular diameters of all 4 stars. Combined with precise Gaia DR3 parallax values and bolometric fluxes, these interferometric measurements translate to stellar radii that are precise to $<2.4\%$ and effective temperatures precise to $<1.7\%$. We compare the measured radii and temperatures to four stellar evolutionary models (Lyon, Dartmouth, PARSEC, MIST). Over the mass range $0.67 - 0.91$ M$_\odot$, all models reproduce the observed properties within a few standard deviations, with no systematic offsets. The pairs are coeval and consistent with independent age estimates, further validating model predictions. With physical separations greater than 20 au, these pairs show no evidence of radius inflation, despite being spectroscopically classified as chromospherically active, and in the case of $ξ$ Bootis, probable members of the adolescent age ($\sim 0.4$ Gyr) Ursa Majoris Moving Group. In contrast, a set of 21 short-period ($P\lesssim$ 10 days) eclipsing binary stars with similar dynamical masses and metallicities are larger and often cooler than these same model predictions. Within this mass range, radius inflation appears to be associated with short-period binary star systems.

High-resolution extinction maps of resolved external galaxies, such as M31, are crucial not only for accurately correcting dust extinction and reddening toward individual sources within the galaxy, but also for characterizing the distribution and evolution of interstellar dust in external galactic environments. We aim to construct a high-resolution and large-coverage stellar-traced extinction map of M31, based on multi-band photometric data from the HST PHAT and PHAST surveys. Using a stellar template library built from the PARSEC stellar evolution models and adopting the average extinction law of M31, we apply a matching method analogous to the SPar algorithm to derive A_V values for approximately 65 million stars. The extinction map is then generated using a HEALPix adaptive-resolution scheme. The resulting map covers approximately two-thirds of the star-forming disk of M31, with a maximum spatial resolution of 1.61 arcsec. The median extinction across the map is A_V = 1.24 mag. Comparison with previously published stellar- and dust-emission-based extinction maps shows broadly similar large-scale morphology, while differences remain in the extinction values. The extinction map further reveals fine-scale features such as inter-arm filaments, supernova-driven cavities, and spur-like structures. The map has been publicly released through the NADC platform and is expected to serve as an important observational basis for extinction correction in subsequent astronomical observations and for related M31 scientific studies.
From a mass invariant in a gravo-turbulent medium to the characteristic mass of the star-forming cores

Pierre Dumond, Gilles Chabrier, Jérémy Fensch, Etienne Jaupart

The origin of the observed universality, or, say, the limited variations, of the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and its precursor, the prestellar core mass function (CMF), and thus of the characteristic mass of stars and cores, across a wide range of different galactic environments remains an open, fundamental problem in astrophysics. In this paper, we demonstrate that we can explain this property from gravity and turbulence only, using a mass time-invariant that remains valid for a broad class of turbulent flows throughout the star formation process. This mass invariant is entirely determined by the large-scale normalisation of the field of density fluctuations in the Galactic Interstellar Medium (ISM), induced by turbulence. Using high-resolution numerical simulations, we first verify that the temporal invariance of this quantity is preserved in a self-gravitating turbulent medium. Then, introducing a self-consistent model of the statistical properties of the density field in the self-gravitating multiphase turbulent ISM, we show that the universality of the characteristic mass of star-forming cores is imposed by this invariant together with the scaling of the Larson relations. Our model successfully predicts the characteristic mass of the cores in Milky Way-like star-forming regions, as well as in more extreme environments such as high-mass star-forming regions and massive early-type galaxies. This scenario suggests that the characteristic mass of the CMF, and then probably the characteristic stellar mass, originates from a universal, fundamental property of turbulence and is imprinted both by the very early stages of the star formation process in the gravo-turbulent interstellar medium, when the dynamics of the medium are still dominated by turbulence, and by the statistical properties of small-scale self-gravitating structures.
