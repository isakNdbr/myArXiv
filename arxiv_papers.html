Episodic Protostellar Outflows: A Proper Motion Study in Serpens South
Authors
Simran Kaur, Adele L. Plunkett, Zhi-Yun Li, Per Bjerkeli, Manuel Fernandez-Lopez, Jon P. Ramsey, Hector G. Arce, Mihika Rao
Comments
No comment found
Primary
astro-ph.SR
Categories
astro-ph.SR astro-ph.GA
Serpens South is a notable protostellar cluster with signatures of recent star formation, including a high protostellar fraction. Within the region, several protostars provide important mechanical feedback via outflows. We investigate the molecular outflow driven by the Class 0 protostar known as CARMA-7, which is located in the central hub of Serpens South. Two epochs of observations with the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) with sub-arcsecond resolution capture the well-collimated, episodic outflow with distinct ejecta knots. This outflow, traced with 12CO J = 3-2 and SiO J = 8-7, appears to be mostly blueshifted to the south and redshifted to the north, with radial velocities up to at least 25 km/s. We present the first proper motion study of this outflow by comparing observations taken in 2017 and 2021. The measured proper motions trace the ejection history and refine the inferred outflow morphology. We find that proper motions of the 12CO J = 3-2 and SiO J = 8-7 knots range between 10 - 40 mas/yr, corresponding to 30-90 km/s. Using radial velocities and proper motions, we determined that the inclinations of the outflow lobes are ~60 -80 degrees relative to the line of sight. Finally, we measure ejection timescales as recent as ~10 to ~120 years, and we discuss this in the context of other known protostars with recent ejecta events.
The vertical distribution of molecular gas in the fourth quadrant of the Milky Way
Authors
L. -H. Lin, F. Wyrowski, S. Neupane, D. Colombo, K. M. Menten
Comments
6 pages + 6 pages appendices, accepted for publication in A&A
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA astro-ph.SR
The vertical distribution of molecular gas in the Milky Way disk is poorly studied, especially in the 4th quadrant and the far side of the Galaxy. While surveys over 20 years ago suffered from coarse beam and non-Nyquist sampling, most of the recent surveys are limited to $|b|<1^\circ$ and/or focus on the Northern Galactic plane. We examine the molecular gas's vertical distribution and properties in the fourth quadrant to check the impact of Galactic structure on current star formation. We mapped two arm tangent regions ($l=[329^\circ.0,327^\circ.0]$ for the Norma-Outer arm, and $l=[310^\circ.0,308^\circ.5]$ for the Scutum-Centaurus arm) with the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line using the 7-pixel LASMA receiver of the APEX 12 m telescope, covering up to $b=[-2^\circ,+2^\circ]$ (or $|z|\sim200$ pc near the tangent points). The noise level of the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line is about 0.4 K [$T_{MB}$] in velocity channels of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$. The vertical distribution around the tangent regions of the spiral arm is fitted with two Gaussian components, the narrow one with an FWHM thickness of 25 pc and the wide one of about 100 pc. Two explanations are proposed: they are two sub-components of the thin disk (the narrowest tracing the spiral arm and the other the inter arms components), or they trace both thin and thick disks that show a smaller thickness when observed with CO 3-2 than when observed with CO 1-0. The latter indicates that the density distribution of the interstellar medium exhibits a steeper gradient at larger heights above the plane. Clumps in the two tangent regions are searched and divided into groups by relative height. Compared with the low relative height clumps ($|z|<50$ pc), the median relative height clumps ($50~\mathrm{pc}<|z|<100~\mathrm{pc}$) have lower excitation temperature and surface density, with fewer high values in the distribution. A similar tentative trend is found for the mass.
A VLT/CRIRES+ study of helium 1.083$μ$m absorption features towards the inner Orion Nebula

Comments
Accepted for publication in MNRAS

JWST NIRCam F115W observations towards the inner Orion Nebula revealed a diverse array of previously unknown dark features. The most likely cause was absorption or suppressed emission of the helium 1.083$μ$m triplet which lies in that filter. We have obtained VLT/CRIRES+ high-resolution spectrographic observations over a selection of different dark targets, confirming that the features are indeed explained by a reduction in the flux of the helium 1.083$μ$m triplet, a reduction that is fairly uniform across the line. The Orion Nebula is a blister HII region, with a dense, bright ionised layer on the far side from the observer near the Orion A molecular cloud and lower density gas elsewhere. Using a simple radiative transfer model, we show that dark features arise when emission from the dense layer is absorbed by intermediate density helium (e.g. in clumps, globules, or winds from objects like proplyds) on the near side of the dense layer.
Dust-induced reversal of the optical wavelength dependence of spiral-arm pitch angles
Authors
Eric E. Martínez-García, Rosa A. González-Lópezlira, Ivânio Puerari, Arturo I. Gómez-Ruiz
Comments
Accepted for publication in MNRAS
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA astro-ph.CO
The classical density-wave scenario predicts a blue-to-red age--colour gradient across the spiral arms of disc galaxies as a consequence of shock-induced star formation. However, observations of spiral-arm pitch angles at different wavelengths sometimes suggest the opposite trend, namely a red-to-blue ``colour jump''. The origin of this apparent contradiction remains unclear. In this work, we investigate the effects of dust attenuation on post-shock stellar emission, together with the streamline motions of gas and stellar populations. We find that dust attenuation in regions with relatively high optical depths, $\hatτ_{V} \gtrsim 0.2$, can produce an apparent red-to-blue spatial sequence, with respect to the shock location, by reversing the relative positions of the flux maxima of stellar populations observed in different bands. Moreover, streamline motions confine post-shock stellar populations near the spiral arms for longer than in a circular-orbit model, thereby modifying the spatial distribution of the emitted light. We conclude that the apparent red-to-blue colour jump does not necessarily contradict the classical density-wave scenario. Instead, it can arise from the same post-shock stellar populations that intrinsically produce a blue-to-red age--colour gradient if their emission is modified by dust attenuation.
Stellar Bars Reshape the Spatial Distribution of UV-bright Clumps in Nearby Galaxies
Authors
Zhimin Zhou, Jianfei Liu
Comments
20 pages, 16 figures. Accepted for publication in A&A
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA
Stellar bars redistribute gas and angular momentum in disk galaxies, but whether they mainly change the amount or the spatial organization of recent star formation remains uncertain. We use archival HST/WFC3 F275W imaging to study 70 barred galaxies and 95 unbarred control galaxies matched in stellar mass, optical color, redshift, and F275W background noise. A uniform dendrogram-based procedure identifies 95,486 UV-bright star-forming clumps. We compare their radial and azimuthal distributions, central F275W emission, and luminosity and size distribution functions. Relative to their controls, barred galaxies show a deficit of clumps at intermediate radii broadly corresponding to the bar region, followed by a local recovery near the bar ends. Their clumps are also preferentially concentrated along the bar major axis. These spatial differences are stronger in high-mass galaxies. Normalized bar length is more closely associated with the radial redistribution and central F275W concentration than bar ellipticity, although its relation to the azimuthal distribution is less uniform. When galactocentric radius is normalized by bar length, the recovery occurs near $R/R_{\rm bar}\sim1$, indicating that the bar sets a characteristic scale for the redistribution. Despite these spatial differences, barred and control galaxies have broadly similar clump luminosity and size distribution functions. Our results suggest that stellar bars mainly reorganize where recent star formation occurs, rather than substantially altering the observed luminosities and sizes of individual UV clumps, and that this effect depends on galaxy mass and bar extent.

We investigate past star formation in NGC 6357 using Gaia DR3 astrometry and Three-mm Ultimate Mopra Milky Way Survey (ThrUMMS) $^{12}$CO($J=1$--0) data. A Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) search independently recovers the previously cataloged cluster G352.8+0.6 in addition to Pismis 24, G353.1+0.6, and G353.2+0.6. Isochrone fitting yields an age of $7.9^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Myr for G352.8+0.6, compared with approximately 1--3 Myr for the three young clusters. Assuming constant relative plane-of-sky motion, a linear traceback places G352.8+0.6 closest to their reference center approximately 1.8 Myr ago, comparable to their inferred ages. The $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ CO component overlaps G352.8+0.6 in projection and shows localized depressions in integrated intensity around three spectroscopically identified massive stars belonging to G352.8+0.6. The $-4$ and $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ components show complementary projected distributions and intermediate-velocity CO emission in position--velocity space. Assuming that the $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ component was physically associated with G352.8+0.6 and shared its transverse motion, our principal interpretation is that the two molecular structures interacted around the epoch indicated by the stellar traceback, approximately 1.8 Myr ago. The resulting gas compression may have contributed to the recent episode of clustered and high-mass star formation in NGC 6357 and to the formation of Shell A, which subsequent stellar feedback may have reshaped.

We present new IRAM 30 m observations of CO(1-0) and CO(2-1) emission in five Coma cluster galaxies undergoing ram pressure stripping. Molecular gas is detected in four galaxies, and CO emission is clearly detected in the stripped tails of three, extending up to $\sim50$ kpc from the stellar disks. The molecular gas masses in the tails range from a few $\times10^8\,M_\odot$ and account for up to 10-20% of the molecular gas remaining in the parent disks. Compared with field scaling relations, several galaxies exhibit elevated molecular-to-stellar gas ratios despite being H I deficient. This enhancement persists toward the lower-mass end of our sample, even in the massive Coma cluster environment, consistent with ram-pressure-induced compression of atomic gas and enhanced H I-to-H$_2$ conversion in the disks. At the same time, the total gas fractions of most galaxies remain close to those of field galaxies with similar stellar masses when the molecular gas in the tails is included, indicating that gas removal and phase transformation act simultaneously. We further investigate the relation between molecular and ionized gas by comparing CO and H$α$ emission in the stripped regions. Although CO is preferentially detected in H$α$-bright regions, the increased scatter compared with previous studies suggests the presence of both star-forming and diffuse CO components in the tails. Our results highlight the dual role of ram pressure stripping in dense environments: it efficiently removes gas from galaxies while also promoting molecular gas formation in both the disks and stripped material, thereby shaping short-term star formation in cluster galaxies.

We investigate the link between dust obscuration and star formation rate (SFR) estimators using Halpha luminosities, L_Halpha and radio luminosities, L_radio. While the Balmer Decrement (BD) is the standard tool for attenuating Halpha, it can fail to account for optically thick dust regions from which Halpha cannot escape. The ratio of SFRs estimated from radio luminosity and L_Halpha corrected for dust attenuation using the BD can be used as a probe of the degree of optically thick dust affecting Halpha. We explore this combining Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU) Early Science data at 887.5MHz in the Galaxy and Mass Assembly (GAMA) G23 field with the GAMA data. We draw on dust estimates using both the BD and the dust mass, M_d, demonstrating systematic underlying dependencies with L_radio/L_Halpha. By combining the BD and M_d, we quantify how they relate to L_radio/L_Halpha and other galaxy properties. We use this approach to establish an Halpha SFR (SFR_Halpha) estimator that accounts for optically thick dust, demonstrating its consistency with conventional SFR indicators. Rather than proposing a replacement for standard correction methods, we aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of a dust geometry model that integrates both BD and M_d parameters. This H_dust parameter provides a more physically complete representation of optically thick environments that traditional methods may overlook, and can produce SFR estimates consistent with traditional techniques.
Collisional excitation of HMgC$_3$N by He: rate coefficients and astrophysical implications
Authors
Liman Tian, Ziyu Xiang, Wenli Zou, Xiaolin Chen
Comments
8 pages,9 figures
Primary

Reliable collisional data are essential for interpreting the rotational emission of HMgC$_3$N under the non-equilibrium conditions of molecular clouds. We investigate the rotational excitation and de-excitation of HMgC$_3$N induced by collisions with He, combining \textit{ab initio} calculations with quantum scattering theory. A two-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) for HMgC$_3$N--He was constructed using the CCSD(T)-F12A method in conjunction with the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set. Quantum close-coupling (CC) calculation were then performed to obtain state-to-state integral cross sections and temperature-dependent rate coefficients for rotational transitions with $Δj=\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm3$, covering rotational levels up to $j=20$ and temperatures from $3$ to $50$~K. The rate coefficients exhibit a clear propensity for $Δj=\pm2$ transitions. The resulting collisional data were incorporated into radiative transfer modelling to constrain the physical conditions and column density of HMgC$_3$N from its observed spectral line emission. A $χ^2$ minimization yields a best-fitting H$_2$ volume density of $n(\mathrm{H}_2)=1.233\times10^5~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and column density of $N(\mathrm{HMgC}_3\mathrm{N})=1.831\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. The derived column density is in approximate agreement with the observational estimate of $(3.0\pm0.6)\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. These results provide new collisional data for non-LTE studies of HMgC$_3$N and support a more realistic treatment of its rotational excitation and radiative transfer in molecular clouds.

Binary interactions play a crucial role in the evolution and eventual outcomes of massive stars. Rich young open clusters provide large coeval populations to constrain the effects of binary evolution. The Double Cluster, with a turn-off mass well above the threshold for core-collapse supernovae, is an excellent test bed for these processes. We used h and chi Persei, together with NGC 663, to constrain the evolutionary pathways of massive stars, particularly the role of binary interaction. We present spectroscopy of around 80 stars to obtain spectral types and derive stellar parameters. We combined this dataset with Gaia astrometry to evaluate their evolutionary status and possible evolutionary paths. We derived atmospheric parameters for almost 60 stars in the upper main sequence (B2V and earlier) and found broad agreement between two independent fully automated procedures. We identified the cluster turn-offs between B1 V and B1.5-2IV and differentiated this feature from the vicinity of theTAMS, where we found a population of B1-B1.5 giants, with a high fraction of Be stars. The bulk of the population in all of the massive clusters studied in the Perseus arm is consistent with single-star evolutionary models at a given age, but some B-type supergiants are much more luminous than any other cluster star. Their typical cluster kinematics and stellar parameters suggest a merger origin. Other lower-luminosity B-type supergiants seem consistent with the rest of the population. Kinematic analysis has indicated that most Be stars have not been dynamically perturbed by a supernova explosion. Those below the turn-off are likely to have been formed via case A mass transfer. Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to ~100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.

Using JWST/NIRISS slitless spectroscopy from the PASSAGE program, we analyze 17 spectroscopically confirmed emission-line galaxies in candidate interacting systems at $z \sim 1.7$--$3.2$. These galaxies span stellar masses of $7.3 \lesssim \log(M_*/M_\odot) \lesssim 10.4$ and global gas-phase metallicities of $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H}) \sim 7.6$--$8.7$. From spatially resolved emission-line maps, we measure radial metallicity gradients separately for each galaxy, finding values from approximately $-0.2$ to $+0.45$ dex kpc$^{-1}$. The mean metallicity gradients in bins of stellar mass and redshift are consistent with previous measurements, and their scatter is not significantly different from that of the heterogeneous literature compilation in either the metallicity-gradient--stellar-mass or metallicity-gradient--redshift relation. We find tentative evidence for an anti-correlation between the metallicity gradient and the metallicity offset from the mass--metallicity relation, with a best-fit slope of $-0.30 \pm 0.06$, possibly reflecting interaction-driven variations in star formation and chemical enrichment. Galaxies in pairs with comparable component masses tend to exhibit positive metallicity gradients, whereas galaxies in unequal-mass pairs tend to exhibit negative gradients; neither trend is statistically significant in the present sample. Overall, our results reveal substantial diversity in the metallicity distributions of candidate interacting systems at cosmic noon and provide tentative evidence that galaxy interactions may influence the redistribution of gas and metals.
An Unusually Clumpy Ultra-Diffuse Galaxy in the Coma Cluster: A Missing Link for Globular Cluster Formation?
Authors

We present a study of Coma-B122, an unusually clumpy star-forming ultra-diffuse galaxy (UDG) in the Coma cluster outskirts, using deep Keck Cosmic Web Imager integral-field spectroscopy and Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam imaging. B122 hosts $\sim10$ luminous star-forming clumps contributing $\sim25$% of its luminosity and $\sim7$% of its stellar mass, with typical clump masses of $\sim10^6\ M_\odot$ comparable to the most massive super star clusters known in nearby starbursting dwarfs. The clumps form a distinctive ring configuration $\sim2$ kpc from the center. Full-spectrum fitting shows that $\sim12.5$% of the diffuse body's stellar mass formed during an extended episode of enhanced star formation over the past $\sim300$ Myr, within which all the clumps assembled. B122 shows evidence of weak ram-pressure stripping (RPS), including a diffuse light distribution truncated on the side facing the Coma center and extended to the opposite side, along with an asymmetric H I velocity profile. Its ordered stellar and ionized-gas rotation, cold gas fraction, and phase-space position are consistent with an early stage of infall. Spatial variations in clump star formation histories and gas-phase abundances further indicate that RPS-driven metal-poor gas inflow may have triggered and sustained the recent star formation. B122 may offer a rare present-day view of how UDGs assemble their globular cluster (GC) systems during cluster infall, although it remains uncertain if the clumps could survive as GCs. Future observations with higher spatial resolution and of more analogs are important to provide a complete picture of the formation and evolution of clumpy low-surface-brightness dwarf galaxies.

[Abridged]Extreme Emission Line Galaxies (EELGs) are compact, low-mass systems undergoing intense bursts of star formation, whose physical conditions resemble those of galaxies in the early Universe. We characterise the local environment of EELGs identified in the J-PAS survey to determine whether their extreme properties are associated with the presence of nearby companions, as expected if galaxy-galaxy interactions were the main trigger of the burst. We analyse 917 EELGs from the J-PAS internal data release at $0.03 \lesssim z \lesssim 0.9$ over $\sim$30 deg$^2$. We use the Pair Probability Function formalism, based on the full photometric redshift probability distributions, to identify companions within projected separations up to 200 kpc and velocity differences up to 500 km s$^{-1}$. Stellar masses, star formation rates, dust attenuation, and metallicity estimates were derived for the EELGs and their companions through SED fitting with CIGALE, and Monte Carlo simulations were used to correct for projection and completeness effects. The average pair fraction of the EELGs is $f_\mathrm{pair} = 0.162^{+0.048}_{-0.033}$, indicating that only $\sim$16% of the sample has detectable companions, a value lower than the observed average for other star-forming galaxies within the adopted limits. When detected within the sensitivity and stellar-mass completeness limits of J-PAS, companions tend to be more massive and to show higher SED-based metallicity estimates than the EELGs, suggesting that the latter are typically the low-mass components of asymmetric pairs. However, lower-mass companions below these limits may remain undetected. No statistically significant correlation is found between the equivalent widths of the [OIII]$λ$5007, H$β$, and H$α$ emission lines and the projected separation to the detected companions within the J-PAS limits.

Xuelun Mei, Dandan Xu, Cheng Li, Yuqian Gui, Haoyi Wang

The Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR) extends the mass-metallicity relation (MZR) by including star formation rate (SFR) as a third parameter. At fixed stellar mass, galaxies with higher SFRs tend to have lower gas-phase metallicities. We investigate the physical origin of the FMR in the context of episodic star formation (Gui et al. 2026, paper I of this series) by tracing the evolution histories of 8,979 central star-forming galaxies selected at $z=0$ in TNG100 back to $z=1$. We find that the overall MZR scatter at $z=0$ ($\sim0.11$ dex) comprises both long-lived differences among galaxies ($\sim0.09$ dex) and temporal fluctuations within individual galaxies ($\sim 0.07$ dex). The galaxy-to-galaxy component shows an anticorrelation between SFR and gas-phase metallicity, as shown by the observed FMR. As for the consequence from temporal fluctuation, we track individual histories and stack the upward and downward SFR phases of a refined sample. Time-shift analysis and stacked histories show a well-behaved sequence in which the cold non-star-forming gas reservoir increases (decreases) first, followed by a decrease (increase) in gas-phase metallicity and then a rise (decline) in SFR. This ordering is consistent with a physical scenario where metal-poor gas first replenishes the cold reservoir and dilutes the gas before SFR rises to the peak, followed by feedback-driven gas heating/removal, resulting in a shift in metallicity from dilution to enrichment as SFR decreases towards the valley. This naturally explains the anticorrelation between the SFR and gas-phase metallicities as indicated by the observed FMR.

Recent high-sensitivity and high-resolution surveys of the Third Galactic Quadrant [$219.75 \leq l \leq 229.75$ and $-5.25 \leq b \leq 5.25$], such as the Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting ( MWISP) survey, have revealed a significant population of molecular clouds (MCs) that appear virially unbound ($α_{vir} > 2$) according to traditional isolated MC models. However, these apparently unstable cloud structures are actually found long-lived and actively undertaking star formation. This inconsistency presents a Virial Paradox. In the present work, we employ a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) to address this discrepancy by re-evaluating the stability of 2,091 MCs . Unlike traditional machine learning, our PINN model embeds the complete Eulerian Virial Theorem including external surface pressure support ($P_{ext}$) directly into the network's loss function. This model autonomously discovers a global Galactic pressure field that scales inversely with vertical height ($|z|$) and Galactocentric radius ($R_{GC}$), consistent with the properties of the Galactic Flare. By incorporating this AI-derived pressure support, we demonstrate a dramatic shift in the stability census: 99.9\% of previously thought unbound clouds are found to be in a state of pressure confined equilibrium ($α_{AI} \leq 2$). Furthermore, we find a robust log-log correlation ($R = 0.64$) between our corrected stability parameter and the star formation rate (SFR). This correlation provides independent physical validation that environmental confinement is a primary regulator of star forming efficiency in the outer Galaxy.

Context. The L1157 outflow is a prototype of chemically active outflows where to look for molecular species linked to complex chemistry. Aims. With the aim of imaging the large (parsec) scale structure of key prebiotic molecules, we have mapped the whole blue-lobe of the L1157 outflow with the Large Millimeter Telescope (LMT), proving the potential of this facility to detect weak and extended molecular emission. Methods. Isocyanic acid, HNCO, an important precursor of complex chemistry, was chosen to be observed in its (4_0,4-3_0-3) transition at 87.925 GHz with the SEQUOIA focal-plane array, covering an area of 3x3 arcmin2 centered at the position of the main shock L1157-B1, which included a large portion of the vicinity of the protostellar region. Results. The HNCO emission was detected not only from the outflow but it also was found tracing a structure of about 0.3 pc in length, corresponding to the filament from which the protostellar system was born. Conclusions. The HNCO molecule was found to be a tracer of the densest part of filaments in molecular clouds, and given its importance in the formation of more complex molecules, its presence in the cold, quiescent filamentary gas indicates that the building blocks of complex chemistry are already well established in stages prior to protostellar warming or shocks. By allowing the detection of structures related with the filament, these observations proved the LMT potentialities to map weak and extended components in molecular clouds.
