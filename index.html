No Supersoft X-rays Before SN 2026ahvu: Constraints on a Potentially Prompt Type Ia Progenitor
Authors
Akos Bogdan, Tonima Ananna
Comments
7 pages, 2 figures
Primary
astro-ph.HE
Categories
astro-ph.HE astro-ph.SR
The nearby Type Ia supernova SN 2026ahvu offers a rare opportunity to probe its progenitor that may have evolved unusually rapidly. If the progenitor formed in either of the two young stellar clusters near the explosion site in NGC 1433, the delay from star formation to explosion could have been $\sim$100 Myr or less. The distance to NGC 1433 remains uncertain, with published estimates of 9.1 Mpc and 18.9 Mpc. Our preliminary fit to the rising ATLAS light curve yields a distance of $17.7^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Mpc. In the classical single-degenerate scenario, a white dwarf steadily burning hydrogen accreted from a non-degenerate companion can emit detectable supersoft X-rays before explosion. We search for this emission using two archival Chandra observations obtained before the explosion. The observations provide a combined exposure of 95.3 ks, with no 0.3-7 keV photons detected within 2" of the supernova position. For a 100 eV blackbody with Galactic foreground absorption, the 95\% bolometric luminosity limits are $6.3\times10^{37}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ and $2.7\times10^{38}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ at 9.1 Mpc and 18.9 Mpc, respectively. At both distances, the observations exclude a hot, compact white dwarf undergoing steady hydrogen burning if the absorption is limited to the Galactic foreground. However, cooler, expanded, or more heavily absorbed photospheres remain viable. Together with the pre-explosion non-detections for SN 2011fe and SN 2014J, our results reinforce the absence of luminous supersoft progenitors in nearby SNe Ia. This pattern is naturally consistent with double-degenerate explosions, although X-ray-faint single-degenerate configurations remain viable. Post-explosion X-ray observations can test for circumstellar interaction and help determine whether material around the progenitor could have obscured a luminous nuclear burning white dwarf.

To investigate the processes governing clustered star formation, we present a multi-wavelength study of the star-forming complex hosting clusters Waterloo 01 and [BDS2003]64 in Sh 2-208. Both clusters exhibit elongated morphologies, with radii of $\sim1.5$ and $\sim0.9$ pc, respectively, and are located at a common distance of $\sim3.5$ kpc. Wat01 displays hierarchical structure ($Q=0.6$) and signature of mass segregation ($Γ_{\rm MSR}=1.5\pm0.6$). Its relaxation time, comparable to or longer than its age, suggests that the observed segregation may have a primordial contribution. The mass function follows a single power law, $Γ=-1.62\pm0.15$, over $\sim0.7$-$19.6$ M$_\odot$. Molecular-line observations reveal distinct velocity components associated with S208 and Wat01, indicating different molecular environments within the complex. Wat01 is strongly shaped by massive-star feedback, with an evacuated cavity, surrounding shell, and compressed globules consistent with an expanding H\,{\sc ii} region; feedback may have promoted localized star formation, although a direct causal connection cannot be established. In contrast, \textit{Herschel} data uncover a hub-filament system (HFS) in S208, with the central hub associated with BDS64 and the H\,{\sc ii} region powered by 'S1'. The filaments remain relatively well-preserved, indicating that gravity continues to play an important role in assembling dense gas toward the hub and in clustered star formation, even as feedback begins to affect the immediate surroundings. Overall, this complex illustrates the coexistence of feedback-processed and comparatively less-disrupted star-forming environments, highlighting the evolving interplay between stellar feedback and gravity in clustered star formation.

Understanding massive star formation in low-metallicity environments is crucial for constraining the universality of star formation across cosmic evolution. In this Letter, we present the first systematic survey of massive protostellar outflows in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC, $Z\sim$0.2--0.5 $Z_\odot$), as part of the Magellanic Outflow and Chemistry Survey (MAGOS). MAGOS targets 40 massive young stellar objects (MYSOs) with luminosities of (1--23)$\times 10^4$ $L_\odot$ with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) at 0.85 mm at a linear resolution of $\sim$0.1 pc. Using CO (3--2), we identify blue- and red-shifted outflows associated with the primary continuum sources toward 29 targets in the LMC and 10 targets in the SMC. Comparing the outflow mass, momentum, energy, and force versus luminosity scaling relations in MAGOS with those in the Galaxy, we find that the LMC CO outflows have a similar slope in the mass-luminosity relation, suggesting that CO remains an effective tracer of massive protostellar outflows at $Z\sim$0.5 $Z_\odot$. In contrast, despite the large scatter, the SMC CO outflows are deficient at high luminosity and show flatter scaling relations than the LMC and the Galaxy. This is consistent with reduced CO abundances due to enhanced photodissociation at high luminosity and low metallicity ($Z\sim$0.2 $Z_\odot$), although the possibility of intrinsically weak outflow launching cannot be ruled out with the current data.
Lindblad and Corotation Resonances as the Common Origin of Moving Groups, Diagonal Ridges, and Velocity Waves in the Milky Way Disc

The Galactic disc is a complex dynamical system showing various kinematic structures, including moving groups (MGs), diagonal ridges (DRs), and velocity waves. To investigate their possible dynamical connection, we analyzed the phase-space distribution of stars from the extit{Gaia} DR3 catalog. We used angular momentum $L_z$ as a key dynamical tracer and examined stellar densities and velocities as functions of $L_z$. We identified stellar groups at specific $L_z$-values and associated them with extrema in the median radial velocity $\left$, known as a velocity wave. By cross-matching the extrema of the $\left$-wave with structures in the $V_R$--$L_z$ and $R$--$L_z$ planes, we established a clear connection between the velocity wave, the main MGs, and the DRs. A similar connection was partially identified for corrugations in the median vertical velocity $\left$. Both velocity waves are strongest near the Galactic mid-plane and decrease in amplitude with increasing vertical distance. Our results demonstrate that MGs, DRs, and velocity waves in the solar neighborhood are different projections of the same dynamical structures associated with Lindblad and corotation resonances. These resonances arise from coupling between the radial and azimuthal stellar motions and are particularly prominent in the thin Galactic disc. Numerical simulations including perturbations from the Galactic bar and spiral arms confirm these conclusions. Our findings indicate that the local stellar disc is currently evolving in a state of dynamical equilibrium. We also show that in-plane velocity oscillations must be considered when interpreting vertical stellar distributions, as they may produce artifacts in vertical profiles.

We investigate how environment is connected to the formation times and three-dimensional stellar shapes of galaxies using the FIREbox cosmological volume simulation. We analyze 1720 resolved galaxies at $z=0$ spanning stellar masses $M_\star\approx5\times10^6$ to $5\times10^{11}\,M_\odot$ and environments ranging from isolated galaxies to groups in $\sim10^{13}\,M_\odot$ halos. We quantify environment using the Perturbation Index (PI), which measures the cumulative tidal influence of neighboring galaxies relative to each galaxy's own binding force. We measure formation times from cumulative star formation histories and shapes from intrinsic stellar axis ratios. Formation times depend most strongly on stellar mass, but below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, galaxies in more strongly perturbing environments formed their stars earlier, most clearly in the late-time star formation of low-mass galaxies. Galaxies in these environments also have larger minor-to-major axis ratios at all masses: more spherical at low and intermediate mass and thicker along the minor axis at high mass. A partial-correlation analysis shows that, below $10^8\,M_\odot$, the environment-shape correlation is no longer significant at fixed formation time, whereas between $10^8$ and $10^{10}\,M_\odot$ a modest correlation remains. Above $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, the weak environment-shape correlation is not explained by formation time. Below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, high-PI galaxies also have lower halo masses at fixed stellar mass; at fixed halo mass, environment is only weakly correlated with formation time and shape, whereas formation time and shape remain correlated. We therefore predict that, at fixed stellar mass, dwarf galaxies in stronger tidal environments should appear rounder in projection, with the difference largely tracking the ages of their stellar populations.
Orbital decomposition and evolution of LEGA-C-like galaxies in IllustrisTNG from z = 1 to z = 0

Stellar orbits encode the dynamical history of galaxies and provide a physically motivated link between galaxy structures and assembly processes. Although orbit-based decompositions are well established in the Local Universe, their evolution across cosmic time, especially at higher redshifts, remains poorly explored. We investigate the orbital structure of \texttt{LEGA-C-like} galaxies in the \texttt{TNG100} simulation from $z=1$ to $z=0$ to identify how stellar mass, star formation activity, environment, merger history, gas content, and ex situ stellar mass growth shape their orbital transformation. We decomposed the stellar orbital structure of simulated galaxies into cold, warm, hot, and counter-rotating (CR) components using time-averaged orbital circularities. We find a strong mass dependence of orbital structure at $z=0$ as well as at $z=1$. Galaxies below $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)\sim11.2$ have a combined warm and cold orbital fraction of $\sim70\%$, whereas more massive systems have a hot-orbit fraction of $\sim60\%$. These trends are consistent with assembly pathways driven predominantly by wet and dry major mergers, respectively. Tracking the same galaxies to $z=0$ reveals a systematic, but mass-dependent, evolution toward lower rotational support: At both low and high stellar masses, the hot-orbit fraction increases, whereas the relative change is greater in low-mass galaxies. The survival of cold structure depends strongly on the star formation state at $z=1$; star-forming progenitors retain substantially larger cold fractions than the quiescent progenitors. At both redshifts, the orbital fractions show a rise (decline) in hot and CR (cold and warm) orbits with the ex situ stellar mass fraction at a fixed stellar mass. These results provide the first evolutionary framework for interpreting orbit-based decompositions of galaxies at the \texttt{LEGA-C} epoch.

We present new radio-continuum observations of the well-known Wolf-Rayet (WR) nebula PCG 11 (Cohen's Ring; named after and in honour of the co-author on the discovery paper, Martin Cohen) using observations from the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP) Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU), Australia Telescope Compact Array (ATCA) and MeerKAT surveys. We find that the elliptical shell surrounding Cohen's Ring has a flux density of 62$\pm$2\,mJy at 1000 MHz. The angular size of the elliptical emission is measured to be 54\arcsec$\times$45\arcsec\ (at P.A.=69.4\D), which agrees well with observations of the source in H$α$ and at 24\,$μ$m in the infrared. Using the measured angular size and a distance of 3.94$\pm$0.27\,kpc, we determine that the nebula has a linear size of $1.03(\pm0.07)\times0.86(\pm0.06)$\,pc. Together with our new and previously measured flux density estimates, we determine a spectral index for Cohen's Ring to be $α$=0.06$\pm$0.02, indicating that the emission from the WR nebula is of thermal origin. Based on a typical average expansion velocity of 75~km~s$^{-1}$ for a WR star with a Red Supergiant (RSG) progenitor, we estimate the age of Cohen's Ring to be $\sim$ 13500 years. Our NANTEN telescope CO observations suggest that Cohen's Ring was formed during a relatively recent mass-loss event, while the associated CO-emitting molecular gas reflects the cumulative effects of stellar-wind feedback over a much longer timescale ($\sim$1.5\,Myr). As one of the few WR ring nebulae with linked molecular gas, Cohen's Ring provides a valuable laboratory for studying the long-term impact of massive-star feedback on surrounding molecular clouds.

The extraplanar diffuse ionised gas (eDIG) is a key tracer of the feedback processes that connect galactic discs and halos. We present the first study of spatially resolved gas-phase chemical abundances in the eDIG of edge-on galaxies, based on integral field spectroscopy obtained with MUSE. Using a sample of four galaxies from the BETIS sample, we derived maps of the oxygen abundance, 12+log(O/H), the nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio, log(N/O), and the ionisation parameter, log(U), with the HII-CHI-Mistry v5.5 code, and we tested the reliability of this method when different ionisation sources are present. We separated the eDIG into star-formation-dominated and shock-contaminated regimes according to the relative contribution of photoionisation and shocks, and investigated their impact on the derived chemical abundances. We find that HII-CHI-Mistry returns systematically lower O/H and N/O values in shock-contaminated regions, especially for N/O, with typical offsets of ~0.1-0.15 dex relative to star-formation-dominated regions, together with a more homogeneous and lower ionisation state (log(U) ~ -3.1). In contrast, star-formation-dominated regions span a wider range of ionisation parameters (log(U) ~ -3.3 to -2.8) while maintaining approximately solar oxygen abundances. Despite these differences, the abundance offsets are comparable to the sampling step of the HII-CHI-Mistry model grid. The inferred oxygen abundance shows little variation between discs and halos. This is compatible with a scenario in which star formation feedback and galactic fountains efficiently recycle oxygen-rich gas between both components, in agreement with hydrodynamical simulations. In contrast, the N/O ratio shows significant radial and vertical variations, particularly in environments with weaker star formation feedback.
From Non-parametric Statistics to Images: Generating Galaxy Morphologies

Non-parametric statistics characterize complex galaxy morphologies but do not specify how to construct images that reproduce those statistics. We present Flow-VAE, which combines a variational autoencoder with a conditional normalizing flow to generate images conditioned on concentration, asymmetry, smoothness (CAS), Gini coefficient (G), and M20. A Gaussian mixture model completes unspecified descriptors and measures the training-distribution support of requested combinations. Using a dataset of 240,000 DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys r-band images, we evaluate fidelity by remeasuring morphological statistics from model outputs and comparing them with the inputs. With a tolerance of +/-10% of each descriptor's adopted range, descriptor-specific recovery rates are 96.62-99.97% for reconstruction and 87.81-99.83% for single-parameter generation among valid measurements. Generated descriptors show regression toward typical training values, while less-supported joint conditions produce broader residuals and systematic offsets. These results quantify the extent to which Flow-VAE can generate galaxy images consistent with prescribed non-parametric morphological statistics.

We present the discovery and characterisation of G195.65$-$0.10 (also known as the $λ$-complex), a giant hub-filament system identified by the Outer Galaxy High Resolution Survey (OGHReS). The complex contains 61 spatially and kinematically coherent HiGAL clumps with a mean velocity of $V_{\rm LSR}=32.85$ km/s and a velocity dispersion between clumps of 1.97 km/s. A $Gaia$ parallax measurement of the central emission-line star MWC 137 associated with an embedded cluster gives a heliocentric distance of 5.2$^{+0.8}_{-0.6}$ kpc, placing the complex at a Galactocentric radius of 13.2 kpc. The molecular structure spans approximately $35\times63$ pc and contains $\sim 1.6\times10^{5}$ M$_\odot$ of molecular gas, including $\sim 5 \times 10^{4}$ M$_\odot$ in dense clumps. The molecular emission is organised into three prominent velocity-coherent filaments that converge on a dense central hub. Of the associated clumps, 16 are protostellar and 45 are gravitationally bound but currently starless. The protostellar clumps are significantly more concentrated towards the hub than the starless population, with median projected offsets of 6.65 and 15.83 pc, respectively. Moreover, the luminosity-to-mass ratio of the protostellar clumps decreases significantly with increasing distance from the hub, indicating that the most evolutionarily advanced sources are preferentially located in the central region. These results reveal a systematic concentration of dense gas and increasingly evolved star formation towards the hub, with organised large-scale gas motions throughout the filamentary network. The $λ$-complex therefore provides an unprecedented laboratory to understand hierarchical and high-mass star formation in the outer Galactic disc.
Kinetic Monte Carlo modeling of ammonium formate NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$ and ammonium cyanate NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ in interstellar ices

JWST observations have revealed evidence for NH$_4^+$ in the ices of the Chamaeleon I dark molecular cloud, with HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ likely counter ions. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated that ammonium formate and ammonium cyanate can form through acid-base reactions involving NH$_3$ at cryogenic temperatures. The formation of these two ammonium salts are simulated to investigate their formation in dark-cloud ices and assess their sensitivity to uncertainties in the chemical reaction network, including the recently considered HOCO-mediated pathway for CO$_2$ formation, as well as variations in the cosmic-ray ionization rate, cloud collapse timescale and density, and elemental depletion. Our modeling framework consists of microscopic kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations of grain-surface chemistry augmented by UCLCHEM gas-phase and physical cloud evolution simulations. The microscopic KMC method provides for explicit simulation of the location, movement and reaction of ice species, making it particularly well-suited to model acid-base chemistry of NH$_4^+$-salt formation, occurring within the ice mantle over extended timescales. The simulations produce the canonical ice-evolution sequence, with an initially H$_2$O-rich ice followed by a layer dominated by CO and CO$_2$. Salt production is dominated by NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$, which is found to form predominantly during the H$_2$O-rich stage as its production is limited by the availability of NH$_3$. NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ is found to form later at the interface between the H$_2$O- and CO-rich ice when the formation of its precursor acid HNCO is enabled. Despite the variations explored, the total NH$_4^+$ abundance, particularly relative to H$_2$O, remains remarkably robust and consistent with the observed abundance. Depending on the variation, a redistribution between counterions HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ is observed.
