Sherlock 'Chrones: The Curious Case of NGC 6791 and Asteroseismic Targets Therein Reveals Mixing Length Dependence on Evolutionary Stage

As single-aged populations containing stars spanning a range of masses but otherwise with similar properties, star clusters provide valuable testbeds for stellar evolution theory. The old, metal-rich, open cluster NGC 6791 is well studied, but the availability of asteroseismic observations of individual cluster members allows for a more in-depth analysis. Driven by mounting evidence that the solar assumption for convection used in almost all 1D stellar models is not adequate, and in light of the increased precision provided by modern data and asteroseismic constraints, we built and compared custom isochrones with varying values of the convective mixing length, $α_\text{MLT}$, to two clusters with well-determined ages: NGC 6791 and the well-studied, metal-poor globular cluster M92. For both clusters, we found that the best-fitting mixing length parameter decreases monotonically as a function of evolutionary phase, finding values between $0.98-1.10α_{\odot}$ for NGC 6791 and $1.16-1.21α_{\odot}$ for M92. In NGC 6791, this corresponds to an 18% decrease in the best fitting mixing length between the main sequence turn-off and upper end of the red giant branch. The degree of variation found within this one object is therefore comparable to variation across the literature in $α_\text{MLT}$ fits for other non-solar targets, calling single-valued $α_\text{MLT}$ fits to clusters into question. It is also consistent with $\log g$-$α_\text{MLT}$ trends predicted by higher-fidelity, 3D convection analyses. Our results therefore illustrate the need for more flexibility in our treatment of the mixing length in isochrones to properly model clusters.
No Supersoft X-rays Before SN 2026ahvu: Constraints on a Potentially Prompt Type Ia Progenitor
Authors
Akos Bogdan, Tonima Ananna
Comments
7 pages, 2 figures

astro-ph.HE
Categories
astro-ph.HE astro-ph.SR
The nearby Type Ia supernova SN 2026ahvu offers a rare opportunity to probe its progenitor that may have evolved unusually rapidly. If the progenitor formed in either of the two young stellar clusters near the explosion site in NGC 1433, the delay from star formation to explosion could have been $\sim$100 Myr or less. The distance to NGC 1433 remains uncertain, with published estimates of 9.1 Mpc and 18.9 Mpc. Our preliminary fit to the rising ATLAS light curve yields a distance of $17.7^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Mpc. In the classical single-degenerate scenario, a white dwarf steadily burning hydrogen accreted from a non-degenerate companion can emit detectable supersoft X-rays before explosion. We search for this emission using two archival Chandra observations obtained before the explosion. The observations provide a combined exposure of 95.3 ks, with no 0.3-7 keV photons detected within 2" of the supernova position. For a 100 eV blackbody with Galactic foreground absorption, the 95\% bolometric luminosity limits are $6.3\times10^{37}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ and $2.7\times10^{38}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ at 9.1 Mpc and 18.9 Mpc, respectively. At both distances, the observations exclude a hot, compact white dwarf undergoing steady hydrogen burning if the absorption is limited to the Galactic foreground. However, cooler, expanded, or more heavily absorbed photospheres remain viable. Together with the pre-explosion non-detections for SN 2011fe and SN 2014J, our results reinforce the absence of luminous supersoft progenitors in nearby SNe Ia. This pattern is naturally consistent with double-degenerate explosions, although X-ray-faint single-degenerate configurations remain viable. Post-explosion X-ray observations can test for circumstellar interaction and help determine whether material around the progenitor could have obscured a luminous nuclear burning white dwarf.

To investigate the processes governing clustered star formation, we present a multi-wavelength study of the star-forming complex hosting clusters Waterloo 01 and [BDS2003]64 in Sh 2-208. Both clusters exhibit elongated morphologies, with radii of $\sim1.5$ and $\sim0.9$ pc, respectively, and are located at a common distance of $\sim3.5$ kpc. Wat01 displays hierarchical structure ($Q=0.6$) and signature of mass segregation ($Γ_{\rm MSR}=1.5\pm0.6$). Its relaxation time, comparable to or longer than its age, suggests that the observed segregation may have a primordial contribution. The mass function follows a single power law, $Γ=-1.62\pm0.15$, over $\sim0.7$-$19.6$ M$_\odot$. Molecular-line observations reveal distinct velocity components associated with S208 and Wat01, indicating different molecular environments within the complex. Wat01 is strongly shaped by massive-star feedback, with an evacuated cavity, surrounding shell, and compressed globules consistent with an expanding H\,{\sc ii} region; feedback may have promoted localized star formation, although a direct causal connection cannot be established. In contrast, \textit{Herschel} data uncover a hub-filament system (HFS) in S208, with the central hub associated with BDS64 and the H\,{\sc ii} region powered by 'S1'. The filaments remain relatively well-preserved, indicating that gravity continues to play an important role in assembling dense gas toward the hub and in clustered star formation, even as feedback begins to affect the immediate surroundings. Overall, this complex illustrates the coexistence of feedback-processed and comparatively less-disrupted star-forming environments, highlighting the evolving interplay between stellar feedback and gravity in clustered star formation.

Ultra-luminous X-ray (ULX) sources are bright, off-nuclear X-ray point sources, mostly associated with high-mass X-ray binaries (HMXBs), with a compact object accreting from a donor star at super-Eddington luminosities. Although their HMXB nature implies a connection to star formation processes, previous works have found that luminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs) appear to have a deficit of ULXs compared to their very high star formation rates (SFRs). Here, we analyze three Chandra observations of NGC 3256, a merging LIRG with SFR ~50-60 Msun/yr. Based on local scaling relations, it could be expected to host between 100-120 individual ULXs, which could have a non-negligible impact on the energetics and feedback processes of the galaxy. We aim to characterize all its detected X-ray point sources to determine if there is a ULX deficit in this galaxy. We also aim to explore the potential impact of host-galaxy obscuration and metallicity in said deficit. We characterize the luminosity of each X-ray point source in each Chandra observation, and use all observations to determine their potential ULX nature and the fraction of transient ULXs, as well as construct a ULX luminosity function for this galaxy. We find that the galaxy hosts 10-15 ULXs, making it one of the most ULX-rich galaxies known to date. While comparison with the ULX/SFR ratio of nearby galaxies predicted as many as ~100 ULXs, we show that, when accounting for its very high metallicity, NGC 3256 does not have a ULX deficit; and may even present an excess. While the high obscuration in LIRGs has previously been invoked to explain the ULX deficit, we conclude that it is not required to explain the ULX to SFR ratio in NGC 3256. If obscuration still plays a role, then LIRGs may actually be significantly more efficient at producing ULXs than normal galaxies (of the same metallicity), in disagreement with previous results.

The chemical abundance ratio [Eu/$α$] has been proposed as a discriminator between ex-situ and in-situ stellar populations in the Milky Way and external galaxies. Here we show with a high-quality, homogeneous dataset of integrated-light abundances for 28 globular clusters (GCs), where 21 are extragalactic and 7 galactic, that this behaviour holds in other Local Group galaxies. In particular, we find metal-rich ([Fe/H] > --0.7) M31 GCs have uniformly low [Eu/Si]$\sim-0.4$, indicating they represent an in-situ population. Lower metallicity GCs in M31 generally show high [Eu/Si] $\sim+0.4$, suggesting they originated in accreted progenitors, and these GCs lie at larger projected radii from the M31 centre. Furthermore, we compare the M31 GC abundances with similar determinations for the M31 bulge for Na, Mg, Si, Ti, Ca, Eu, Cr, Mn, Ba, Ni, and Cu. The in-situ M31 GCs and M31 bulge exhibit similar abundance trends, excepting Ba -- and possibly Cr and Ti, suggesting similar star formation processes. [Eu/Si] in the M31 bulge is consistent with in-situ Milky Way stars, but is higher than the M31 in-situ GCs. For M33 and intermediate-mass dwarf galaxies, we identify a mixture of in-situ and ex-situ GCs, while for lower-mass dwarfs, we identify primarily in-situ GCs. These results suggest that accreted GCs in massive galaxies originated, at least in part, from intermediate-mass, intermediate-metallicity progenitors. We support earlier suggestions that [Eu/$α$] is a powerful in-/ex-situ separator for extragalactic GCs, demonstrating the potential of high-resolution integrated-light abundances for extragalactic archaeology.

We study the local regulation of star formation efficiency (SFE) in star-forming regions of nearby galaxies using spatially resolved data from the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) and the ALMA-MaNGA QUEnching and STar formation (ALMaQUEST) surveys. We define the local SFE as $Σ_{\rm SFE} \equiv Σ_{\rm SFR}/Σ_{\rm H_2}$, where $Σ_{\rm SFR}$ is the star formation rate surface density derived from dust-corrected $\rm Hα$ emission, and $Σ_{\rm H_2}$ is the molecular hydrogen surface density derived from CO measurements. Combining these data with dynamical properties from the MaNGA DynPop project, we use random forest regression to identify the dominant factors of $Σ_{\rm SFE}$. Galactic shear, quantified by the Oort parameter $A_{\rm Oort}$, emerges as the dynamical property most strongly associated with lower SFE on average, exceeding the predictive power of mass surface density (either total or stellar), Toomre stability, and mid-plane pressure. However, these quantities provide only modest predictive power and primarily trace the radial variation of SFE. After removing the shear-related radial trends, only weak correlations remain between SFE and other local galactic properties, implying that the remaining azimuthal variations are likely governed by cloud-scale processes below our resolution limit. Furthermore, the relationship between SFE and shear varies among galaxies and weakens in gas-rich regions, suggesting that gas density may modulate the impact of shear. This behavior is consistent with competition between shear-induced cloud stabilization and shear-driven cloud-cloud collisions. Overall, galactic shear appears to regulate the large-scale radial distribution of SFE, while cloud-scale interstellar medium physics may drive the remaining variation.

Context: Mapping gas flows in the interstellar medium is key to understanding the formation of structure in our Milky Way. Methods that simultaneously reconstruct clouds' distances and line-of-sight velocities are called kinetic tomography (KT). Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are powerful tracers for such maps because they trace the integrated dust density and the dust-weighted velocity. Previous DIB-KT works had small samples with coarse kpc-scale resolution and could not capture vertical disk structure. Aims: Make 4D (3D position + radial velocity) maps of the Milky Way from new, high-quality 15,273 Å DIB detections in APOGEE DR17. Methods: We combine variational inference and Gaussian processes with a mixture likelihood for low signal-to-noise detections and marginalization over distance uncertainties. We calibrate the model on mock fields and spectral injection tests and validate the map against independent tracers of the dust density (3D dust maps) and velocity substructure (masers and young stellar clusters). Results: We recover a posterior distribution over 3D maps of the density and velocity structure of the 15,273 Å DIB within 6 kpc of the Sun. We measure the large-scale structure of the DIB disk (scale length, height and flare) and detect vertical distortions consistent with the warp. On smaller scales, the map resolves distance ambiguities in gas longitude-velocity diagrams. From the second-order velocity structure function, we measure a slope (0.64 $\pm$ 0.04) consistent with Kolmogorov turbulence, amplitude consistent with expectations for molecular clouds, and a preliminary ratio of compressive-to-solenoidal modes. Conclusions: With larger DIB catalogs expected from SDSS-V, this framework should resolve kinematic substructure at the scales needed to constrain the formation and dynamics of individual clouds and voids. JWST observations have revealed evidence for NH$_4^+$ in the ices of the Chamaeleon I dark molecular cloud, with HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ likely counter ions. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated that ammonium formate and ammonium cyanate can form through acid-base reactions involving NH$_3$ at cryogenic temperatures. The formation of these two ammonium salts are simulated to investigate their formation in dark-cloud ices and assess their sensitivity to uncertainties in the chemical reaction network, including the recently considered HOCO-mediated pathway for CO$_2$ formation, as well as variations in the cosmic-ray ionization rate, cloud collapse timescale and density, and elemental depletion. Our modeling framework consists of microscopic kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations of grain-surface chemistry augmented by UCLCHEM gas-phase and physical cloud evolution simulations. The microscopic KMC method provides for explicit simulation of the location, movement and reaction of ice species, making it particularly well-suited to model acid-base chemistry of NH$_4^+$-salt formation, occurring within the ice mantle over extended timescales. The simulations produce the canonical ice-evolution sequence, with an initially H$_2$O-rich ice followed by a layer dominated by CO and CO$_2$. Salt production is dominated by NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$, which is found to form predominantly during the H$_2$O-rich stage as its production is limited by the availability of NH$_3$. NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ is found to form later at the interface between the H$_2$O- and CO-rich ice when the formation of its precursor acid HNCO is enabled. Despite the variations explored, the total NH$_4^+$ abundance, particularly relative to H$_2$O, remains remarkably robust and consistent with the observed abundance. Depending on the variation, a redistribution between counterions HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ is observed.

We present the discovery and characterisation of G195.65$-$0.10 (also known as the $λ$-complex), a giant hub-filament system identified by the Outer Galaxy High Resolution Survey (OGHReS). The complex contains 61 spatially and kinematically coherent HiGAL clumps with a mean velocity of $V_{\rm LSR}=32.85$ km/s and a velocity dispersion between clumps of 1.97 km/s. A $Gaia$ parallax measurement of the central emission-line star MWC 137 associated with an embedded cluster gives a heliocentric distance of 5.2$^{+0.8}_{-0.6}$ kpc, placing the complex at a Galactocentric radius of 13.2 kpc. The molecular structure spans approximately $35\times63$ pc and contains $\sim 1.6\times10^{5}$ M$_\odot$ of molecular gas, including $\sim 5 \times 10^{4}$ M$_\odot$ in dense clumps. The molecular emission is organised into three prominent velocity-coherent filaments that converge on a dense central hub. Of the associated clumps, 16 are protostellar and 45 are gravitationally bound but currently starless. The protostellar clumps are significantly more concentrated towards the hub than the starless population, with median projected offsets of 6.65 and 15.83 pc, respectively. Moreover, the luminosity-to-mass ratio of the protostellar clumps decreases significantly with increasing distance from the hub, indicating that the most evolutionarily advanced sources are preferentially located in the central region. These results reveal a systematic concentration of dense gas and increasingly evolved star formation towards the hub, with organised large-scale gas motions throughout the filamentary network. The $λ$-complex therefore provides an unprecedented laboratory to understand hierarchical and high-mass star formation in the outer Galactic disc.

The non-equilibrium state of the Galactic disk has drawn extensive attention since ESA's \textit{Gaia} launch. Complex structure appears across a multi-parameter space of stellar abundances, ages, and kinematics. Using GALAH DR4, we investigate ridge and phase-spiral structures in chemical-abundance, phase ($v_i,x_i$), action--angle ($J_i,θ_i$), and frequency--action ($Ω_i,J_i$) spaces ($i=R,φ,z$). Our sample includes 333,412 disk stars with reliable chemodynamical data. In the $R$--$v_φ$ plane, ridges trace near-constant $J_φ$ tracks and show coherent variations in $θ_R$ and $θ_φ$. In $J_φ$--$J_R$, ridges are most prominent when encoded by $z_{\rm max}$ and preferentially associate with metal-rich, low-$α$ populations. In the solar neighbourhood ($R=7.2$--$9.2$\,kpc), we recover a clear phase spiral in $z$--$v_z$. Encoded in $v_φ$, $Ω_R$, or $Ω_φ$, one-armed spirals appear, whereas the $v_R$-encoded map shows a two-armed spiral. Projecting actions onto $z$--$v_z$ reveals residual spiral signatures in $J_R$ and $J_φ$, indicating that the phase spiral encodes in-plane orbital information. Both structures depend on age: ridges remain at similar locations with varying contrast, while the phase spiral is more tightly wound in younger and more loosely wound in older populations. Several ridges correspond in $J_φ$ to known moving groups, and excluding stars in all six moving-group regions makes the phase spiral less distinct in mean radial velocity maps. These links support interpreting ridges, phase spirals, and moving groups within a common phase-space dynamical framework. Combined with trends in orbital properties, age, and abundance, these findings suggest a common dynamical origin and distinct chemodynamical responses of a perturbed Galactic disk.

Using APOGEE red clump stars and open clusters with precise ages, we investigate the age--metallicity relation (AMR) across the Galactic disk as a function of Galactocentric radius $R$ and vertical height $|Z|$. For stars older than $\sim$6--8 Gyr, the AMR shows little dependence on either $R$ or $|Z|$ within the uncertainties, suggesting rapid and well-mixed chemical evolution in the early disk. In contrast, younger populations exhibit a clear negative radial metallicity gradient, with the inner disk being more metal-rich than the outer disk, consistent with inside-out disk growth since $\sim$6--8 Gyr ago. Most importantly, at fixed location, the AMR for ages $\lesssim6$--8 Gyr is non-monotonic: [Fe/H] decreases toward younger ages, reaches a minimum at intermediate ages, and then rises again. Beyond $R\sim11$\,kpc, the relation becomes even more complex, exhibiting an additional reversal and a distinct metallicity peak at an age of $\sim2$\,Gyr. Such complex behavior cannot be explained by continuous local enrichment alone, but instead points to the combined effects of a multi-phase gas-accretion history and radial migration. Episodes of metal-poor gas inflow can dilute the interstellar medium and reset its chemical evolution, while subsequent enrichment raises the metallicity again; radial migration further reshapes the observed AMR by mixing stellar populations formed at different radii and metallicities. A toy model incorporating inside-out growth, two gas-inflow episodes, and radial migration qualitatively reproduces the observed trends, highlighting the combined roles of episodic gas accretion and radial migration in shaping the chemical evolution of the Galactic disk.
From a mass invariant in a gravo-turbulent medium to the characteristic mass of the star-forming cores

The origin of the observed universality, or, say, the limited variations, of the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and its precursor, the prestellar core mass function (CMF), and thus of the characteristic mass of stars and cores, across a wide range of different galactic environments remains an open, fundamental problem in astrophysics. In this paper, we demonstrate that we can explain this property from gravity and turbulence only, using a mass time-invariant that remains valid for a broad class of turbulent flows throughout the star formation process. This mass invariant is entirely determined by the large-scale normalisation of the field of density fluctuations in the Galactic Interstellar Medium (ISM), induced by turbulence. Using high-resolution numerical simulations, we first verify that the temporal invariance of this quantity is preserved in a self-gravitating turbulent medium. Then, introducing a self-consistent model of the statistical properties of the density field in the self-gravitating multiphase turbulent ISM, we show that the universality of the characteristic mass of star-forming cores is imposed by this invariant together with the scaling of the Larson relations. Our model successfully predicts the characteristic mass of the cores in Milky Way-like star-forming regions, as well as in more extreme environments such as high-mass star-forming regions and massive early-type galaxies. This scenario suggests that the characteristic mass of the CMF, and then probably the characteristic stellar mass, originates from a universal, fundamental property of turbulence and is imprinted both by the very early stages of the star formation process in the gravo-turbulent interstellar medium, when the dynamics of the medium are still dominated by turbulence, and by the statistical properties of small-scale self-gravitating structures.
