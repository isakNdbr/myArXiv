The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. I. Evidence from Low-angular-momentum, High-$α$ Stars Scattered Out of the Protodisk by Merger Events
Authors
Tiancheng Sun, Maosheng Xiang, Shaolan Bi, Jifeng Liu, Meng Zhang, Yaqian Wu
Comments
19 pages, 10 figures, Published in AJ
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA astro-ph.SR
Stars born in the high-$α$ disk can later acquire low angular momentum through dynamical scattering during early merger events. Their age--metallicity distribution thus provides a fossil record of the history of both star formation and dynamical perturbations in the early Milky Way disk. Here we identify a sample of 2,078 such stars from more than two million Gaia DR3 subgiants with robust determinations of ages, kinematics, and abundances. From this sample we reconstruct their intrinsic age--metallicity distribution with a forward modeling approach. Our results confirm that these scattered stars are older and more metal-poor than the bulk of the high-$α$ disk on average. In particular, the joint age--metallicity distribution reveals a robust two-component feature: a broad, dominant component with a peak age of $\sim$11.5~Gyr and ${\rm [Fe/H]}\simeq-0.59$, and an older and more metal-poor component with a peak age of $\sim$12.5~Gyr and ${\rm [Fe/H]}\simeq-0.86$. The presence of this two-component structure suggests that the high-$α$ disk experienced episodic phases of star formation and chemical enrichment. The age distribution of the younger Splash component, compared with that of the high-$α$ disk, provides evidence for a substantial dynamical perturbation at a lookback time of 10.8--11.5~Gyr, possibly associated with an early passage of Gaia--Sausage--Enceladus (GSE). The older Splash component, with a peak age of $\sim$12.5~Gyr, may preserve signatures of an earlier interaction, possibly associated with the proposed Low-energy--Kraken--Heracles (LKH) event.

We perform a multiple velocity component analysis of ammonia (NH$_3$) gas from KEYSTONE, a Green Bank Telescope survey covering 11 giant molecular clouds (GMCs) at distances from 0.7 to 2.7 kpc. We extend existing codes to enable simultaneous fitting of up to three velocity components in NH$_3$ (1,1) with radiative transfer. Using our multiple component fitting technique, we produce the best maps of the centroid velocity and velocity dispersion in these clouds to date. Over the entire survey, multiple velocity components were seen in 13% of pixels where we detect and fit NH$_3$ emission, primarily in clusters at the heart of the GMCs. In these multiple component pixels, the median measured NH$_3$ linewidth decreased by a factor of 1.4 compared to equivalent single-component measurements, but there are also significant cloud-to-cloud variations. Regions with multiple velocity components tend to be found in the densest zones of the GMCs and at the sites of high-mass star formation, indicative of multiple filaments, cores, or complex velocities associated with the formation of massive stars. Disentangling these velocity features will allow future quantitative analysis of coherent motions, such as infall and outflow, in these complex environments. The multi-component data products are available on the CADC.

Whether the high-$α$ disk experienced a continuous or episodic star-formation history remains an open question. Here we investigate the age--metallicity distribution of the high-$α$ disk using approximately 145,000 Gaia DR3 subgiants with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]>-1$, 6.5~kpc $\lesssim R\lesssim9.5$~kpc, and $|Z|\lesssim1.5$~kpc. We adopt a forward-modelling framework that accounts for individual age and metallicity uncertainties and corrects for sample selection effects. The reconstructed distribution reveals two components: a broad, dominant population peaking at $\sim10.6$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.38$, and an older, more metal-poor population peaking at $\sim12.4$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.79$. These features suggest an episodic history of star formation and chemical enrichment in the high-$α$ disk, qualitatively consistent with cosmological simulations in which recurrent gas accretion drives variations in the star formation rate. The age of the older component is close to the $\sim12.3$~Gyr epoch inferred for the proposed Low-energy--Kraken--Heracles accretion event, suggesting a possible connection between this early interaction and enhanced disk star formation. There is tentative evidence that the dominant population is possibly composed of two sub-components with age distributions peaking at $\sim11.1$ Gyr and $\sim10.0$ Gyr, respectively. Together, these results favour a picture in which the high-$α$ disk was built through recurrent episodes of enhanced star formation rather than a single temporally smooth phase, even for the regime of ${\rm [Fe/H]}>-1$.

We present five technical validations of SCUBA-2/POL-2 polarimetry on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope at 450 and 850 $μ$m, using 174 archival fields in 69 molecular cloud regions and subsets appropriate to each test. Extended-source calibration avoids a deterministic shift of $Δβ=-1.05$ in the dust emissivity index $β$ that would result from calibrating extended emission using peak brightness. We modeled Stokes $I$ noise with additive and signal-dependent components. The measured median ratio of surface-brightness noise at 450/850 $μ$m is 25. Independent tests of Stokes $Q,U$ noise support approximately equal additive variances and weak error correlations, providing a basis for propagating polarization uncertainties. Corrections determined for individual fields reduce additive zero-level offsets of order $1$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$ in the 450 $μ$m Stokes $Q$ and $U$ intensities. The three-step regression leaves a median combined residual amplitude of $0.07$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$. Independent comparisons with SOFIA/HAWC+ at 154 and 214 $μ$m support the correction in strongly affected fields. We matched the 450 $μ$m maps to the modeled 850 $μ$m beam and the common $12.6''$-$300''$ range of angular scales. We compared three reductions of the same 450 $μ$m observations across 30 sources. Taking the median angular residual relative to the final product for each source, we find that beam matching reduces the median over sources from $6.3^\circ$ to $4.7^\circ$. After matching, 29 of the 30 sources have median residuals below $10^\circ$. We also quantified how residual $Q/U$ offsets propagate in ratios of polarization fractions, differences in spectral indices, and polarization angles. These validations provide procedures and quantitative references for interpreting POL-2 measurements and combining them with polarimetry at other wavelengths.

Recent molecular line observations with high spatial resolutions have revealed vertical chemical stratification in Class I/II disks (i.e. chemical composition changing with height in the vertical direction of the disk), as well as a radial variation of ionisation rates. However, such chemical layered structures in Class 0 disk and envelope systems have been investigated only for limited objects, such as L1527. In the context of the ALMA Large Program FAUST, we report the results of molecular line observations toward the disk and envelope around the low-mass Class 0 protostar NGC 1333 IRAS 4C in the Perseus Molecular Cloud. Previous observations have shown that the disk rotation axis of this object is largely tilted to the line of sight, making it suitable for studying the molecular abundance distribution as a function of disk height. We found that the molecules known as gas density tracers such as C18O and H2CO are distributed around the disk midplane. In addition, molecules known as tracers of photochemical reactions such as CCH and c-C3H2 are distributed in the low-density upper layers of the disk, unlike the distribution in L1527. Moreover, the column density ratio of HCO+/CO derived from the H13CO+ and C18O line emission suggests that the ionisation degree and the ionisation rate vary with the disk radius and they are larger in the outer region of the disk and envelope.

JWST is revealing abundant populations of high-$z$ galaxies with unforeseen properties: UV-luminous galaxies with high nitrogen enrichment, populations of very massive stars (VMSs; $>100\ M_\odot$), and early forms of massive black hole growth such as the Little Red Dots (LRDs). Here we present all these phenomena appearing simultaneously in COLA1, a luminous ($M_{\rm UV}=-21.3$) galaxy at $z=6.6$. New NIRSpec medium-resolution data reveal a blue UV continuum ($β_{\rm UV}=-2.5$) with strong emission lines including NIV] $\lambda1486$, stellar P Cygni profiles, and broad HeII $\lambda1640$. We fit the UV with stellar population models, and find that the models including VMSs (limited to $300\ M_\odot$) are statistically preferred. The best-fit stellar population has $M_*\approx10^8\ M_\odot$, and a young age of $2.4\pm0.8$ Myr. The rest-optical shows a redder continuum ($β_{\rm opt}=-1.1$) and broad Balmer lines with Balmer absorption akin to LRD spectra. By evolving the best-fit stellar population we find that the UV could dim by 1-2 mag in $\sim5$ Myr, and hence this system would be observed as a typical V-shaped LRD. We measure a supersolar $\rm log_{10}(N/O)=-0.07\pm0.05$ and subsolar $\rm log_{10}(C/O)=-0.64\pm0.11$; therefore $\rm log_{10}(N/C)=+0.58\pm0.12$. The independent density-sensitive NIV] $λ\lambda1483,1486$ and CIII] $λ\lambda1906/1909$ ratios require two zones with $n_e\gtrsim10^{6.5}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $n_e\lesssim10^{3.5}$ cm$^{-3}$, respectively. We estimate $\rm log_{10}(N/C)>1.3$ (2$σ$) for the dense zone, requiring extreme N/C enrichment mechanisms such as highly rotating $>100\ M_\odot$ VMSs, tidal disruption events, or supermassive stars. We speculate that runaway collisions of massive stars in a dense stellar cluster may have lead to the seeding of an intermediate-mass black hole resulting in the formation of an LRD.

Granulation and mesogranulation represent different spatial and temporal scales of convection, but are commonly assumed to follow the same stellar atmospheric scaling relations in red giants. We test this assumption using the largest sample assembled for this purpose to date -- 9,328 Kepler red giants spanning the red giant branch and red clump phase, cross-matched with APOGEE stellar parameters. We model each star's power spectrum background to obtain the radius-corrected amplitude and characteristic frequency of both components, and derive empirical scaling relations with effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity through Bayesian model comparison. Gaussian Process and symbolic regression provide independent validation. We find that granulation and mesogranulation are not self-similar: their surface-gravity dependencies are statistically indistinguishable, consistent with a shared envelope geometry, but their temperature dependencies diverge: mesogranulation's amplitude rises steeply with Teff, whereas granulation's shows no statistically justified dependence, and both frequencies respond weakly and similarly. We propose that this pattern is consistent with granulation forming in a thin near-surface layer, where radiation takes over from convection and the Mach number depends only weakly on Teff. Mesogranulation originates deeper, where convection carries the full flux and its Mach number and cell size increase with Teff. Metallicity raises both amplitudes but has little effect on the frequencies. Our results indicate that the self-similarity assumption underlying two-component background models does not hold, with direct implications for background modeling and its downstream application in Galactic archaeology.

The connection between galaxy structure, star formation activity, and large-scale environments provides important insights into the evolution of massive galaxies. However, the relative importance of different galaxy properties in predicting quenching and the environmental dependence of structurally distinct galaxy populations remain poorly constrained at cosmic noon ($1\leq z<3$). In this work, we investigate massive galaxies with $\log M_*/{\rm M}_{\odot}>10.5$ at $1\leq z<3$ using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from the COSMOS-Web survey, combining measurements of central stellar mass surface density ($Σ_1$), star formation activity, and galaxy clustering. We find that $Σ_1$ is the most informative predictor of quenching among the considered properties, including stellar mass, effective radius, formation redshift, and local overdensity. The quenched fraction shows a strong dependence on $Σ_1$, increasing rapidly above a characteristic $Σ_1$. We further show that compact quenched galaxies exhibit a tendency toward stronger clustering than compact and extended star-forming galaxies, while compact and extended star-forming galaxies show similar clustering properties. These results highlight the importance of $Σ_1$ in tracing quenching and reveal that structural diversity among star-forming galaxies is not strongly reflected in their large-scale environments, providing new constraints on the interplay between galaxy structure, quenching, and halo environments during cosmic noon.

We present a near-infrared spectroscopic follow-up study of seven H$α$ emitter candidates at $z\sim2$ in the ZFOURGE-COSMOS field, selected via the $K_{\rm s}$-band flux excess method. Spectra obtained with Subaru/SWIMS confirm strong H$α$ emission line at $z=2.06-2.49$ for all the targets. Three galaxies are low-mass systems ($\log(M_\star/M_\odot) < 9$) with high specific SFRs, while four are high-mass systems ($\log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 10$) on the star-forming main sequence. The low-mass galaxies show consistent H$α$ flux measurements between the $K_{\rm s}$-band flux excess method and the spectroscopy, whereas the high-mass galaxies show higher H$α$ flux inferred from the $K_{\rm s}$-band flux excess than the spectroscopic measurement which may be attributed to their extended star formation distributions. The low-mass galaxies exhibit extreme [O III] $\lambda5007$ emission with high rest-frame equivalent widths ($\mathrm{EW}_{\mathrm{rest}}$) ranging from 480 to 1064 $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$. They are characterized by high [O III]/H$β$ ratios ($>4$) and weak [O II] and [N II] emission lines, indicating high ionization parameters and low metallicity. The line ratios are comparable to those of star-forming galaxies at $z \gtrsim 6$ and their local analogs. JWST/NIRCam imaging reveals a clear mass-dependent color gradient: the low-mass galaxies present positive F115W-F277W radial gradients with blue cores, while the high-mass galaxies exhibit negative gradients with red cores. These results suggest distinct assembly pathways for low- and high-mass galaxies during the cosmic noon epoch.

The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.

The dust extinction curve is an important probe of the properties of dust grains, particularly their size distribution. The properties of dust grains are expected to change significantly in dense molecular clouds due to shielding from interstellar radiation and increased frequency of grain-grain and grain-gas collisions. We measure the dust extinction curve shape parameter R(V) in 9 nearby, dense molecular clouds using data from the APOGEE spectroscopic survey, in combination with ten-band photometry from PS1, 2MASS, and WISE. We analyze the results to determine how the extinction curve varies in nearby molecular clouds. We find that most molecular clouds show a positive correlation between R(V) and E(B-V), but the strength of the correlation varies significantly between clouds. In addition, we find that the clouds with the strongest correlation tend to have very high dispersion in R(V) at fixed E(B-V), which tightens dramatically when clouds are subdivided into separate regions. This suggests that local environmental conditions beyond density shape the R(V)-E(B-V) relation.

The Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf spheroidal galaxy hosts several known globular clusters and numerous additional cluster candidates identified toward its crowded central regions. Establishing the nature of these candidates is difficult because apparent spatial and astrometric structure can arise from the underlying Sgr population. We investigate sixteen Minni stellar-cluster candidates with KMOS spectroscopy to determine whether the observed stars are compatible with Sgr and whether any candidate field shows evidence for a distinct stellar system. We analyse H-band KMOS spectra of 341 targets from the VVVX-GalCen Spectroscopic Survey, deriving radial velocities (RVs) and empirical metallicities. We compare these with an independent Sgr spectroscopic sample and use VVVX photometry and Gaia DR3 astrometry to test for localized stellar excesses relative to local control fields. Of the 341 KMOS targets, 256 (75%) have RVs within the adopted Sgr interval, and their metallicities broadly overlap the Sgr comparison population. No field shows statistically compelling kinematic evidence for a distinct system after accounting for the multiple candidates examined. Minni 329 shows the strongest spatial excess, but this is not significant after multiple-testing correction and is not accompanied by significant central concentration. The KMOS targets predominantly trace the Sgr population, but Sgr compatibility alone does not confirm the individual candidates as distinct stellar clusters. None of the sixteen fields can be confirmed as a separate stellar system with the present data, although this does not establish that the original candidates are spurious. Deeper imaging and larger spectroscopic samples are required to determine their physical nature.

Galaxies with extreme amounts of neutral atomic hydrogen (HI), known as HI-eXtreme (HIX) galaxies, paradoxically exhibit normal or low star formation rates. To investigate the bottleneck in their star formation, we present ALMA CO(1-0) observations for five HIX survey galaxies: ESO208-G026, ESO378-G003, ESO381-G005, ESO417-G018, and ESO055-G013. While these systems possess massive, extended HI discs, their molecular gas discs are compact with normal H$_{2}$ masses relative to their stellar mass. Dynamical modelling with 3DBarolo reveals that the HI and molecular gas are kinematically consistent. Both gas phases show high velocity dispersions, indicating enhanced turbulence compared to regular late-type galaxies. We calculate the specific angular momentum for the stellar, atomic, and molecular components alongside the global stability parameter ($q$). Most of the system angular momentum is stored in the HI discs, rendering them globally stable against collapse. This stability suppresses the atomic-to-molecular gas conversion, as evidenced by extremely long HI depletion times contrasted with typical H$_{2}$ depletion times. The high angular momentum and HI excess likely originate from past gas accretion or recent tidal interactions; this is especially apparent in ESO378-G003 and ESO381-G005, which exhibit extended HI structures and positive tidal indices.

Whether the formation of the Galactic bar was connected to the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) merger, the last significant merger experienced by the Milky Way, remains an open question. The bar formation epoch has been inferred from the star formation history of the nuclear stellar disc (NSD) traced by Mira variables, and the GSE merger epoch from the age-metallicity relation of red giants. However, these epochs rest on different stellar tracers with different age scales and cannot be compared directly. We recalibrate the Mira period-age relation onto the age scale of APOGEE red giants, with ages from a Bayesian neural network trained on Kepler asteroseismic ages. We fit the Gaia DR3 proper motions of 2394 O-rich disc Mira variables with an action-based quasi-isothermal distribution function whose parameters vary with period, and derive the period-age relation by matching their vertical velocity dispersion as a function of period to that of the APOGEE red giants as a function of age in Funakoshi et al. (2025). This places the NSD star-formation rise and peak at 8.5-10.5 Gyr ago on the red giant age scale, overlapping with the final stage of the GSE-induced starburst, thick-to-thin disc transition and the contraction of the star-forming gas disc. If NSD star formation traces bar formation, these coincidences suggest a possible scenario in which the GSE seeded the bar formation, while fresh gas supplied by the GSE and outer disc gas driven inward by its tidal torques, increased the baryonic dominance of the inner Galaxy.

We use a novel suite of constrained cosmological simulations of the Local Group to investigate the formation of alpha--Fe bimodalities in stellar discs of Milky Way analogues. In agreement with past studies using the Auriga model, we show that in our simulations the high-alpha sequence forms in a merger-induced starburst, which further quenches star formation, allowing the new stellar populations to form at low [alpha/Fe]. Producing a bimodality that is prominent at large radii, with a significant [alpha/Fe] gap and overlapping metallicity as in the Milky Way, requires the quenching of star formation across all radii where the bimodality is observed. The quenching can be achieved either with a galaxy-wide starburst, or with a retrograde merger reducing angular momentum in the outer disc and allowing more efficient dilution by the metal-poor gas in the circumgalactic medium (CGM). We show that mergers can also tilt the disc, changing the direction of galactic outflows and enriching a larger volume of the CGM with galactic metals, leading to low [alpha/Fe] scatter in the subsequently formed low-alpha sequence. We argue that the slopes in [alpha/Fe] of the two alpha sequences are crucial in narrowing down the mechanism that leads to their formation. We additionally uncover an indirect role that mergers play in enhancing the accretion of metal-poor gas onto galaxies. The smoothly accreted gas can dominate the gas content in the CGM and aid in the formation of distinct chemical sequences, especially at the low-metallicity end.

Understanding how stars form at low metallicity is challenging because carbon monoxide (CO) emission, which usually probes the molecular gas reservoir, is faint, and tends to be concentrated in parsec-scale clumps. To overcome this difficulty, in order to study the molecular clouds in metal-poor environments, we have acquired CO(2-1) ALMA maps at 3-4 pc resolution of four nearby dwarf galaxies with nebular (O/H) metallicities ranging from roughly 1/9 Z_sun to 1/3 Z_sun. Significant CO signal in the cubes has been identified through two masks, a ``strict'', high-confidence, mask and a ``broad'' one for better completeness. The former traces the cores of the clouds, while the latter includes fainter emission in the vicinity of the cores. Through a cloud-finding algorithm applied to the two sets of masked cubes, we have identified more than 200 molecular clouds across the sample, with roughly 50 in NGC3109, at 1/9 Z_sun, the lowest metallicity parsec-scale cloud population yet observed. The best-fit size-linewidth relations for the resolved clouds are similar among all galaxies, with a slope like the Milky Way (MW), but with lower velocity dispersion at a given cloud size. For a fixed CO-to-H_2 MW conversion factor, the cloud virial ratios tend to be large, and show a weak increasing trend with increasing cloud size, and decreasing apparent surface densities and metallicity. We find evidence for faint but significant CO emission outside CO-bright cores, spanning similar velocities to those observed in the cores. We attribute this to effective shielding by gas and dust, despite the low metallicities of our sample, since the CO tends to be cospatial with dust as traced by far-infrared emission.

We investigate size evolution and disk formation with a new suite of eleven cosmological zoom-in simulations, Massive Assembly of Galaxies in the Early universe (MAGE), run with RAMSES at $\sim10\,\mathrm{pc}$ resolution until $z\simeq9$. The target galaxies span stellar masses of $M_\star\simeq2\times10^8$-$3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ at $z=9$. The stellar half-mass radius grows steeply with stellar mass at low masses, turns over near $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$, and evolves weakly with redshift. The intrinsic UV sizes in MAGE overlap the compact end of observed distributions, including the compact strong-line systems, but not the more extended population at $z>9$. Dust, PSF-limited size measurements, and our overdense environments may contribute to this offset. In the simulations, stars dominate the mass within the half-mass radius above $M_\star\simeq5\times10^8\,M_\odot$, where the central circular speed exceeds a characteristic stellar-feedback scale. Outflows are then increasingly retained within the galaxy, consistent with gas recycling on galactic rather than halo scales, which sustains central gas supply and drives a compaction-like central buildup. The turnover in size at $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$ coincides with rapid growth of rotational support to $v/σ\simeq1.5$-$3$ in star-forming gas and stars younger than 10Myr, as the central circular speed exceeds the halo virial speed, which the star-forming gas velocity dispersion tracks. The subsequent low central gas fraction, declining central star formation, and younger outskirts suggest the onset of inside-out quenching.

Through a combined rest-optical and NIR observing campaign, the Large Binocular Telescope Primordial He Abundance ($Y_{\rm p}$) Project has delivered a precision measurement of $Y_{\rm p}$. Given the stringent requirements to measure gas-phase He abundances, the optical spectra contain emission features from a plethora of metals in the Interstellar Medium (ISM). We present gas-phase N, O, Ne, S, Cl, Ar, K and Fe abundance patterns in 62 low-metallicity nebulae. We assess ionization correction factors for each element and select those that reproduce the observed ionic abundance trends. The Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O abundances do not evolve with metallicity and agree with the solar ratios. Given the Type Ia enrichment of S and Ar, the solar S/O and Ar/O suggest extended star formation histories in local galaxies relative to high-$z$ galaxies at fixed O/H. We report the first ISM K abundances in low-metallicity extragalactic nebulae; both Cl/O and K/O are constant with O/H, indicating the production of these odd-Z elements follows that of $α$ elements. Consistent with prior analyses, gas-phase Fe/O is observed to decrease with O/H due to dust depletion; correcting for this produces an average [Fe/O]$=$$-$0.80, implying Fe-deficient stars capable of producing hard ionizing spectra. Finally, the metallicity-insensitive log(N/O) plateau from 50 nebulae is $-$1.43$\pm$0.10, consistent with previous works. Five galaxies have N/O significantly enhanced relative to the primary plateau, and we discuss their properties, mechanisms that could bias measurements of N/O, and nucleosynthetic progenitors that can produce enhanced N at low metallicity.
