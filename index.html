Anchoring the Chromospheric Activity$-$Rotation Relation: A Study of the Pleiades and Group X
Authors
A. Núñez, M. A. Agüeros, J. L. Curtis, C. A. Ospinal Achicanoy
Comments
Accepted for publication in the AAS Journals
Primary
astro-ph.SR
Categories
astro-ph.SR astro-ph.GA
We present a study of the rotation and the chromospheric H$α$ activity of low-mass stars in the $\approx$120 Myr-old Pleiades and the $\approx$400 Myr-old Group X open clusters. We combine published and new rotation periods ($P_\mathrm{rot}$) from TESS and ZTF, and use LAMOST, SDSS, and MDM spectra to measure H$α$ equivalent widths (EWs). While overall the Group X distributions of $P_\mathrm{rot}$ and EWs are as expected, detailed comparisons to the Pleiades and to the 700-Myr-old benchmark Praesepe produce some surprises. For F through mid K stars, the Group X color$-$period sequence lies between those of the Pleiades and of Praesepe, as expected. But the $P_\mathrm{rot}$ for its late K and early M dwarfs and the Praesepe slow-rotator sequence overlap, indicating that the 700 Myr-old stars have not spun down significantly despite being nearly twice as old as their Group X counterparts. In the Rossby number$-L_\mathrm{Hα}/L_\mathrm{bol}$ plane, rapid rotators in Group X are saturated, as is seen in older clusters, whereas the rapid rotators in the Pleiades show a weak but measurable dependence of $L_\mathrm{Hα}/L_\mathrm{bol}$ on rotation. This implies that the chromospheric activity$-$rotation relation attains its mature form between 120 and 400 Myr. Dividing the sample by color reveals that K, M0, and M1 dwarfs are no longer saturated by $\approx$120 Myr, M2 and M3 dwarfs become unsaturated between $\approx$400 and $\approx$700 Myr, and later M dwarfs remain largely saturated through field ages even as their saturated $L_\mathrm{Hα}/L_\mathrm{bol}$ levels decline, indicating that chromospheric evolution is not determined by rotation period alone.

Diffuse X-ray emission in galaxies originates from stellar-feedback-heated gas and unresolved compact sources. We investigate its thermal component and the scaling of X-ray luminosity with star formation rate (SFR), including galaxies with very low SFRs. We analyzed eROSITA/SRG data from the four all-sky surveys (eRASS:4) for a blind, volume-limited sample of 140 galaxies within 25 Mpc from the Updated Nearby Galaxy Catalog (Karachentsev et al. 2013), cross-matched with HECATEv2 (Kyritsis et al. 2026b). Early-type galaxies were analyzed separately, while star-forming galaxies were divided into low-, medium-, and high-specific-SFR (sSFR) subsamples. We modeled the integrated X-ray spectra with thermal plasma and power-law components, stacked spectra for each subsample, and derived total and thermal X-ray luminosities. The stacked spectra show a stronger soft thermal component at high sSFR, whereas low-sSFR and early-type galaxies are increasingly dominated by the harder non-thermal continuum. For star-forming galaxies, the total 0.5-8 keV luminosity follows $\log L_X \sim 39.5 + 0.9\log\mathrm{SFR}$, with an intrinsic scatter of about 0.9 dex. The 0.5-2 keV thermal luminosity follows $\log L_X \sim 38.7 + 1.1\log\mathrm{SFR}$, with an intrinsic scatter of about 0.7 dex. The thermal luminosity remains well correlated with SFR down to $\sim 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ and is consistent with previous relations for star-forming galaxies. By contrast, the flatter and more scattered total X-ray luminosity-SFR relation suggests that unresolved X-ray binaries likely dominate at low SFR, while thermal X-ray emission remains a robust tracer of stellar feedback.

In galaxies, stellar populations represent a superposition of both in situ and accreted components, tracing internal chemical evolution as well as the histories of merged satellites. We investigate how assembly history shapes the chemical properties of present-day stellar populations in low-mass dwarf galaxies. We analyze a suite of EDGE-INFERNO star-by-star cosmological zoom-in simulations of galaxies with present-day halo mass $\sim1.4\times10^9\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$. We systematically vary galaxy growth while preserving the present-day halo mass, engineering our simulations to span a continuum of assembly histories. Early-forming systems form most of their stars in a single progenitor, with centrally concentrated star formation and a continuous enrichment history. In contrast, our late-forming systems assemble by accretion of multiple progenitors with their own stellar populations, resulting in fragmented star formation across several halos with independent chemical evolution tracks. These regimes produce qualitatively different chemical signatures: single-progenitor systems exhibit connected, well-defined trends in abundance space, while multi-progenitor systems show multiple, distinct stellar populations with limited overlap. We find that metallicity distribution functions provide initial constraints on assembly history for samples of at least $50$-$100$ member stars. Multi-element abundance analysis significantly improves the recovery of distinct components, although robust reconstruction still requires larger samples. Fully separating in situ and accreted populations likely requires kinematic information. Accreted stars can contribute substantially even at high metallicities in multi-progenitor systems, while external enrichment can cause accreted material to follow similar chemical trends as in situ stars, particularly in the single-progenitor regime.

We investigate the thermal phase structures and kinematics of atomic hydrogen (H I) gas in the Draco nebula to clarify how infalling H I evolves toward cold atomic and molecular clouds. Using the DHIGLS H I 21 cm data, we apply the Regularized Optimization for Hyper-Spectral Analysis (ROHSA) algorithm to decompose the emission into spatially coherent Gaussian components, and classify them as warm neutral medium (WNM), lukewarm neutral medium (LNM), and cold neutral medium (CNM). We identify two kinematically distinct WNM components: WNM1 near $-3$ km s$^{-1}$ and WNM2 near $-30$ km s$^{-1}$. The LNM forms a broad velocity bridge between two WNMs, whereas the CNM is concentrated within a narrower intermediate velocity range. CO emission is also confined to this interval and is associated with CNM-rich regions. The H I mass fractions are $\sim 41\%$, $\sim 23\%$, $\sim 17\%$, and $\sim 19\%$ for WNM1, WNM2, LNM, and CNM, respectively, with the LNM and CNM together accounting for $\sim 36\%$ of the total H I mass. No comparably independent WNM component is recovered at intermediate velocities, although a weak broad component may remain unresolved. The observed phase structures are consistent with a dynamically compressed multiphase interaction layer in which compression increases the gas density, shortens the radiative cooling time, and drives the evolution from WNM through thermally unstable gas toward the CNM. The present interaction may both create new CNM, and further compress or grow pre-existing dense structures. Our results provide the missing link between previous evidence for dynamical interaction and molecular cloud formation in Draco, and identify CNM formation as a key step in transforming gas accreting toward the Galactic disk into cold atomic and molecular clouds.
Turbulence modes in molecular clouds in M33: a challenging test of the Brunt-Federrath framework

Understanding the turbulent structure of molecular clouds is essential for linking galactic environment to the regulation of star formation. Although Galactic molecular clouds have been studied extensively, extending turbulence-mode analysis beyond the Milky Way remains challenging because of the limited spatial resolution and dynamic range of extragalactic observations. We assess the applicability of the Brunt \& Federrath (2014; BF14) framework to molecular clouds in M33. We apply BF14 to 128 $^{13}$CO(2-1) clouds, selecting a primary quality-controlled sample of 54 clouds and a more restrictive 25-cloud subsample defined using a stricter extraction-padding criterion (PAD=2) for quantitative analysis.Tests of cloud and noise power spectra, Fourier-space cutoffs, extraction padding, and Monte Carlo uncertainties demonstrate that the retained spatial frequencies are dominated by astrophysical signal and that the inferred turbulence-mode fractions are robust against reasonable analysis choices. Both samples yield consistent distributions of the solenoidal fraction, with mean values of $R \simeq 0.22$ for both samples and median values of $R \simeq 0.18$ and $0.17$, respectively, indicating that compressive motions dominate the turbulent energy budget. No strong systematic variations of $R$ are found with galactocentric distance or tracers of recent star formation. These results demonstrate that, despite the challenging spatial resolution of current extragalactic molecular-line observations, the BF14 framework can be applied reliably to nearby galaxies using spectroscopic observations alone. To our knowledge, this constitutes the first application of the full BF14 formalism to extragalactic molecular-cloud observations.
