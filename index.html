Tidal Braking Does Not Dominate Open-Cluster Slow Rotators
Authors
Zixun Zhan, Chenyu He, Weijia Sun, Chengyuan Li
Comments
14 pages, 7 figures
Primary
astro-ph.SR
Categories
astro-ph.SR astro-ph.GA
Extended main-sequence turn-offs (eMSTOs) observed in open clusters younger than $2~\mathrm{Gyr}$ are commonly attributed to a spread in stellar rotation rates. Tidal synchronization in close binary systems has been proposed as a possible mechanism for producing slowly rotating stars. To test this hypothesis, we measured the radial-velocity (RV) dispersions of slow-rotator samples associated with the eMSTOs of eight open clusters using ESO VLT/GIRAFFE and SALT/RSS spectroscopy. We extended the analysis to 40 open clusters using Gaia DR3 RV measurements. The observed cluster RV dispersions are generally substantially smaller than those predicted for binary populations dominated by close systems with orbital periods for which tidal synchronization is expected to be effective. In particular, all 40 clusters in the Gaia sample have RV dispersions below the prediction of the $P=10$ d binary-population model. These results suggest that close, tidally synchronized binaries are unlikely to be the dominant origin of slow-rotator populations in young and intermediate-age open clusters, although a contribution from undetected or wider binary systems cannot be excluded.
A Large-Sample Study of Interstellar Reddening in Galactic Star Clusters

The interstellar reddening (extinction or absorption) is caused mainly by the dust grains of different sizes and compounds. All astrophysical observations are somehow affected by this effect, depending on the wavelength and location of the observed object. Different methods and techniques have been developed over the last nearly one hundred years to determine the reddening and to map the solar neighbourhood. The members of open clusters are a consistent population of stars formed together with a homogeneous metallicity. Therefore, the reddening can be estimated in a statistical way, avoiding all problems of single stars and spectral-energy-distribution fitting, for example. Other than for most available works, we can also use more massive upper main-sequence stars for the analysis. We used classical colour-colour diagrams (also known as the Q-method) for (B-V), (BP-RP), (U-B), (U-BP), (U-RP), and (U-G) to determine consistent mean reddening values and their errors. The corresponding standard lines for the fitting are independent of the age and metallicity. We determined colour excesses for 5377 Galactic open clusters covering Galactocentric distances from two to twenty kpc, respectively. The derived values were checked with existing dust and extinction maps. In general, we found good agreement. The solar neighbourhood of +-1500 pc shows a very patchy structure of the dust composition. Our results lay the foundations for constructing a new map of the reddening (dust) distribution and estimating clusters' metallicities by isochrone fitting, keeping the distances and reddening values fixed.

Large spectroscopic surveys have measured atmospheric parameters for millions of Milky Way stars, but differences in instruments, wavelength ranges, analysis methods, and assumptions introduce survey-dependent systematics that complicate their joint use. We aim to place effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity estimates from major spectroscopic surveys onto a common, externally validated scale, and to provide a homogenised catalogue for large-scale Galactic studies. We construct the calibration hierarchically. We first define an internal reference scale from the best-populated and best-overlapped surveys (APOGEE, GALAH, and LAMOST LRS), and model survey-to-reference corrections and their uncertainties with machine-learning regression. The scale is then extended to LAMOST MRS, Gaia RVS, RAVE, DESI, Gaia-ESO, and SEGUE, and anchored to high-quality measurements from the PASTEL database. The calibration reduces survey-to-survey offsets and improves consistency in independent overlap samples: independent calibration of GALAH and LAMOST LRS temperatures onto the APOGEE scale reduces their median offset from 44.5 K to 6.2 K. The final catalogue contains about 10.13 million stars, including 6.10 million in the recommended high-quality subset and 3.69 million in the metallicity-optimised subset. For the high-quality subset, Gaia benchmark stars give median residuals of 30 K in $T_{\rm eff}$, 0.03 dex in $\log g$, and $-0.02$ dex in [Fe/H]. The [Fe/H]-optimised subset gives median residuals of 0.003 dex against Gaia benchmark stars, 0.05 dex for globular clusters, and $-0.02$ dex for open clusters. As an example application, we confirm the presence of metal-poor stars on thin-disc-like orbits, including stars with [Fe/H] $< -2$, showing that the catalogue can be used to study rare Galactic populations on a consistent multi-survey scale.

Molecular outflows probe protostellar accretion and mass transfer from clump scales to forming stars. We present an ALMA study of CO (J=2-1) outflows toward 13 massive clumps from the SQUALO sample, spanning a broad evolutionary range and exhibiting clump-scale infall. We identify 51 high-velocity CO lobes associated with 31 outflow systems and derive their physical and dynamical properties assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium. Outflow inferred accretion rates are typically 10^{-5}-10^{-3} Msun/yr, consistent with active massive star formation. The maximum outflow velocity shows little dependence on candidate driving-core mass, indicating that it is not a reliable tracer of protostellar mass in clustered environments. Outflow momentum flux correlates positively with clump surface density, whereas accretion rate shows a tentative, statistically nonsignificant decrease with increasing virial parameter. Total outflow inferred accretion rate shows a tentative positive relation with clump-scale infall rate but remains systematically lower, suggesting that the detected outflows represent only part of the large-scale mass flux. The clump L/M ratio moderately anticorrelates with projected outflow length, although projection, observational limitations, and the embedded source population prevent a unique evolutionary interpretation. Together with the continuum derived fragmentation properties, our results show that compact cores embedded within infalling clumps drive the outflows. The tentative relation between clump-scale infall and protostellar accretion suggests a possible connection across these scales, although our observations do not directly trace mass transfer from filaments to individual protostars.

We investigate the kinematics of the Carina Nebula using a sample of young stellar objects (YSOs) cross-matched with Gaia DR3, together with $^{12}\mathrm{CO}\,(1\mbox{--}0)$ molecular gas observations and APOGEE radial-velocity data. The results show that the proper motions of the YSOs in the four peripheral subregions of Carina display a systematic outward expansion, and that the two-dimensional expansion velocities of the subregions are approximately $0.57\mbox{--}4.56~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The YSOs in the Central Region exhibit ordered rotation around an axis at a position angle of $86.9\pm1.5^\circ$, with a rotation velocity of $1.75\pm0.03~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Further momentum estimates suggest that the current expansion in the peripheral regions may result from the combined effects of feedback from massive OB stars and possible past supernova explosions. These results indicate that feedback from massive stars plays a dominant role in the dynamical evolution of the giant molecular cloud in the Carina Nebula.

The observed stellar mass-metallicity relation for ultra-faint galaxies in the Local Group (LG) is in tension with many theoretical predictions, suggesting the need for modified or additional channels of elemental enrichment at low metallicities. We present a suite of FIRE-3 cosmological baryonic zoom-in simulations of isolated ultra-faint galaxies run to z=0 (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 30 Msun, 0.1 pc) and LMC-mass environments (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 110 Msun, 0.5 pc), with different sub-grid models for Population III stars. Under our Pop. III model(s), star particles forming below a critical metallicity Z_crit represent an initial mass function (IMF) consisting of only massive stars (8-100 Msun). We examine how various stellar feedback processes impact the star formation histories (SFHs) and elemental abundances of ultra-faint galaxies. We find that our Pop. III model variants introduce an effective metallicity floor in ultra-faints at z=0, at Z_floor ~ 1e-5 Zsun to 1e-3 Zsun or [Fe/H]_floor ~ -7 to -5 depending on the model. However, simply including the Pop. III model does not systematically guarantee that all (or even most) ultra-faints enrich up to [Fe/H] ~ -2.5 to fully resolve the tension with the observed mass-metallicity relation, even though we obtain some galaxies at that level. Crucially, the ultra-faints that do enrich in [Fe/H] to this level observed in the LG have extended SFHs after Pop. III enrichment and significant iron enrichment from white dwarf (Type Ia) supernovae, indicating that iron enrichment from Pop. III alone is not sufficient. We also examine the ways in which early-time Pop. III feedback impacts the metallicity distributions and [Mg/Fe] abundance ratios in ultra-faint galaxies, and make a prediction for the stellar mass - [Mg/H] metallicity relation from our simulations.

The next generation of galaxy surveys will bring a plethora of new observations, a vast majority of which will be observed with a few photometric broadbands, limiting the information we can infer from traditional SED fitting methods. To maximize the scientific value of these surveys, techniques that can go beyond traditional summary statistics in extracting information from galaxy observations are needed. Moving beyond integrated photometry, we introduce the Katachi-PRIME method to do spectral energy distribution (SED) based inference that jointly incorporates morphological and SED information. Katachi-PRIME uses implicit likelihood inference (ILI) to incorporate realistic morphological priors from gri broadband images, to make predictions of: stellar mass (M$_*$), specific star formation rate (sSFR), redshift (z), age (t$_{50}$), dust attenuation (Av), and gas-phase metallicity (12+log(O/H)) of the galaxy. We find that this improves constraints compared to traditional 5-band photometry. We then use the learned morphological priors to examine the extent to which morphology encodes information about past star formation, finding a stark dichotomy in how morphology encodes star formation history. While the stable morphologies of main-sequence and quiescent galaxies can predict past star formation out to $\sim 4-7$ Gyr and $\sim3-5$ Gyr, respectively, starburst and green-valley populations above and below the main sequence tend to experience structural disruptions that obscure all but the most recent star formation activity. Methods like Katachi-PRIME maximize the scientific value of future telescopes by providing broad-baseline SED-level property estimates by incorporating morphological information.

We present a resolved far-infrared/sub-millimetre study of dust in the nearby spiral galaxy M81 using Herschel PACS/SPIRE and JCMT SCUBA-2 observations from 70 to $850\,μ\mathrm{m}$. We model the dust emission using both single-temperature modified blackbody (SMBB) fits and point process mapping (PPMAP), comparing pixel-by-pixel SED-fitting with image cubes of dust surface density as a function of position and temperature. For fixed $β= 1.9$ and a dust opacity of $κ_{350\,μ\mathrm{m}} = 1.92\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}\,\mathrm{g}^{-1}$, SMBB gives an integrated dust mass of $(1.77 \pm 0.09) \times 10^{7}\,M_\odot$, while PPMAP gives $(2.65 \pm 0.13) \times10^{7}\,M_\odot$. The higher PPMAP mass is expected because real lines of sight contain dust over a range of temperatures, whereas SMBB assumes a single effective temperature and can underestimate the dust mass. Both methods show enhanced dust surface densities along the spiral arms and a radially declining dust temperature, with the warmest dust in the central region. Variable-$β$ fits indicate only moderate spatial variation in $β$ for well-constrained pixels, but the strong $T$-$β$ degeneracy motivates a fixed emissivity index for the main analysis. Combining the dust maps with HI data, we find a positive radial gas-to-dust ratio gradient, with $d\log_{10}(\mathrm{GDR})/dR = 0.065\,\mathrm{dex}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$. Comparison with M81's metallicity gradient implies a declining dust-to-metals ratio, potentially consistent with more efficient grain growth in the inner disc. The star formation rate is modest, $\sim 0.27\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, and is strongly concentrated along the spiral arms. These results suggest that the dust, gas, and star-forming structures of M81 are strongly shaped by its spiral arms, while the central region hosts the warmest dust in the disc.

Dust attenuation remains a dominant systematic uncertainty in measurements of an emission line, and therefore galaxies properties derived from the emission lines such as star formation rates. Corrections based only on the Balmer decrement require an assumed attenuation-curve slope, even though that slope varies among galaxies. We use JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy from JADES to constrain effective dust attenuation curves for 386 star-forming galaxies at $0.5\leq z\leq7$. For each galaxy, we combine H$α$, H$β$, and one additional hydrogen recombination line and solve for the normalization $τ_V$ and slope $n$ of a power-law attenuation curve, allowing the slope to vary from galaxy to galaxy. We found slopes and normalization span a broad range even for galaxies at similar redshifts. Additionally, our method using Paschen transitions provided substantially greater constraints the attenuation-curve slope than Balmer-only combinations. When comparing to SMC-like or Calzetti-like corrections we find our method produces more consistent star formation rates with less scatter. These improved dust corrections provide an avenue to better describe star formation rates in the epoch of peak star formation and the early universe.

To determine the role of spiral arms and stellar feedback on the formation of stars, we examine the positions of optically identified and emerging star clusters in NGC 628. We find that the youngest clusters extend further into the spiral arms at small radii than at large radii, as expected when the angular flow rate through a wave-like arm increases with greater distance from corotation. The embedded or emerging star clusters (eYSCs) are identified relative to four distinct morphological features in the 7.7 micron image: spiral arm filaments, spiral arm spurs, spiral interarm feathers, and bubbles. Spiral arm-related eYSCs are presumably first-generation stars made from interstellar gas that was directly compressed by the density wave, while clusters along the rims of bubbles are secondary generations produced by feedback compression. The mass functions of the first and second generations differ: eYSCs in spiral arm filaments extend to significantly higher masses than eYSCs in bubbles. Secondary star formation continues downstream from primary star formation, removing the expected age gradient through the arms. Bubble eYSCs account for approximately one-third of all the emerging cluster mass. The outer arms have a more mixed distribution of spiral- and bubble-related eYSCs than the inner arms, potentially providing a way to distinguish wave and material arms and to find corotation in wave-like arms. Among the 17 most massive clusters with log M(Msun)>4, eight of them are in four tight pairs; in at least one case, the dynamical time for the pair is comparable to the cluster ages.

Nuclear star clusters and supermassive black holes are the dominant compact components in galaxy nuclei. They can coexist within the same system, and their relative prominence traces the history of gas inflow, star formation, and dynamical evolution on parsec scales. Key questions remain open concerning the physical channels that assemble nuclear star clusters, the mass regimes in which supermassive black holes become dynamically dominant, and the conditions under which compact stellar nuclei are suppressed, destroyed, or replaced by cusp-like structures. We describe how near-infrared integral-field spectroscopy with SHARP on the Extremely Large Telescope, combined with high-resolution imaging from MICADO, enables quantitative tests of nuclear formation and evolution scenarios within a unified framework. The joint analysis of stellar dynamics, stellar populations, and nuclear structure allows black-hole masses, internal kinematics of nuclear star clusters, and signatures of gas inflow to be measured consistently within the same systems, and defines the mass and distance ranges where robust constraints can be obtained. Recent discoveries of compact active nuclei at high redshift indicate that efficient black-hole growth can occur in systems with weak or compact stellar components. Although nearby galaxies are not evolutionary analogues of early systems, the diversity of nuclear configurations accessible with SHARP and MICADO provides a local laboratory for isolating the physical mechanisms that regulate the coupled evolution of central black holes and nuclear stellar structures.

We present SEDust, an automated pipeline for spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting and the estimation of stellar mass-loss and dust-production rates. We apply this pipeline to quantify the mass-loss and dust injection rates in the dwarf galaxy NGC~6822, focusing on long-period variables (LPVs) and pulsating asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars as major contributors to dust feedback into the interstellar medium. The total stellar mass-loss rate is found to be $(3.70 \pm 1.16) \times 10^{-3}\ M_{\odot} \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. Two alternative methods (an LPV-based estimate and DESK) give totals that differ from this value by factors of $\sim$2.3 (lower) and $\sim$2.4 (higher), respectively, which we adopt as an estimate of the method-dependent systematic uncertainty. A positive correlation between stellar luminosity, intrinsic reddening caused by circumstellar dust self-extinction, and mass-loss rate is observed. The specific mass-return rate from AGB stars is $(7.40 \pm 2.30) \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, indicating that mass return from AGB stars alone is insufficient to sustain the current star formation rate of NGC~6822. This result implies the need for external gas accretion or additional mass-return mechanisms.

Aims. We aim to develop a framework for producing CSST-like mock images of resolved stellar populations from simulated galaxies, demonstrated here using two TNG50 galaxies as illustrative test cases. Methods. Starting from simulated galaxies from TNG50, we use the population synthesis code TRILEGAL to sample individual stars from star particles, retaining bright stars explicitly and treating the remaining unresolved light, including faint stellar light and nebular emission from star-forming regions, as a diffuse background component of the galaxy. Based on the dust distribution inferred from the gas mass and metallicity of gas cells, we use the radiative transfer (RT) code SKIRT to construct the dust model. From this model, we compute the line-of-sight extinction for individual stars, while image-level transmission maps for the diffuse light are derived through RT simulations. For young stars still embedded in their natal molecular clouds, we additionally include birth-cloud attenuation. The resulting dust-attenuated stellar catalogues and diffuse-light images are then processed with the CSST simulation pipeline, which applies PSF convolution, sky background, and instrumental noise. Results. Our framework produces mock CSST images for both a spiral galaxy and a dwarf irregular galaxy from TNG50 in the seven photometric bands. The mock images combine resolved stars, unresolved diffuse light, and dust attenuation within a unified framework, and include the main observational effects expected for the survey. The images are provided with known astrometric and photometric calibration information, and are intended as realistic image-level inputs for subsequent source detection, photometric recovery, and completeness analyses of resolved stellar populations. The framework is designed to be adaptable to other hydrodynamic simulations and observing configurations.
