The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. II. The Multi-component Age-Metallicity Structure of the High-$α$ Disk

Tiancheng Sun, Maosheng Xiang, Jifeng Liu, Meng Zhang, Yaqian Wu
Comments

astro-ph.GA

Whether the high-$α$ disk experienced a continuous or episodic star-formation history remains an open question. Here we investigate the age--metallicity distribution of the high-$α$ disk using approximately 145,000 Gaia DR3 subgiants with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]>-1$, 6.5~kpc $\lesssim R\lesssim9.5$~kpc, and $|Z|\lesssim1.5$~kpc. We adopt a forward-modelling framework that accounts for individual age and metallicity uncertainties and corrects for sample selection effects. The reconstructed distribution reveals two components: a broad, dominant population peaking at $\sim10.6$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.38$, and an older, more metal-poor population peaking at $\sim12.4$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.79$. These features suggest an episodic history of star formation and chemical enrichment in the high-$α$ disk, qualitatively consistent with cosmological simulations in which recurrent gas accretion drives variations in the star formation rate. The age of the older component is close to the $\sim12.3$~Gyr epoch inferred for the proposed Low-energy--Kraken--Heracles accretion event, suggesting a possible connection between this early interaction and enhanced disk star formation. There is tentative evidence that the dominant population is possibly composed of two sub-components with age distributions peaking at $\sim11.1$ Gyr and $\sim10.0$ Gyr, respectively. Together, these results favour a picture in which the high-$α$ disk was built through recurrent episodes of enhanced star formation rather than a single temporally smooth phase, even for the regime of ${\rm [Fe/H]}>-1$.

We present five technical validations of SCUBA-2/POL-2 polarimetry on the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope at 450 and 850 $μ$m, using 174 archival fields in 69 molecular cloud regions and subsets appropriate to each test. Extended-source calibration avoids a deterministic shift of $Δβ=-1.05$ in the dust emissivity index $β$ that would result from calibrating extended emission using peak brightness. We modeled Stokes $I$ noise with additive and signal-dependent components. The measured median ratio of surface-brightness noise at 450/850 $μ$m is 25. Independent tests of Stokes $Q,U$ noise support approximately equal additive variances and weak error correlations, providing a basis for propagating polarization uncertainties. Corrections determined for individual fields reduce additive zero-level offsets of order $1$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$ in the 450 $μ$m Stokes $Q$ and $U$ intensities. The three-step regression leaves a median combined residual amplitude of $0.07$ mJy arcsec$^{-2}$. Independent comparisons with SOFIA/HAWC+ at 154 and 214 $μ$m support the correction in strongly affected fields. We matched the 450 $μ$m maps to the modeled 850 $μ$m beam and the common $12.6''$-$300''$ range of angular scales. We compared three reductions of the same 450 $μ$m observations across 30 sources. Taking the median angular residual relative to the final product for each source, we find that beam matching reduces the median over sources from $6.3^\circ$ to $4.7^\circ$. After matching, 29 of the 30 sources have median residuals below $10^\circ$. We also quantified how residual $Q/U$ offsets propagate in ratios of polarization fractions, differences in spectral indices, and polarization angles. These validations provide procedures and quantitative references for interpreting POL-2 measurements and combining them with polarimetry at other wavelengths.

Molecular clouds exhibit complex, multi-scale dynamics driven by the interplay of turbulence, self-gravity, and gas pressure. Modelling these systems with conventional grid-based methods becomes increasingly expensive as spatial resolution and dimensionality increase. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) offer a mesh-free alternative by incorporating the governing equations directly into the training objective, but their ability to model realistic turbulent self-gravitating flows remains largely unexplored. We apply PINNs to isothermal molecular clouds initialized with astrophysically motivated turbulent velocity fields, considering subsonic and supersonic evolution in two and three dimensions. We benchmark the predicted density and velocity fields against the finite-volume code Athena++. PINNs accurately recover the global evolution of the flow over multiple free-fall times across turbulent realizations, Mach numbers, and dimensionalities. Their training cost also exhibits weak dependence on spatial resolution, increasing by less than 25% from 128^3 to 1024^3 in our experiments. These results demonstrate that PINNs can model substantially more realistic self-gravitating turbulent flows than previously explored and provide a promising complementary framework for studying these systems.

JWST is revealing abundant populations of high-$z$ galaxies with unforeseen properties: UV-luminous galaxies with high nitrogen enrichment, populations of very massive stars (VMSs; $>100\ M_\odot$), and early forms of massive black hole growth such as the Little Red Dots (LRDs). Here we present all these phenomena appearing simultaneously in COLA1, a luminous ($M_{\rm UV}=-21.3$) galaxy at $z=6.6$. New NIRSpec medium-resolution data reveal a blue UV continuum ($β_{\rm UV}=-2.5$) with strong emission lines including NIV] $\lambda1486$, stellar P Cygni profiles, and broad HeII $\lambda1640$. We fit the UV with stellar population models, and find that the models including VMSs (limited to $300\ M_\odot$) are statistically preferred. The best-fit stellar population has $M_*\approx10^8\ M_\odot$, and a young age of $2.4\pm0.8$ Myr. The rest-optical shows a redder continuum ($β_{\rm opt}=-1.1$) and broad Balmer lines with Balmer absorption akin to LRD spectra. By evolving the best-fit stellar population we find that the UV could dim by 1-2 mag in $\sim5$ Myr, and hence this system would be observed as a typical V-shaped LRD. We measure a supersolar $\rm log_{10}(N/O)=-0.07\pm0.05$ and subsolar $\rm log_{10}(C/O)=-0.64\pm0.11$; therefore $\rm log_{10}(N/C)=+0.58\pm0.12$. The independent density-sensitive NIV] $λ\lambda1483,1486$ and CIII] $λ\lambda1906/1909$ ratios require two zones with $n_e\gtrsim10^{6.5}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $n_e\lesssim10^{3.5}$ cm$^{-3}$, respectively. We estimate $\rm log_{10}(N/C)>1.3$ (2$σ$) for the dense zone, requiring extreme N/C enrichment mechanisms such as highly rotating $>100\ M_\odot$ VMSs, tidal disruption events, or supermassive stars. We speculate that runaway collisions of massive stars in a dense stellar cluster may have lead to the seeding of an intermediate-mass black hole resulting in the formation of an LRD.

Granulation and mesogranulation represent different spatial and temporal scales of convection, but are commonly assumed to follow the same stellar atmospheric scaling relations in red giants. We test this assumption using the largest sample assembled for this purpose to date -- 9,328 Kepler red giants spanning the red giant branch and red clump phase, cross-matched with APOGEE stellar parameters. We model each star's power spectrum background to obtain the radius-corrected amplitude and characteristic frequency of both components, and derive empirical scaling relations with effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity through Bayesian model comparison. Gaussian Process and symbolic regression provide independent validation. We find that granulation and mesogranulation are not self-similar: their surface-gravity dependencies are statistically indistinguishable, consistent with a shared envelope geometry, but their temperature dependencies diverge: mesogranulation's amplitude rises steeply with Teff, whereas granulation's shows no statistically justified dependence, and both frequencies respond weakly and similarly. We propose that this pattern is consistent with granulation forming in a thin near-surface layer, where radiation takes over from convection and the Mach number depends only weakly on Teff. Mesogranulation originates deeper, where convection carries the full flux and its Mach number and cell size increase with Teff. Metallicity raises both amplitudes but has little effect on the frequencies. Our results indicate that the self-similarity assumption underlying two-component background models does not hold, with direct implications for background modeling and its downstream application in Galactic archaeology.

The dust extinction curve is an important probe of the properties of dust grains, particularly their size distribution. The properties of dust grains are expected to change significantly in dense molecular clouds due to shielding from interstellar radiation and increased frequency of grain-grain and grain-gas collisions. We measure the dust extinction curve shape parameter R(V) in 9 nearby, dense molecular clouds using data from the APOGEE spectroscopic survey, in combination with ten-band photometry from PS1, 2MASS, and WISE. We analyze the results to determine how the extinction curve varies in nearby molecular clouds. We find that most molecular clouds show a positive correlation between R(V) and E(B-V), but the strength of the correlation varies significantly between clouds. In addition, we find that the clouds with the strongest correlation tend to have very high dispersion in R(V) at fixed E(B-V), which tightens dramatically when clouds are subdivided into separate regions. This suggests that local environmental conditions beyond density shape the R(V)-E(B-V) relation.

We obtained and analyzed long-slit spectra of three lenticular galaxies with optical rings: PGC 48114, ESO 234-011, and ESO 444-076. Ionized gas was detected in all of them. In PGC 48114, the excitation mechanism of the ionized gas differs along the ring, despite the consistent kinematics of the ionized gas and stars, as we showed earlier. Narrow-band images of PGC 48114 in the H$α$ and [NII]$λ$6583 emission lines revealed a star formation arc in the northwestern part of the ring. In ESO 234-011 and ESO 444-076, the ionized gas along the long slit is shock-excited and have kinematic discrepancy with the stars. This implies accretion of matter at an angle to the main disk of the S0 galaxies, which likely inhibits ongoing star formation. The companions of these S0 galaxies are late-type galaxies with extensive ongoing star formation. These companions may be the sources of the gas, or each pair may be feeding from a common gas reservoir.

Galaxy interactions can induce bursts of star formation (SF), while internal or secular processes can also trigger starbursts (SB). Recent studies of the spatial distribution of SF in high-mass (M$_{*}>10^{10}M_{\odot}$) SB galaxies show that SF is centrally concentrated in merger-driven SBs, whereas in secularly driven SB systems, SF is uniformly enhanced across the system. However, it is not clear whether such distinct spatial signatures are present in low-mass (M$_{*} < 10^{10} M_{\odot}$) SB galaxies. In this context, we studied the SF properties of a sample of 272 isolated SB (i.e., with no massive galaxy, within a 1 Mpc$^3$ volume) dwarf galaxies consisting of 17 post-merger and 255 non-interacting SB galaxies, spanning a stellar mass range of $10^{7}-10^{9.6} M_{\odot}$ and a redshift range of 0.01-0.12. For this study, we utilise FUV imaging data from GALEX to estimate the star formation rates of our sample galaxies. We investigated the spatial variation of SB activity by comparing star formation rate surface density (SFRD) across radial bins for post-merger and non-interacting samples of dwarfs. We find an enhancement in SFRD across all radial bins for both samples, and that the enhancement is more or less uniform across the galaxy, within uncertainties. This indicates that SF in low-mass SB galaxies is uniformly enhanced throughout the galaxy, regardless of the mechanism that triggers the SB. This contrasts with observations in massive galaxies, where SF is centrally concentrated in merger-driven SB systems. The lack of central concentration of SF in low-mass merger systems could be due to their shallow gravitational potentials and the compression of extended gaseous envelopes during interactions, which trigger SF across these galaxies. Deeper, higher-spatial-resolution observations are essential for better understanding the spatial distribution of SF in low-mass galaxies.

Galaxies with extreme amounts of neutral atomic hydrogen (HI), known as HI-eXtreme (HIX) galaxies, paradoxically exhibit normal or low star formation rates. To investigate the bottleneck in their star formation, we present ALMA CO(1-0) observations for five HIX survey galaxies: ESO208-G026, ESO378-G003, ESO381-G005, ESO417-G018, and ESO055-G013. While these systems possess massive, extended HI discs, their molecular gas discs are compact with normal H$_{2}$ masses relative to their stellar mass. Dynamical modelling with 3DBarolo reveals that the HI and molecular gas are kinematically consistent. Both gas phases show high velocity dispersions, indicating enhanced turbulence compared to regular late-type galaxies. We calculate the specific angular momentum for the stellar, atomic, and molecular components alongside the global stability parameter ($q$). Most of the system angular momentum is stored in the HI discs, rendering them globally stable against collapse. This stability suppresses the atomic-to-molecular gas conversion, as evidenced by extremely long HI depletion times contrasted with typical H$_{2}$ depletion times. The high angular momentum and HI excess likely originate from past gas accretion or recent tidal interactions; this is especially apparent in ESO378-G003 and ESO381-G005, which exhibit extended HI structures and positive tidal indices.

Whether the formation of the Galactic bar was connected to the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) merger, the last significant merger experienced by the Milky Way, remains an open question. The bar formation epoch has been inferred from the star formation history of the nuclear stellar disc (NSD) traced by Mira variables, and the GSE merger epoch from the age-metallicity relation of red giants. However, these epochs rest on different stellar tracers with different age scales and cannot be compared directly. We recalibrate the Mira period-age relation onto the age scale of APOGEE red giants, with ages from a Bayesian neural network trained on Kepler asteroseismic ages. We fit the Gaia DR3 proper motions of 2394 O-rich disc Mira variables with an action-based quasi-isothermal distribution function whose parameters vary with period, and derive the period-age relation by matching their vertical velocity dispersion as a function of period to that of the APOGEE red giants as a function of age in Funakoshi et al. (2025). This places the NSD star-formation rise and peak at 8.5-10.5 Gyr ago on the red giant age scale, overlapping with the final stage of the GSE-induced starburst, thick-to-thin disc transition and the contraction of the star-forming gas disc. If NSD star formation traces bar formation, these coincidences suggest a possible scenario in which the GSE seeded the bar formation, while fresh gas supplied by the GSE and outer disc gas driven inward by its tidal torques, increased the baryonic dominance of the inner Galaxy.

In this work we investigate the mass density - size scaling law in molecular clouds (MCs). This relation reflects the fractal nature of MCs and plays a key role for understanding the physics, structure and evolution of these objects. We make use of the notion "ensemble of MCs", introduced in our previous work (Donkov, Veltchev \& Klessen, 2017), in which all MCs with the same probability density function (PDF) of mass density and effective size are represented by an abstract spherical cloud with the same PDF and size. In this spirit, the model is built on the base of abstract scales of the clouds' substructures (which are simply the radii of the spherical object). We consider two forms of the mass density - size scaling law: differential and integral, which in turn reveal the local and the global fractal clouds' structure. Both scaling functions are characterized by their scaling exponents, which can be explicitly expressed by the PDF of mass density, in the general case. Moreover, we derive a first order linear differential equation connecting the two scaling exponents and obtain its exact solution. As examples we apply this abstract construction to two PDFs: the so called power-law tail and the log-normal. Both have great importance for MC structure and evolution, as the latter corresponds to the earlier stages of clouds' evolution, when supersonic turbulence dominates the physical processes, while the former describes the latest stages of evolution, when star-formation takes place. The obtained results for the scaling exponents in both examples are qualitatively and numerically consistent with respective observations and simulations of MCs.

Through a combined rest-optical and NIR observing campaign, the Large Binocular Telescope Primordial He Abundance ($Y_{\rm p}$) Project has delivered a precision measurement of $Y_{\rm p}$. Given the stringent requirements to measure gas-phase He abundances, the optical spectra contain emission features from a plethora of metals in the Interstellar Medium (ISM). We present gas-phase N, O, Ne, S, Cl, Ar, K and Fe abundance patterns in 62 low-metallicity nebulae. We assess ionization correction factors for each element and select those that reproduce the observed ionic abundance trends. The Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O abundances do not evolve with metallicity and agree with the solar ratios. Given the Type Ia enrichment of S and Ar, the solar S/O and Ar/O suggest extended star formation histories in local galaxies relative to high-$z$ galaxies at fixed O/H. We report the first ISM K abundances in low-metallicity extragalactic nebulae; both Cl/O and K/O are constant with O/H, indicating the production of these odd-Z elements follows that of $α$ elements. Consistent with prior analyses, gas-phase Fe/O is observed to decrease with O/H due to dust depletion; correcting for this produces an average [Fe/O]$=$$-$0.80, implying Fe-deficient stars capable of producing hard ionizing spectra. Finally, the metallicity-insensitive log(N/O) plateau from 50 nebulae is $-$1.43$\pm$0.10, consistent with previous works. Five galaxies have N/O significantly enhanced relative to the primary plateau, and we discuss their properties, mechanisms that could bias measurements of N/O, and nucleosynthetic progenitors that can produce enhanced N at low metallicity.

We investigate size evolution and disk formation with a new suite of eleven cosmological zoom-in simulations, Massive Assembly of Galaxies in the Early universe (MAGE), run with RAMSES at $\sim10\,\mathrm{pc}$ resolution until $z\simeq9$. The target galaxies span stellar masses of $M_\star\simeq2\times10^8$-$3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ at $z=9$. The stellar half-mass radius grows steeply with stellar mass at low masses, turns over near $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$, and evolves weakly with redshift. The intrinsic UV sizes in MAGE overlap the compact end of observed distributions, including the compact strong-line systems, but not the more extended population at $z>9$. Dust, PSF-limited size measurements, and our overdense environments may contribute to this offset. In the simulations, stars dominate the mass within the half-mass radius above $M_\star\simeq5\times10^8\,M_\odot$, where the central circular speed exceeds a characteristic stellar-feedback scale. Outflows are then increasingly retained within the galaxy, consistent with gas recycling on galactic rather than halo scales, which sustains central gas supply and drives a compaction-like central buildup. The turnover in size at $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$ coincides with rapid growth of rotational support to $v/σ\simeq1.5$-$3$ in star-forming gas and stars younger than 10Myr, as the central circular speed exceeds the halo virial speed, which the star-forming gas velocity dispersion tracks. The subsequent low central gas fraction, declining central star formation, and younger outskirts suggest the onset of inside-out quenching.
