Contributions of massive stars and classical novae to Galactic 26Al
Authors
Manja L. Zimmerer, Thomas Siegert, Niklas C. Bauer, Tristan Bouchet, Patrik Ehrmann, Mika A. Gelowicz, Laura Eisenberger, Saurabh Mittal, Tomohiko Oka, Rudi Reinhardt, Manuel R. H. W. Skalka, Dimitris Tsatsis, Hiroki Yoneda, Arianna Vasini

The radioactive isotope 26Al is believed to be mainly produced by massive stars and ejected into the interstellar medium through their winds and supernovae. Due to its lifetime of 1.04 Myr, it traces recent nucleosynthesis and star formation. 60Fe is a similarly long-lived (lifetime 3.8 Myr) isotope, and only originating in ccSNe. Stellar evolution models in combination with Galactic Chemical Evolution (GCE) models tend to over-predict the 60Fe/26Al ratio. Population synthesis models that attempt to explain both, the 26Al mass in the Galaxy and its spatial distribution, only match the observational constraints with an enhanced star formation rate (SFR) of >5 Msun/yr. Classical novae may contribute to the 26Al production, potentially lowering the SFR inferred from massive stars and alleviating tension in the 60Fe/26Al ratio. However, previous studies suggest their contribution is negligible or < 10%. In this work we aim to determine the mass distribution of 26Al as a function of Galactocentric radius and estimate the contribution of (very) massive stars and classical novae to the 1.8 MeV signal. We are using 20 yr of INTEGRAL/SPI observations of the gamma-ray line at 1.8 MeV to test different assumptions on the radial structure of 26Al and compare the resulting mass distribution to different GCE models. For our baseline analysis, all tested source models prefer substantial nova contributions, with best-fit fractions of 77-84%. This alleviates tension in the 60Fe/26Al ratio and lowers the SFR from 26Al measurements accordingly to 1-2 Msun/yr. While it is difficult to gauge both contributions directly, next generation MeV telescopes can potentially identify individual massive stars, such as Gamma-2-Velorum.
The Impact of Flyby Interactions on the Rotation of Open Clusters

Kyle R. Tregoning, Jeff J. Andrews
Comments
17 pages, 6 figures, submitted for publication in ApJ, comments welcome

astro-ph.GA astro-ph.SR
The Gaia astrometric mission has provided the first evidence for the bulk rotation of open clusters. While this rotation may arise from residual spin inherited from a cluster's parent molecular cloud, it is unclear whether this explanation always suffices, as angular momentum is carried away by escaping stars on the timescale of two-body relaxation. In particular, the recent observance of significant rotation ($v_{\rm rot} = 0.81 \pm 0.06$ km s$^{-1}$) in the dynamically old ($\approx 4$ Gyr) open cluster M67 motivates a study of another potential source of spin for Solar neighborhood clusters: tidal torquing from gravitational encounters with passing giant molecular clouds (GMCs). We derive an analytic estimate for the change in angular momentum of a cluster due to an impulsive interaction with a passing GMC, and verify this expectation by performing $N$-body simulations over a range of encounter parameters using REBOUND. For highly energetic encounters, significant angular momentum can be transferred, but the stars that receive the most angular momentum are ejected, leaving the cluster remnant with negligible net rotation. Even when considering a random walk of many GMC-cluster encounters, the integrated effects of these interactions cannot explain observations of dynamically old rotating open clusters. However, we find that within $\sim 5$ Myr post-encounter, some disrupted clusters could be confused as rotating clusters while their stars are still dispersing. Using the local density of GMCs, we estimate that up to $\approx 1\%$ of open clusters in the Milky Way may carry this faux-rotation signal.

The study of galaxies outside the Milky Way using observations of resolved stellar populations has experienced extraordinary growth over the past two decades. There now exist panchromatic photometry and precise astrometry for hundreds of millions of individually resolved stars in hundreds of galaxies that span $\sim10^9$ in luminosity, $\sim10^3$ in metallicity, and sample a wide range of environments. Here, we review advances in extragalactic astronomy enabled by resolved stellar population imaging. We summarize major observational programs and focus on four interconnected science areas: the star formation histories of galaxies, 'bursty' star formation, the stellar initial mass function, and the orbital histories of Local Group galaxies. We review the literature in each area, synthesize key findings, identify emerging trends, and discuss how new and future facilities (e.g., JWST, LSST, Euclid, Roman, CSST, ELTs, HWO) will enable further progress. Finally, we highlight shortcomings in current datasets and discuss promising directions for new areas of investigation.
Binary Progenitors of Electron-capture Supernovae

Liji Zhong, Yong Shao

Electron-capture supernovae (ECSNe) arise from a narrow progenitor parameter space between oxygen-neon white dwarf formation and iron-core collapse supernovae, governed by the competition between core growth and envelope removal. Binary interactions modify this balance through mass transfer and mergers. Using the binary population synthesis framework POSYDON, we model ECSN progenitors across metallicities from $Z/Z_\odot=2$ to $10^{-4}$, focusing on the initial parameter space, pre-supernova stripping, envelope-based classification, surviving companions, post-supernova orbits, and cosmic event rates. The lower edge of the binary ECSN initial primary mass lies near $7\,M_\odot$, owing to efficient convective overshooting in the POSYDON grids, whereas the upper edge increases with metallicity because stronger line-driven winds require larger initial masses to build carbon-oxygen cores within the ECSN range. Stable mass transfer and mergers occupy distinct regions in initial mass ratio and orbital period space. ECSNe in bound binaries are divided into hydrogen-rich Type II ($\sim30\%$), IIb ($\sim10\%$), and Ib ($\sim60\%$) outcomes according to residual hydrogen and helium envelopes. Across all channels, approximately one-third of ECSNe have a bound companion at explosion; the remaining two-thirds do not. Among these bound systems, approximately $90\%$ of the companions are main-sequence stars at explosion. These companions exhibit a bimodal spin distribution, including a rapidly rotating component associated with prior mass transfer. Evolved-star and compact-object companions each account for only a few percent. Combining the formation efficiencies with cosmic star formation and metallicity evolution models yields an intrinsic ECSN rate peaking at $z\sim2-3$ and declining at higher redshifts. Over cosmic history, ECSNe contribute approximately $1\%-4\%$ of all core-collapse supernovae.
A Collimated Protostellar Outflow Likely Driven by Jet and Reshaped by Environment in the Sagittarius C Cloud of the Central Molecular Zone

The Central Molecular Zone (CMZ) at the center of the Galaxy provides a nearby laboratory for studying star formation under extreme physical and chemical conditions. Recent ALMA (Atacama Large Millimeter/Submillimeter Array) observations have identified 43 candidate protostellar outflows in three CMZ clouds. One of these, the Sgr C-F#2 outflow in Sagittarius C (Sgr C), has a highly collimated lobe extending approximately 0.8 pc in projection. Here we present a joint analysis of ALMA observations of SiO J=8-7, 5-4, and 2-1. We use local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and non-LTE methods to constrain the SiO column densities, H2 number density, and gas temperature. The low SiO rotational temperatures and higher kinetic temperatures inferred from non-LTE methods suggest subthermal excitation. The elongated SiO morphology, shock-excited gas, position-velocity structure, and the alignment of the red shifted molecular lobe with JWST infrared knots altogether favor jet-dominated entrainment, whereas a broader disk-wind component close to the source cannot be excluded. The outflow shows asymmetries in its morphology and physical properties, which can be attributed to dense gas on the path of its lobe impeding its propagation and feedback from a nearby HII region imposing different pressure on its two sides.

Blue cores in brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) trace recent central activity associated with cooling cluster atmospheres. We search for blue cores at $z<0.6$ in five eROSITA all-sky surveys using X-ray data, optical imaging, and optical spectroscopy. We identify 379 blue-core BCGs among 11352 systems (3.3%), although contamination increases toward higher redshift. Of these, 98 (0.86%) are X-ray bright. Their defining signature is a central $g-r$ color at least 0.2 mag bluer than the reference population within $a=20$ kpc before returning to the normal BCG envelope. Ha emission equivalent width correlates strongly with the blue continuum excess, consistent with young stellar components comprising ~0.1-1% of pre-existing stellar mass. Line ratios indicate widespread LINER-like excitation, with a larger star-forming contribution in blue Ha emitters but substantial non-stellar excitation. This activity is closely linked to the thermodynamics of the host-cluster gas. At $0.1R_{500}$, cooling times decrease from BCGs without significant Ha emission to red and then blue Ha emitters. Blue BCGs preferentially occur in clusters with $L_{\rm X,300}>10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$; red Ha emitters also favor higher X-ray luminosities. The blue-core fraction rises with optical richness. For systems with $t_{\rm cool}(0.1R_{500})<20$ Gyr, doubling richness increases the odds of hosting a blue-core BCG by $2.45\pm0.39$. This indicates that cluster scale carries information beyond cooling time, plausibly through the amount of gas available for cooling and condensation. The scarcity of blue BCGs without significant Ha emission disfavors isolated short bursts followed by long Ha-quiet fading phases. It can instead be explained by sustained or closely recurrent star formation over timescales much longer than 100 Myr, or by Ha persisting through additional excitation mechanisms as the young stellar component fades.

EP241021a is a peculiar fast X-ray transient exhibiting a long X-ray plateau and an optical rebrightening, both challenging the traditional jet afterglow model. Whether its nature is a stellar explosion or a tidal disruption event (TDE) remains debated. Here we present late-time HST/WFC3 and Gemini-N/GMOS observations of its host galaxy at $z=0.748$. After $\sim300$ days post-trigger, the transient emission had faded below detection, revealing a dwarf disk galaxy with an effective radius $R_e=1.7$ kpc, a stellar mass $M_\star\sim3\times10^8M_\odot$, and a high star formation rate of $\sim5\ M_\odot\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$. Combined with previous very long baseline interferometry measurements, the transient can be located in the galactic disk with a projected physical offset of $0.95\pm0.13$ kpc from the galaxy center. Host-subtracted optical light curves show that the rebrightening ceased at $\sim47$ days, followed by a sudden drop with a power-law index steeper than $-3.3$, coincident with a similar drop in the X-ray light curve. This synchronous drop in both bands is consistent with jet shut-off, and the required month-long jet activity suggests a persistently accreting compact object fed by a TDE. Among the TDE scenarios, the off-nuclear location and the starburst host galaxy slightly favor the post-supernova micro-TDE scenario in a binary system, in which a natal kick drives the newborn compact object into a tidal disruption of its companion. Nevertheless, an off-nuclear intermediate-mass black hole TDE or a stellar-mass black hole TDE induced by dynamical scattering in a dense stellar cluster cannot yet be excluded.

In this extension of our "Reigniting the FUSE" series, we have obtained new far-ultraviolet spectroscopy of 3 starbursting brightest cluster galaxies with the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS) aboard HST. With these new data, in combination with archival COS data of 2 additional systems, we measure star formation rates (SFRs) from the young stellar continuum and cooling rates through $10^{5.5}$ K gas with the O VI $1032,1038\,\mathring{\rm A}$ doublet ($\dot{M}_{\rm O\,VI}$). We use these measurements to increase the size of the "Reigniting the FUSE" sample, which itself contains high resolution far UV spectroscopy for 29 nearby massive elliptical galaxies from FUSE, and extend it 1 dex upwards in SFR and cooling rate. Our derived SFRs agree well with photometric GALEX$+$WISE SFRs from the literature. The new data also reinforce the previously observed trend in cooling efficiency ($\dot{M}_{\rm O\,VI}/\dot{M}_{\rm max}$; where $\dot{M}_{\rm max}$ is the X-ray luminosity-based cooling rate) versus mass, with groups cooling more efficiently than clusters by a factor of $\sim$4-5. When we isolate a sample of the most star-forming central galaxies, we find that the star formation rates, X-ray spectroscopic cooling rates, and UV spectroscopic cooling rates are all consistent with one another, on average, while retaining substantial system-to-system scatter, reflecting time-depenent multiphase condensation and cold gas reservoir evolution. The remainder of the sample, in contrast, overwhelmingly shows ratios of ${\rm SFR}/\dot{M}_{\rm O\,VI} \sim {\rm SFR}/\dot{M}_{\rm X,spec} \sim 10\%$.

We investigate the dependence of the mass-metallicity relation (MZR) on star formation and galaxy morphology at 1.71000 galaxies. The observations span >85 arcmin^2 from the pure-parallel PASSAGE and OutThere surveys. We derive gas-phase metallicities using a Bayesian framework that combines multiple strong emission-line ratios calibrated against direct auroral-line metallicity measurements from JWST. We constrain stellar masses, star formation rates (SFRs), and morphological properties using ancillary HST and JWST imaging. We stack spectra by stellar mass to constrain the MZR over log(M*/M)~6.5-11, and find that it is well described by a second-order polynomial, with a steep low-mass slope and metallicities<10% solar at log(M*/M)<7.5. Additional stacking by SFR and morphology reveals strong evidence for a fundamental metallicity relation (FMR). Specifically, at log(M*/M)<9.5, galaxies with lower SFRs are more metal-rich than those with higher SFRs at fixed mass. At log(M*/M)<9.5, we find that larger galaxies, and those having lower SFR surface densities, bulges, and/or spiral features are significantly metal rich relative to their smaller, higher SFR surface density, bulge-less, and/or non-spiral counterparts. These results suggest the secondary dependencies of the MZR reflect variations in the baryon cycle, such that recent metal-poor gas accretion drives concentrated star formation and metallicity dilution, while structurally developed galaxies retain more chemically enriched gas reservoirs.

So far, the POETS survey has focused on studying at high angular resolution the properties of both 22.2 GHz H2O masers and the radio continuum emission near young stellar objects (YSOs). Now, we want to add the information coming from another star formation tracer, the Class II 6.7 GHz CH3OH (methanol) maser, towards the same POETS targets. We seek to probe the existence of 6.7 GHz maser emission near POETS sources with high sensitivity observations and study their kinematic properties with respect to local radio continuum and 22.2 GHz maser emission at high angular and spectral resolutions. Within the POETS survey, we used the Very Large Array (VLA) in the A configuration, operating at high spectral resolution (0.1 km/s) and achieving a sensitivity of a few mJy/beam per channel map, in order to (i) probe the emission from methanol gas in the 6.7 GHz maser transition and (ii) trace its absolute spatial distribution and local standard of rest velocity. We present a preliminary analysis of 6.7 GHz CH3OH maser emission toward a subsample of the POETS survey (22 fields observed so far), 60 % of which show detection above a brightness temperature threshold of 10^7 K. Five sources among this subsample are new detections. We draw a 6.7 GHz maser brightness function by studying the distribution of the intrinsic luminosity of the maser population and predict that more than 30 % of the whole population is undetected at typical interferometric sensitivities of a few 10 mJy/beam. We confirm that 6.7 GHz masers arise close to the young star they are associated with and quantify that, on average, 90 % of them are distributed within radii of 3000 au only. Furthermore, we find a tight correlation between the 6.7 GHz maser luminosity coming from a given source and the local free-free emission, suggesting that the radio continuum provides the dominant source of seed photons amplified by the methanol gas.

We present NOEMA CO(1-0) observations of the inner $\sim$500 pc of M33 at a resolution of $0.91''\times0.37''$ ($3.7\times1.5$ pc), resolving the compact molecular gas structures and revealing their relation to recent massive star formation. The most prominent structure is a southeastern molecular filament (SE filament) that extends 76 pc along its principal axis, is 23 pc wide, and contains three CO-bright knots. Across eight independent transverse cuts, the adjacent H$α$ ridge is displaced preferentially to one side of the principal CO ridge by a median $10.0^{+0.9}_{-1.4}$ pc. Young OB candidates are more closely associated with H$α$ than with compact CO. The filament shows little velocity change along its length but a resolved gradient across its minor axis. Its elongated morphology, transverse kinematics, and ordered CO-H$α$ distribution are consistent with locally asymmetric molecular-gas compression and subsequent massive star formation. Across the wider field, 23 well-measured compact clumps have a median radius of 2.67 pc and a median velocity dispersion of 2.15 km s$^{-1}$. Their linewidth and apparent virial-parameter distributions show no clear difference between the SE filament and the rest of the field. Although the local spiral potential and pattern speed remain unconstrained, the observations provide a resolved view of a compression-compatible molecular-to-ionized-gas sequence in the center of M33.

We detect two high-confidence star-forming sources in the environment of a chemically near-pristine ([Fe/H] $<-3.66~;2σ)$ Damped Ly$α$ system (DLA) using Keck/KCWI. These are found via the hydrogen Ly$α$ emission line at $Δv=-1200$ kms$^{-1}$ (LAE1) and $Δv = -200$ kms$^{-1}$ (LAE2) relative to the DLA redshift ($z_{\rm abs} = 3.078$) at an impact parameter of $b=66$ physical kpc and $b=137$ physical kpc. We measure Ly$α$ fluxes of $F(\rm Lyα)_{\rm LAE1} =4.4\pm 0.7\times10^{-18}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$ and $F(\rm Lyα)_{\rm LAE2} =3.8\pm 0.9\times10^{-18}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}$. This marks the first detection of star formation in the environment of a chemically near-pristine DLA. We use red channel Keck/KCRM data to place limits on the associated He II 1640 A emission flux of these sources, $F(\rm He\,II)<3.8\times10^{-19}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}~(3σ)$ and $F(\rm He\,II) <3.7\times10^{-19}~\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}~(3σ)$. The weak and narrow properties of the Ly$α$ emission, combined with the inferred position on the mass-metallicity relation, suggest a metal-paucity of these galaxies. Open clusters exhibit diverse spatial structures that provide insight into their formation and dynamical evolution. We investigate the relationship between stellar mass segregation, network topology, and dynamical age in 871 dynamically unevolved open clusters ($τ= t/t_{\rm rh} \leq 1$) from Gaia-based catalogs. We construct three-dimensional k-nearest-neighbor graphs and measure Degree Centrality (DC), Closeness Centrality (CL), and Betweenness Centrality (BC). For each measure, we define a network-based mass segregation ratio (MSR) by comparing the mean centrality of the most massive stars with that of all cluster members. We test the stability of these measures across connectivity parameters $k=5$--20 and compare them with the classical 3D MSR, $Λ_{\rm MSR,3D}$, and dynamical age. All three network ratios show positive correlations with $Λ_{\rm MSR,3D}$, strongest for closeness centrality, while their correlations with dynamical age are weak. These results demonstrate that network analysis provides a complementary approach to studying stellar structure and mass segregation in open clusters.

We present the deepest HI observations of the two systems with Dproj < 100 kpc, and $Δ$ vsys < 200 km/s in the MHONGOOSE survey. The MeerKAT observations have a 3$σ$ NHI = 10$^{17.9}$ cm$^{-2}$ over 16 km/s at 4 kpc scales. The new data detect HI in seven galaxies -- the interacting NGC 3511/3513 pair, and an interacting triplet (NGC 4781, NGC 4790 and UGCA 308) along with two new (2MASX J1255 and MKT 25413) dwarf satellites. Six galaxies lie on the star-forming main sequence, and their HI gas fractions are typical for such systems. In contrast, MKT 25413 has a log(MHI/Msun) = 5.7, which is the lowest MHI detected of a galaxy that is not a Milky Way satellite. This low surface brightness dwarf galaxy shows an anomalously low star formation and HI fraction for its stellar mass. We find ubiquitous low column density HI surrounding the galaxies, which is likely a consequence of mild tidal interactions from fly-bys in the past few hundred megayears. We derive the stellar and H$α$ kinematics from VLT/MUSE observations and the H2 kinematics from ALMA observations. The kinematics of the stellar and multiphase interstellar medium of the interacting galaxies display ordered rotation, and the atomic-to-molecular gas ratios are indistinguishable from star-forming field galaxies. The stellar morphology of all the interacting galaxies (except the low-mass dwarf UGCA 308) does not show major disruptions, and there is no stellar component associated with the HI tails, bridges or clouds in the intra-group mediums, down to $μ_{g}$ = 29 mag arcsec$^{-2}$. We surmise that the mild environmental interactions in the close systems are able to pull the diffuse HI out of the disk and redistribute it. The gas is not in equilibrium, although it remains bound and is in the process of falling back onto the disk.

We present the Star-formation from Photometry through the Addition of Medium-bands (SPAM) survey, a 62.9 hr JWST/NIRCam imaging program to add ten new filters, nine medium-band filters, and one broad-band filter to the CEERS legacy dataset. We describe the observation strategy, present the data, and detail the data-reduction process. We include a complete reduction of imaging in the legacy CEERS footprint, inclusive of all publicly available photometric parallel and GO observations within the legacy CEERS footprint. We present a multi-band photometric and photometric redshift catalog which includes all medium- and broad-band filters available on NIRCam. We find that adding medium bands from the SPAM and MINERVA programs improves photometric redshifts. We discuss how these data advance the SPAM survey's key science goals: better-constrained photometric redshifts, identification of high-redshift overdensity members, improved recovery of emission-line strengths, and more accurate star formation histories.
