Detection of HNCOH+ in the interstellar medium
Authors
M. Rey-Montejo, I. Jimenez-Serra, M. Sanz-Novo, V. Lattanzi, S. Thorwirth, V. M. Rivilla, S. Zeng, L. Colzi, D. San Andres, A. Megias, A. Martinez-Henares, M. A. Requena-Torres, S. Martin, B. Tercero, P. de Vicente

Letter, Accepted in A&A
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA astro-ph.SR
Only five out of the $\sim$350 molecules detected in the interstellar medium (ISM) carry the isocyanate functional group (NCO), the essential substructure of peptide bonds in proteins. Among them are isocyanic acid (HNCO) and its most stable N-protonated isomer H$_2$NCO$^+$. Previous studies have predicted that the O-protonated isomer HNCOH$^+$ should be present in the ISM; yet this species has remained elusive. We search for the metastable HNCOH$^+$ towards the Galactic Center molecular cloud G+0.693-0.027, where H$_2$NCO$^+$ has already been detected. We use an ultra-deep broadband spectral survey carried out towards G+0.693-0.027 with the Yebes 40-m, IRAM 30-m and APEX telescopes covering nearly 150 GHz of bandwidth between 31 and 275 GHz. We report the first detection of HNCOH$^+$ in the ISM with a column density of $N$ = (9.9 $\pm$ 0.5)$\times$10$^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$, which corresponds to a molecular abundance relative to H$_2$ of (7.3$^{+1.3} _{-1.0}$)$\times$10$^{-11}$. With the measured H$_2$NCO$^+$ column density of $N$ = (2.6 $\pm$ 0.3) $\times 10^{12}$ cm$^{-2}$, this yields a HNCOH$^+$/H$_2$NCO$^+$ ratio of 3.8$^{+0.5} _{-0.4}$. This result is consistent with recent kinetic calculations that favour the formation of HNCOH$^+$ over H$_2$NCO$^+$. The detection of HNCOH$^+$ in the ISM broadens the inventory of molecules known to contain the isocyanate functional group and reinforces recent works showing that isomer abundances in the ISM do not necessarily correlate with thermodynamic stability.
UnEZ: A Fully Unsupervised Self-Templating Neural Network for Redshift and Line Strength Estimation
Authors
Asha Abd, Karl Glazebrook, Colin Jacobs
Comments
22 pages, 17 figures. Submitted to MNRAS
Primary
astro-ph.IM
Categories
astro-ph.IM astro-ph.GA cs.LG
As spectroscopic surveys grow, estimating galaxy properties such as metallicity, star formation rate, and redshift becomes computationally cumbersome. Methods of estimating such properties rely on template spectra or curated redshift catalogs, which fail to represent the diversity of objects seen in large-scale surveys. We present UnEZ, a fully unsupervised, physics-informed machine learning model that can predict spectroscopic redshifts and line strengths with no prior redshift or line strength labels. An encoder predicts emission and absorption line amplitudes/widths, and a fixed deterministic decoder constructs a unique template, estimated redshift, redshift confidence curve, and spectral model. UnEZ was tested on the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Data Release 18 due to its large dataset and thoroughly vetted redshift catalog which serves as an independent benchmark. Despite the lack of templates, line strength labels, or redshift labels, UnEZ's performance competes with modern redshift estimation algorithms. At SNR > 10, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 0.459% and a normalized median absolute deviation (NMAD) of $1.237 \times 10^{-4}$. At SNR > 3, UnEZ achieves an outlier fraction of 3.89% and an NMAD of $1.471 \times 10^{-4}$. Furthermore, we demonstrate how UnEZ's line strength and redshift predictions can be utilized for various purposes such as constructing template spectra required in traditional redshift estimation algorithms. UnEZ illustrates how a fully unsupervised neural network can allow redshift, among other galaxy properties, to emerge naturally from the spectra themselves, providing a template-free, label-free approach that scales to the demands of future surveys.

Determining how the Milky Way warp evolves provides constraints on the formation and dynamical evolution of galactic disks. Using Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment Data Release 19 (APOGEE DR19) spectroscopy, we estimate ages for 222,355 red giants. With Gaia astrometry and geometric distances, we analyze 168,824 of these stars in five age bins spanning $0$--$10$ Gyr. The warp-related radial vertical-velocity ($V_Z$) profile of young red giants ($0$--$2$ Gyr) is broadly consistent with those of classical Cepheids and young open clusters. Over $9\leq R<12$ kpc, we find that the mean $V_Z$ values of the intermediate-age red giants ($2$--$4$ and $4$--$6$ Gyr) are $1.48\pm0.46$ and $1.49\pm0.52$ km s$^{-1}$ higher than that of the young population, respectively. By contrast, at $R\simeq12$--$13$ kpc, the $V_Z$ profiles of the $0$--$6$ Gyr red giants are consistent within their uncertainties. The intermediate-age enhancement persists across our robustness tests and may reflect smaller effective kinematic warp onset radii for the intermediate-age populations. The observed velocity differences may arise from the combined effects of earlier perturbations and the present-day warp, or from differences in vertical structure among stellar populations.

Context. Hickson Compact Groups (HCGs) present an ideal environment for studying galaxy interactions. HCG 16 is a group of five galaxies in an intermediate stage of evolution (Phase 2), with several members displaying signs of interaction, including distorted morphologies, tidal features, and enhanced star formation rates. Aims. We aim to better understand the evolution of HCG 16 and its member galaxies, focusing on the relationship between neutral hydrogen and star formation, while reconstructing the interaction history of the group. Methods. We analysed MUSE data processed with pyPipe3D to extract H$α$ images and stellar population synthesis results, and reduced MeerKAT data to produce a high-resolution HI cube. We examined the structure and dynamics of the H$α$ and HI lines in both 2D and 3D, and produced spatially resolved star formation histories to compare the inner and outer parts of the galaxies. Results. We identify a major star formation episode in HCG 16 beginning ~400-500 Myr ago, coinciding with the passage of NGC 848 close to the other member galaxies, and that continued at least until 50-60 Myr ago in some galaxies. The HI distribution is highly perturbed in the group, although HCG 16a retains a nearly regular disk, while HCG 16b has associated H$α$ tails. Conclusions. The passage of NGC 848 probably activated star formation in HCG 16, but cannot by itself explain the current starburst activity in HCG 16c and 16d. The evolution of the group is better explained by multiple interactions among its members than by a single strong encounter.

We investigate past star formation in NGC 6357 using Gaia DR3 astrometry and Three-mm Ultimate Mopra Milky Way Survey (ThrUMMS) $^{12}$CO($J=1$--0) data. A Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) search independently recovers the previously cataloged cluster G352.8+0.6 in addition to Pismis 24, G353.1+0.6, and G353.2+0.6. Isochrone fitting yields an age of $7.9^{+1.5}_{-1.4}$ Myr for G352.8+0.6, compared with approximately 1--3 Myr for the three young clusters. Assuming constant relative plane-of-sky motion, a linear traceback places G352.8+0.6 closest to their reference center approximately 1.8 Myr ago, comparable to their inferred ages. The $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ CO component overlaps G352.8+0.6 in projection and shows localized depressions in integrated intensity around three spectroscopically identified massive stars belonging to G352.8+0.6. The $-4$ and $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ components show complementary projected distributions and intermediate-velocity CO emission in position--velocity space. Assuming that the $+2$ km s$^{-1}$ component was physically associated with G352.8+0.6 and shared its transverse motion, our principal interpretation is that the two molecular structures interacted around the epoch indicated by the stellar traceback, approximately 1.8 Myr ago. The resulting gas compression may have contributed to the recent episode of clustered and high-mass star formation in NGC 6357 and to the formation of Shell A, which subsequent stellar feedback may have reshaped.
Stellar Bars Reshape the Spatial Distribution of UV-bright Clumps in Nearby Galaxies
Authors
Zhimin Zhou, Jianfei Liu
Comments
20 pages, 16 figures. Accepted for publication in A&A
Primary
astro-ph.GA
Categories
astro-ph.GA
Stellar bars redistribute gas and angular momentum in disk galaxies, but whether they mainly change the amount or the spatial organization of recent star formation remains uncertain. We use archival HST/WFC3 F275W imaging to study 70 barred galaxies and 95 unbarred control galaxies matched in stellar mass, optical color, redshift, and F275W background noise. A uniform dendrogram-based procedure identifies 95,486 UV-bright star-forming clumps. We compare their radial and azimuthal distributions, central F275W emission, and luminosity and size distribution functions. Relative to their controls, barred galaxies show a deficit of clumps at intermediate radii broadly corresponding to the bar region, followed by a local recovery near the bar ends. Their clumps are also preferentially concentrated along the bar major axis. These spatial differences are stronger in high-mass galaxies. Normalized bar length is more closely associated with the radial redistribution and central F275W concentration than bar ellipticity, although its relation to the azimuthal distribution is less uniform. When galactocentric radius is normalized by bar length, the recovery occurs near $R/R_{\rm bar}\sim1$, indicating that the bar sets a characteristic scale for the redistribution. Despite these spatial differences, barred and control galaxies have broadly similar clump luminosity and size distribution functions. Our results suggest that stellar bars mainly reorganize where recent star formation occurs, rather than substantially altering the observed luminosities and sizes of individual UV clumps, and that this effect depends on galaxy mass and bar extent.

We present Hubble Space Telescope (HST) imaging of six Local Volume dwarf galaxies identified by the SEAMLESS survey - Cetus B, Hydrus A, Ursa Major C, Hydra B, Grapes, and M83-dw14 - along with Centaurus C (Cen C), discovered serendipitously in the M83-dw14 field. We report the discovery of M83-dw14 and Cen C for the first time. Our HST imaging resolves each system into individual stars, enabling measurements of tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) distances (3.6-6.5 Mpc), structural parameters, and star formation histories (SFHs). We complement these data with new HI observations from the Very Large Array for Ursa Major C, Hydra B, and M83-dw14 (also covering Cen C), and from the Green Bank Telescope for Grapes, together with existing HI limits for Cetus B and Hydrus A. With accurate TRGB distances, we place each target in its environmental context, ranging from a confirmed satellite (M83-dw14) to isolated systems (Hydrus A, Hydra B, Grapes, Cen C). Cen C ($M_V=-7.96$) is among the faintest isolated dwarfs known. It matches the size and luminosity of Leo T, the lowest mass gas-rich galaxy currently known, yet unlike Leo T it shows neither detected HI ($\log(M_{\rm HI}/M_{\odot})<5.3$) nor recent star formation. Ursa Major C shows strongly asymmetric, spatially offset HI, suggesting that feedback may efficiently displace gas in such low-mass systems. Overall, our sample exhibits a broad range of SFHs and quenching states that does not track simply with present-day environment. This diversity is consistent with recent evidence that similarly varied SFHs occur even among isolated low-mass dwarfs, pointing to the important roles of inhomogeneous reionization, stochastic mass assembly, and internal feedback alongside environmental effects.

Here we present deep JWST/NIRISS wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) that covers $\mathrm{Hα}$ and $\mathrm{Hβ}$ for galaxies at $0.5 < z < 3.5$ in the NGDEEP survey. The NGDEEP NIRISS observations detect emission line fluxes from galaxies in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field to $\mathrm{m_{F150W}} \lesssim$ 28 mag, enabling studies of star-formation based on $\mathrm{Hα}$ (or $\mathrm{Hβ}$, if necessary) in low-mass galaxies with $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 7}$ at z = 0.5 and $\mathrm{\log(M_*/M_{\odot}) \gtrsim 8}$ at z = 3.5. Understanding dust evolution is essential for interpreting the properties of galaxies across cosmic time. Nearby galaxies have revealed a detailed picture of dust enrichment, but whether the same picture applies in the early Universe remains unclear. Here we explore the origin of interstellar dust in simulated galaxies at $z \sim 5$. Starting from the dust-to-stellar mass relation, we select five simulated star-forming galaxies with $SFR> 1 M_\odot yr^{-1}$ and $ log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 8$. We focus on three low-mass systems with comparable stellar masses ($log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 8.7$) but significantly different dust masses ($log(M_{\rm dust}/M_\odot) \sim 5.1-6.2$), to investigate the origin of the scatter in the relation. Our analysis combines outputs from the cosmological hydrodynamical simulation dustyGadget, a semi-analytical model for stellar dust production, and radiative-transfer post-processing with SKIRT. We confirm that stellar dust production alone reproduces only the low-dust branch of the relation. Grain growth is required to recover both its S-shape and the observed scatter, as established for nearby galaxies. The transition to efficient grain growth occurs at lower metallicity than locally, consistent with the denser interstellar medium of high-redshift systems. Differences in dust content reflect different stages of galaxy assembly. Progressively longer star formation histories lead to greater chemical enrichment and denser ISM, promoting increasingly efficient grain growth. We therefore identify the assembly stage as the key regulator of dust growth at high redshift. Mock observations show that JWST multi-band imaging can reveal the structural diversity of galaxies within the scatter, although the reconstruction of clumpy morphologies may remain incomplete. Their dust continuum emission remains below the sensitivity of current ALMA continuum surveys.
Collisional excitation of HMgC$_3$N by He: rate coefficients and astrophysical implications

Liman Tian, Ziyu Xiang, Wenli Zou, Xiaolin Chen
Comments

Reliable collisional data are essential for interpreting the rotational emission of HMgC$_3$N under the non-equilibrium conditions of molecular clouds. We investigate the rotational excitation and de-excitation of HMgC$_3$N induced by collisions with He, combining \textit{ab initio} calculations with quantum scattering theory. A two-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) for HMgC$_3$N--He was constructed using the CCSD(T)-F12A method in conjunction with the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set. Quantum close-coupling (CC) calculation were then performed to obtain state-to-state integral cross sections and temperature-dependent rate coefficients for rotational transitions with $Δj=\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm3$, covering rotational levels up to $j=20$ and temperatures from $3$ to $50$~K. The rate coefficients exhibit a clear propensity for $Δj=\pm2$ transitions. The resulting collisional data were incorporated into radiative transfer modelling to constrain the physical conditions and column density of HMgC$_3$N from its observed spectral line emission. A $χ^2$ minimization yields a best-fitting H$_2$ volume density of $n(\mathrm{H}_2)=1.233\times10^5~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and column density of $N(\mathrm{HMgC}_3\mathrm{N})=1.831\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. The derived column density is in approximate agreement with the observational estimate of $(3.0\pm0.6)\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. These results provide new collisional data for non-LTE studies of HMgC$_3$N and support a more realistic treatment of its rotational excitation and radiative transfer in molecular clouds.
Can the $\sim 5$ kpc starburst ring in MW create eROSITA bubbles?

Mukesh Singh Bisht, Kartick C. Sarkar

eROSITA bubbles (eRBs) in our Galaxy are thought to be due to the energy injection by the central star-forming region or the central Super Massive Black Hole. Identifying the origin of these bubbles is crucial in constraining the origin of the Fermi Bubbles and the nature of the Galactic feedback. However, some recent opinions suggest that the eRBs may not even have been produced by any central source, but rather a result of a star-formation ring at $R\sim 5$ kpc. In this letter, we perform detailed hydrodynamic simulations to refute that claim and show that such a ring of star formation alone is not able to produce the symmetric eROSITA bubbles - one needs a compact energy injection from the Galactic Center to produce these bubbles. Our study, therefore, suggests that the eROSITA bubbles truly represent the nuclear outflow from our Galaxy.

While numerous studies have shown that satellite galaxies experience declining star formation rates after infall, direct comparisons across different surveys and redshifts are complicated by varying sample selections, environmental metrics, and techniques used to derive galaxy properties. In this work, we select satellite galaxies from the highly complete Galaxy and Mass Assembly (GAMA) Survey and the Deep Extragalactic VIsible Legacy Survey (DEVILS) to investigate environmental quenching out to z~0.4. These surveys derive galaxy properties and environments in a consistent manner, minimizing systematic biases and enabling a robust comparison across redshift. Using projected phase space as a proxy for infall time, we quantify the evolution of the quenched fraction and trace how satellites evolve relative to the star-forming main sequence (SFMS). We find consistent trends between GAMA and DEVILS, with satellites exhibiting a steady decline in star formation relative to the SFMS at a rate of ~0.14 dex/Gyr following infall. Extending this analysis to include visual morphology, we find similar declining trends for both early- and late-type systems. Overall, we find no significant redshift evolution between GAMA and DEVILS, indicating that group and cluster environments have a comparable impact on the star formation activity of satellite galaxies over the past ~4.5 Gyr.

The Milky Way is commonly depicted as having four major spiral arms, including the Norma-Outer arm, and a central stellar bar whose properties remain debated. Masers associated with young high-mass stars have been used to trace a segment of the "Norma arm" extending from one end of the Galaxy's central stellar bar. Recent work suggests that these masers may reside along a spiral arm or occupy the dominant x1 stellar orbits that give the bar its shape. We now use a new generation of N-body, hydrodynamic simulations to argue that these million-year old masers instead trace the Galactic bar's Quadrant I shock front or, equivalently, its dust lane. This implies that the bar dust-lane is star-forming, shedding new light on our understanding of star formation processes in the Milky Way. By associating the masers with the simulated shock front, we determine the bar's parameters to be: a ~ 4.3 +/- 0.2 kpc, b ~ 2.3 +/- 0.2 kpc, phi = 20 (+5/-2) deg, Omega_p <~ 40 km s^-1 kpc^-1, representing the bar's semi-major and semi-minor half-lengths, inclination angle and pattern speed, respectively. This raises questions about the Milky Way's spiral structure and suggests that the Galactic bar is long, slow and star-forming.

Using JWST/NIRISS slitless spectroscopy from the PASSAGE program, we analyze 17 spectroscopically confirmed emission-line galaxies in candidate interacting systems at $z \sim 1.7$--$3.2$. These galaxies span stellar masses of $7.3 \lesssim \log(M_*/M_\odot) \lesssim 10.4$ and global gas-phase metallicities of $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H}) \sim 7.6$--$8.7$. From spatially resolved emission-line maps, we measure radial metallicity gradients separately for each galaxy, finding values from approximately $-0.2$ to $+0.45$ dex kpc$^{-1}$. The mean metallicity gradients in bins of stellar mass and redshift are consistent with previous measurements, and their scatter is not significantly different from that of the heterogeneous literature compilation in either the metallicity-gradient--stellar-mass or metallicity-gradient--redshift relation. Overall, our results reveal substantial diversity in the metallicity distributions of candidate interacting systems at cosmic noon and provide tentative evidence that galaxy interactions may influence the redistribution of gas and metals.

Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to ~100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.

Leo T, a Milky Way (MW) satellite, is the lowest-mass gas-rich galaxy known to show recent star formation, making it an excellent laboratory for investigating star formation in the ultraviolet (UV) regime within a low-density, metal-poor environment. We present a far-UV (FUV) study using Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope (UVIT) data, aiming to characterize FUV star-forming clusters (SFCs), identify FUV-emitting stars, constrain their properties, and explore star formation in an extremely low-mass, metal-poor galaxy. We examined the galaxy's FUV extent and compared it with the H I distribution. We extracted thirty SFCs and derived their properties, including area, luminosity, and star formation rate (SFR), and compared them with previous studies. FUV-emitting stars and their stellar parameters were identified through cross-matching with catalogs from the Hubble Space Telescope and the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE). We also estimated the global SFR and star formation efficiencies (SFEs) associated with previously identified CO clouds. The FUV luminosities of the SFCs confirm the absence of massive O and B-type stars in Leo T. The FUV-emitting stellar population consists of main-sequence (MS) stars, blue loop (BL) stars, horizontal branch stars, and emission-line (EM) stars. The upper MS and EM stars are concentrated near the optical center, whereas the lower MS and BL stars are more spatially extended. We find that five out of the six FUV-emitting stars with MUSE spectroscopy are extremely metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\leq$ -3). The (NUV - r) color profile indicates that the UV emission is dominated by stellar populations younger than 1 Gyr out to a radius $\lt$ 185 pc. Despite the recent detection of CO(1-0) emission, no SFCs are associated with the molecular clouds, which exhibit extremely low SFEs. The spatial distribution of the SFCs suggests a directional interaction with the MW.
