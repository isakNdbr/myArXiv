Estimating M Dwarf Ages using their Transiting Brown Dwarf Companions
Authors
Aryan Gupta, Steven Giacalone, Joshua N. Winn, Gregory J. Gilbert, Zafar Rustamkulov, Luke B. Handley, Andrew W. Howard, Jonathan Gomez Barrientos, Heather A. Knutson, Morgan Saidel
Comments

Determining M dwarf ages remains challenging because of their slow main-sequence evolution. We investigate using transiting brown dwarfs to constrain system ages through their measured masses and thermal emission. We report Palomar/WIRC secondary-eclipse observations of two close-in brown dwarfs, LP 261-75C and AD 3116B, measuring eclipse depths of $6.1 \pm 0.3$ parts per thousand (ppt) in $K_s$ and $9.1 \pm 0.6$ ppt in $J$, respectively. Combining these observations with space-based transit photometry, radial velocities, and host star spectra, we infer brown dwarf internal luminosities and, assuming coevality, host star ages using five sets of atmospheric and evolutionary models. For LP 261-75C, model-dependent median ages span 1.3-2.9 Gyr, substantially older than the previously proposed age of 0.1-0.2 Gyr based on presumed membership in the AB Doradus moving group. This result, the host star's high metallicity, and the companion's measured radius disfavor AB Dor membership. The revised age implies a mass of $\approx 62$-$68$ $M_{\mathrm{Jup}}$ for the wide-orbit companion LP 261-75B, compared with an earlier estimate of $\approx 21$ $M_{\mathrm{Jup}}$. AD 3116B, a member of the 0.6-0.8 Gyr Praesepe cluster, provides a test of brown dwarf evolutionary models through independent constraints on its mass, age, and luminosity. Its inferred age depends strongly on cloud treatment, spanning 0.4-0.6 Gyr for cloudy models and 1.0-1.3 Gyr for cloudless models. Among the models considered, Sonora Diamondback, which includes cloud condensation and cloud clearing across the L/T boundary, provides a plausible simultaneous match to the observed eclipse depth and radius. These results demonstrate a new method to constrain M dwarf ages using emission observations of their brown dwarf companions, while highlighting the sensitivity of the inferred temperatures and ages to evolutionary model differences.

The Auriga-California Molecular Cloud (AMC) is a nearby star-forming region with a moderate level of star formation activity. Previous infrared surveys have shown that its young stellar population is concentrated along a dense filament extending north of NGC 1579. We searched for new manifestations of ongoing star formation in the AMC by identifying optical and molecular outflows, Hα emission-line stars, and young stars associated with reflection nebulae. Narrowband Hα and [SII] imaging, together with slitless spectroscopy, were obtained with the 1-m Schmidt telescope of the Byurakan Observatory. The optical data were complemented by near-infrared images from the Spitzer Enhanced Imaging Products (SEIP) and by WISE/unWISE archival data. We discovered seven new Herbig-Haro (HH) objects and outflows associated with several young stellar objects, including deeply embedded infrared sources and stars surrounded by compact reflection nebulae. Nearly all of the optical HH objects have molecular hydrogen counterparts, and several additional candidate H2 outflows without optical counterparts were identified. Particularly noteworthy is a compact region around V347 Per that appears to host at least three molecular outflows. We also present a list of newly identified Hα emission-line stars and a catalogue of stars associated with optical reflection nebulae. Both populations are concentrated primarily along the northern dark filament of the cloud. The spatial distribution of the newly identified HH objects, molecular outflows, emission-line stars, and nebulous young stars confirms that the northern filament is the principal site of ongoing star formation in the AMC. The close correspondence between the optical and molecular outflowsfurther suggests that most of their driving sources are deeply embedded Class 0/I young stellar objects.

We present a study that simultaneously fits for key fast radio burst (FRB) population properties using the Second CHIME/FRB Catalog while calibrating for instrumental selection effects. Our inferred volumetric rate at $z=0$ is $1.1^{+0.4}_{-0.3}\times 10^{5}\,{\rm Gpc}^{-3}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ above an isotropic-equivalent energy of $10^{39}$ erg and below a scattering timescale of 30 ms at 600 MHz. The evolution of the FRB rate relative to the cosmic star formation rate remains statistically unconstrained. Assuming a Schechter function for the energy distribution, isotropic emission, and 1-GHz emission bandwidth, we find a characteristic maximum energy of $2.0^{+2.6}_{-1.0} \times 10^{41}$ erg and a differential power-law index of $-1.41\,^{+0.27}_{-0.21}({\rm stat.})\,\pm\,0.35({\rm sys.})$. The host galaxy contribution to the observed dispersion measure (DM) is modeled as a log-normal distribution; assuming a total Galactic contribution of 80 $\text{pc cm}^{-3}$, we find a median host-galaxy DM contribution of $199^{+34}_{-36}\;\text{pc cm}^{-3}$ in the rest-frame. Finally, we find that the average FRB spectrum, described by a spectral index of $α=-1.0^{+0.5}_{-0.6}$, is inconsistent with being flat at the $\sim2σ$ level. We note an excess of high-S/N bursts at low DMs in the observed sample that is not predicted by the best-fit model. Testing alternative models does not alleviate this tension; future population studies may mitigate this limitation by incorporating robust fluence and redshift measurements.

[ABRIDGED] Context: Whether the initial mass function (IMF) varies with environment remains a central question in star formation. The multiple populations of Orion OB1b provide an ideal laboratory to test possible IMF variations. Aims: We aim to characterise the stellar populations of Orion OB1b by identifying its constituent groups, assigning membership probabilities to individual sources, and determining their IMFs. Methods: Using Gaia DR3 astrometry and photometry, we identified stellar groups through clustering analysis and assigned membership probabilities with a probabilistic mixture model. Stellar masses were derived from extinction-corrected photometry using pre-main-sequence evolutionary models. Results: We identified eight kinematically distinct groups and a non-clustered population, deriving ages and IMFs spanning ${\sim}0.08$--$10\,M_\odot$ for six groups, all consistent with being gravitationally unbound. Each IMF was fitted with a lognormal distribution below $1M_\odot$ and a power law above. The high-mass regime is statistically indistinguishable across all groups, while four groups also exhibit consistent IMFs over the full mass range. The remaining two groups show differences that may reflect membership uncertainties rather than intrinsic IMF variations. The combined IMF is characterised by $m_c=0.27^{+0.02}_{-0.02},M_\odot$, $σ=0.45^{+0.03}_{-0.03}$, and $Γ=1.85^{+0.16}_{-0.14}$. The non-clustered population exhibits a broader and more uncertain IMF, although residual field contamination cannot be ruled out. Conclusions: Our results support previous evidence for the complex internal structure of OB1b. The IMF in OB1b appears broadly consistent across its kinematically distinct groups and compatible with other young populations in Orion.

We present a comprehensive photometric, astrometric, and dynamical study of four Galactic open clusters, NGC 1662, NGC 1778, NGC 2422, and NGC 2437, using Gaia DR3 data. Probable cluster members were identified through astrometric and photometric criteria. Radial density profiles were modelled with a King-type surface-density distribution to derive structural parameters. Mean parallaxes and proper motions yielded astrometric distances of 410 +/- 12 pc (NGC 1662), 1761 +/- 335 pc (NGC 1778), 477 +/- 18 pc (NGC 2422), and 1681 +/- 172 pc (NGC 2437). Fundamental astrophysical parameters were estimated through Bayesian isochrone fitting using PARSEC evolutionary models within an MCMC framework. The resulting ages (log(t/yr)) are 8.91 (-0.33/+0.24), 8.20 (-0.11/+0.18), 7.80 (-0.11/+0.06), and 8.56 (-0.34/+0.27), respectively. Luminosity functions were transformed into present-day mass functions using an isochrone-based mass-luminosity relation. The derived mass-function slopes are broadly consistent, within uncertainties, with the classical Salpeter form. Mean systemic radial velocities from Gaia DR3 and complementary spectroscopic surveys enabled the determination of Galactic space velocities (U, V, W) in the Local Standard of Rest frame. Backward orbital integrations over 3 Gyr were performed using galpy with the MWPotential2014 Galactic potential. The resulting orbits are nearly circular (e <= 0.106), with limited vertical excursions (Z_max < 0.3 kpc), consistent with thin-disc kinematics. Inferred birth radii suggest possible outward radial displacement for NGC 2422, NGC 2437, and NGC 1778, and inward displacement for NGC 1662. Overall, all four clusters appear to have evolved within relatively stable Galactic disc orbits.

The well-established calibrations of astrophysical parameters derived from classical photometric systems (e.g. those of Johnson, Stromgren-Crawford, and Vilnius) are rarely applied at present. Instead, spectral energy distribution fitting techniques and the very broad Gaia photometric system are typically employed. However, these approaches have proven highly inaccurate for upper main-sequence stars. In this work, the Geneva seven-colour photometric system was synthesised from flux-calibrated low-resolution Gaia spectrophotometry (BP/RP spectra). The available ground-based observations have made it the most homogeneous dataset, offering a number of astrophysical calibrations and applications. The GaiaXPy Tool was used to synthesise the seven filters. The original Geneva U had to be exchanged with the SDSS u because it is found to be significantly blueward of the cut-off of the BP/RP spectra. The ground-based observations were used to correct for offsets and the known temperature dependence of the effective temperature in the ultraviolet region of the BP/RP spectra. The test using well-known effective temperature and metallicity calibrations on the synthetic photometry (with 21 080 stars) demonstrated the high accuracy of this approach. Further checks for possible correlations between the projected rotational velocity and binarity yielded negative results. As a direct application, the metallicity of 630 open clusters (and, thus, the radial Galactic metallicity gradient) was determined. Finally, we compiled a catalogue of 25 881 397 objects. This work demonstrates that alternatives exist to the widely used three-band Gaia photometric system. The Geneva seven-colour approach can help calibrate and analyse astrophysical parameters, especially those of upper main-sequence stars.

Ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs) are extreme low-surface-brightness systems whose formation pathways remain debated. Globular clusters (GCs) can trace their early assembly, but wide-area studies of UDG GC populations remain scarce. We study GC systems of 545 high-quality UDG candidates identified in the Kilo-Degree Survey (KiDS) with UDGnet, using a dedicated GC-detection pipeline. We identify 24 UDGs with $N_{\rm GC} \geq 3$ (14 associated with groups or clusters and 10 in the field), and use the globular cluster luminosity function to estimate their distances and physical sizes. At fixed luminosity or stellar mass, group- and cluster-associated UDGs tend to have higher GC counts and specific frequencies than field UDGs, although the distributions overlap substantially. Most systems with $S_N \gtrsim 40$ reside in groups or clusters, while field UDGs generally occupy the lower part of the distribution. Group- and cluster-associated UDGs also more often lie near the upper envelope of the $N_{\rm GC}$--$M_\star$ relation. Empirical GC--halo mass relations consequently imply higher halo masses at fixed stellar mass and lower stellar-to-halo mass ratios, although this environmental trend is mild. These findings are consistent with efficient early GC formation followed by suppressed or prematurely quenched field-star formation, suggesting that GC-rich UDGs may represent an extension of the dwarf-galaxy population. The environmental trends may reflect differences in early assembly, quenching, infall, and survival histories rather than a sharp field--cluster dichotomy.

We analyse the chemical, spatial, and kinematic properties of stars in the solar neighbourhood using DESI Data Release 1 combined with Gaia astrometry. A high-quality FGK dwarf sample is separated into low-$α$ and high-$α$ populations using Gaussian mixture modelling in the [$α$/Fe]--[Fe/H] plane. The low-$α$ population is more metal rich, dominates near the Galactic mid-plane, and shows negative radial and vertical metallicity gradients. The high-$α$ population is more metal poor, has a steeper vertical metallicity gradient, an approximately flat radial metallicity profile, and dynamically hotter kinematics. Despite these differences, the two chemically defined populations overlap substantially in velocity space. These results indicate distinct chemical enrichment histories for the low-$α$ and high-$α$ populations, while showing that chemical classification does not map uniquely onto present-day kinematic components. The observed chemo-dynamical structure is consistent with the combined effects of chemical enrichment, radial redistribution, and secular dynamical evolution in shaping the Galactic disc.

Detailed studies with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) have shown that dust is widespread even in UV-luminous galaxies at $z\gtrsim6.5$. Multi-band continuum observations are critical to fully characterize these early dust reservoirs. We present a homogeneous analysis of multi-band ALMA observations from the REBELS Large Program and seven follow-up programs, targeting thirteen massive galaxies at $6.5\lesssim z\lesssim7.7$ in at least two bands, including Band 8. Through optically thin modified blackbody fitting, we infer the dust masses, temperatures, and infrared luminosities of our targets. Their dust temperatures span $T_\mathrm{dust}\approx30-70\,\mathrm{K}$, and a stack reveals an average $T_\mathrm{dust}=49_{-8}^{+11}\,\mathrm{K}$. Three sources nominally qualify as ultra-luminous infrared galaxies (ULIRGs; $L_\mathrm{IR}>10^{12}\,L_\odot$), with total star formation rates of $\mathrm{SFR}_\mathrm{UV+IR}\approx160-300\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, the highest of which is found in our most distant target, REBELS-18 at $z=7.67$. Their star formation is heavily obscured, with $\gtrsim90\%$ emerging in the infrared, compared to an already large average obscured fraction of $f_\mathrm{obs}=0.75\pm0.11$ for the stack. These SFRs are consistent with the reconstructed growth histories of some massive quiescent galaxies at $z\approx4-5$, suggesting that dusty yet UV-bright galaxies at $z\sim7$ are plausible progenitors of such systems. The sample moreover displays large dust-to-stellar mass ratios ($M_\mathrm{dust}/M_\star = 0.4_{-0.2}^{+0.3}\%$), best reproduced by models in which dust grows efficiently in the interstellar medium. This demonstrates that a dust-rich, IR-luminous, and heavily obscured galaxy population is already in place well into the Epoch of Reionization.

Deuterated molecules probe the physical and chemical evolution of molecular clouds and star-forming regions. Their abundances depend on temperature, density, and evolutionary stage. DCN and DNC are relatively abundant deuterated isomers that probe temperature-dependent deuterium chemistry. Although [HCN]/[HNC] is often thought to increase in warmer gas, the temperature dependence of [DCN]/[DNC] remains less well constrained. To investigate how [DCN]/[DNC] depends on the gas kinetic temperature, we observed the $J$=1-0 transitions of DCN, DNC, H$^{13}$CN, HN$^{13}$C, HC$^{15}$N, and H$^{15}$NC toward 101 late-stage massive star-forming regions using the Yebes 40m telescope. Gas kinetic temperatures were estimated from CH$_3$CCH 5-4, whose rotation temperature is closely related to the kinetic temperature of dense gas. These temperatures were adopted in the column density calculations and in the analysis of temperature-dependent abundance ratios. We find a moderate positive correlation between the abundance ratio [DCN]/[DNC] and kinetic temperature, which is statistically significant and indicates that DCN becomes progressively more abundant relative to DNC in warmer gas. A weak negative correlation with temperature is observed for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HNC})$, whereas no statistically significant correlation is found for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HCN})$. The [HCN]/[HNC] ratios show substantial scatter and no statistically significant correlation with temperature, possibly due in part to optical-depth effects in the $^{13}$C isotopologues and the limited sensitivity of the $^{15}$N isotopologue observations. These results suggest that [DCN]/[DNC] may be governed not only by HCN/HNC isomeric chemistry but also by temperature-dependent deuterium fractionation pathways and the chemical evolution of dense gas.

High-resolution extinction maps of resolved external galaxies, such as M31, are crucial not only for accurately correcting dust extinction and reddening toward individual sources within the galaxy, but also for characterizing the distribution and evolution of interstellar dust in external galactic environments. We aim to construct a high-resolution and large-coverage stellar-traced extinction map of M31, based on multi-band photometric data from the HST PHAT and PHAST surveys. Using a stellar template library built from the PARSEC stellar evolution models and adopting the average extinction law of M31, we apply a matching method analogous to the SPar algorithm to derive A_V values for approximately 65 million stars. The extinction map is then generated using a HEALPix adaptive-resolution scheme. The resulting map covers approximately two-thirds of the star-forming disk of M31, with a maximum spatial resolution of 1.61 arcsec. The median extinction across the map is A_V = 1.24 mag. Comparison with previously published stellar- and dust-emission-based extinction maps shows broadly similar large-scale morphology, while differences remain in the extinction values. The extinction map further reveals fine-scale features such as inter-arm filaments, supernova-driven cavities, and spur-like structures. The map has been publicly released through the NADC platform and is expected to serve as an important observational basis for extinction correction in subsequent astronomical observations and for related M31 scientific studies.

We present a preliminary asteroseismic investigation of the pulsating stars in the intermediate-age open cluster NGC 2126. The TESS Full Frame Images and ground-based photometry from the previous study, Dileep et al. (2025), were used to identify the pulsation modes of δ Scuti member stars using period-luminosity relations and pulsation modelling. Our analysis resulted in a tentative identification up to the first radial overtone in stars N1, N2 and ZV1. Additionally, the second radial overtone was secured in N1. The disentangled pulsation and eclipse light curves of the binary star V551 Aur (V6) from our previous research were employed to study pulsation amplitude and phase modulations across the orbital period. The primary component of V6 exhibits pulsations showing amplitude and phase modulations, indicating tidal influence on the oscillations. Modelling the pulsation as the fundamental radial mode reveals that in order for the mode to be radial, the star must be significantly brighter than observed. Hence, the mode is not the fundamental radial mode or the overtones. Low-resolution spectroscopy was used to search for chemical peculiarities in the pulsating members, and we found that star ID 143 has features similar to an Am star.

We present deep X-shooter follow-up spectroscopy of AMORE6, an exceptionally metal-poor galaxy at z = 5.725. The new spectrum provides stringent upper limits on rest-frame ultraviolet metal lines, including NV, CIV, NIV], NIII], and OIII]. The non-detection of CIV breaks the density-metallicity degeneracy present in previous analyses based on the [OIII]/Hbeta ratio, excluding the high-density, chemically enriched gas scenario and strengthening the evidence for an extremely metal-poor environment. The X-shooter spectrum also reveals a narrow Lyman-alpha line with a velocity dispersion sigma_v = 23.2 +/- 0.5 km/s and a nearly symmetric profile centered at the systemic redshift measured from Hbeta. Combining the Lyman-alpha line width with the measured source size, we infer a dynamical mass M_dyn = (1-22) x 10^7 Msun, assuming that the line width traces gravitational motions. Spectral energy distribution modeling using Pop III and Pop II stellar population libraries yields stellar masses (1-5) x 10^5 Msun. Although all models reproduce the observed blue UV continuum and Halpha equivalent width (EW), none simultaneously reproduces the Hbeta luminosity and EW, with the observed Hbeta luminosity exceeding the model predictions by up to 3.7 sigma. Possible explanations include departures from standard recombination conditions or limitations in the adopted stellar population models. We discuss possible interpretations of AMORE6, including massive-star formation in an atomic-cooling halo and a gravitationally bound star cluster.

JWST observations have revealed a higher-than-expected abundance of bright galaxies at $z \gtrsim 10$. Several scenarios have been proposed to explain this excess, one of which invokes a top-heavy stellar initial mass function (IMF) in the early Universe. In this work, we construct spectral energy distribution (SED) templates based on a redshift- and metallicity-dependent IMF ($z$-$Z$ dependent IMF) motivated by the star-cluster simulations of Chon et al. (2022) and the redshift-dependent IMF model of Steinhardt et al. (2020). We implement these templates in a semi-analytic galaxy model built on a large-box $N$-body simulation to investigate the impact of a top-heavy IMF on the ultraviolet luminosity function (UVLF) of high-redshift galaxies. The adopted $z$-$Z$ dependent IMF enhances the UV luminosity of young stellar populations, particularly at low metallicity, and increases the scatter in the UV luminosity-to-star formation rate conversion factor relative to the Kroupa IMF. Without dust attenuation, both top-heavy IMF models predict a substantially enhanced bright end of the UVLF. However, when dust attenuation is included and calibrated at $z=7$, IMF-dependent metal enrichment and the resulting dust attenuation partly offset the intrinsic UV enhancement. The self-consistent model broadly reproduces the observed UVLF at $z\sim10$, although discrepancies remain at $z\gtrsim12$. For the selected high-redshift UVLF constraints, the best-fitting dust optical depths are 51% and 21% of the fiducial values at $z=9.7$ and $13.04$, respectively, indicating a preference for weaker effective dust attenuation. The highest-redshift comparison does not provide a robust constraint. Our results highlight the importance of accounting for both enhanced stellar UV emission and IMF-dependent metal enrichment and dust attenuation when assessing the impact of a top-heavy IMF on high-redshift galaxies.
